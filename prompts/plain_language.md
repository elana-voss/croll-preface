# Task: one unit of what the book carries, in Plain Language

Your bundle holds the book's lines a checked page cites and that page, labelled `CHECKED PAGE`, which sets out one unit of what the book carries, with its evidence. Retell it in everyday words for a reader who has never met this book or its subject. This page is much shorter than the checked page and carries none of its apparatus. Use the book's lines to get the sense right, not to add points; where a point's lines are missing, the checked page and its quotes are your evidence.

## What stays and what goes
- Keep every point, every condition, every warning, the answer on process and every open question.
- Leave out quotes, repeated evidence and the discussion of how a point was established.
- Add nothing.

## How to write
- Short sentences in everyday words, one idea each, in connected prose.
- No quotes from the book.
- A word of the subject that an ordinary reader would not know is not used: say instead what the book says the thing is or does. Where the book does not say, describe the thing by how the text handles it. Use the book's word only where no description is possible, once.
- A name an ordinary reader knows stays, in today's spelling of the same word. Never put a different word in its place.
- Keep the book's words and yours apart. What the book says is told as the book's; what the author reports in order to reject is told as rejected. An Interpreted point opens with `Our reading is` and gives the reason. Where the checked page gives several readings, give each.
- This page tells what the book says, not what the reader should do: write `the book says to ...`, never `do ...`.
- Do not soften doubt and do not add it: what the checked page leaves open stays open.
- Write about the thing, not about your wording.

## References
End each paragraph and list item with a reference bracket for what it says, `[SEC.12]`. The brackets serve only the check and are removed by script before the page is published, so no sentence may mention or lean on them.

## The page
`# <the unit's name, in today's spelling> in Plain Language`. One opening paragraph: what the page describes, that it draws on this book alone, that it gives no quotes or passage numbers and avoids the book's special words, and that the page with the checked page's title gives the evidence for each point. Then one `##` sub-heading for each sub-heading of the checked page that has points, in its order, in everyday words. Then `## What the book leaves open`: a list. Leave out what the checked page gives nothing for; never stretch to fill a section.

Write the page to the file your task names, ending with the closing lines. Then reply with one line: the file written and its word count.
