# Task: write the page for one section of the book

Your bundle holds one whole section of the book and, labelled `UNVERIFIED CANDIDATE`, the joined readings of it. Write a page in English that tells a present-day reader what this section says, step by step, as the book itself divides and argues it.

## Content
- Follow the section's own order and its own heads. Say what the author holds, what he rejects and on what grounds, whom he quotes, and what he says he hides or gives only to some readers.
- Every claim about the book carries a reference in square brackets after it: `[SEC.12]`, `[SEC.12–14; N041]`. A quote is set in curly quotation marks, “like this”, and the bracket after it in the same paragraph names where it stands. Quote at most 40 words at a time. Note references as you read; do not reconstruct them afterwards.
- Keep the voices apart: when the words are someone else's, say whose.
- Where the text speaks in a covered way, apply the rule for reading what is hidden and say which kind each reading is: Stated, Interpreted, or several readings.
- The candidates help you find things; they are not proof. Use one only where you see it in the text yourself, and cite the text, never the candidate.
- No straight quotation marks (`"`) anywhere. No words from outside the book: where the book uses its own word for a thing, use that word and explain it from the text.

## Form
Markdown. First line: `# ` and the section's head as the book prints it, in modern spelling. Short paragraphs; a sub-heading for each step of the section's argument. About 1,200 to 2,500 words.

If your task names a half of the section, your bundle holds that half only: write its part of the page, about 1,200 to 2,000 words. The first half begins with the `# ` line; the second half has no `# ` line and begins with its first `## ` sub-heading.

Each candidate is one line: reference | kind | voice | tags, if any | “quote” | what it names, if given | note (a note ending in … is cut short).

Write the page to the file your task names, ending with the closing lines. Then reply with one line: the file written and its word count.
