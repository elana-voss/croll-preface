Croll's Admonitory Preface — A Reading

Chapter II. Where that True Physick is to be Found

Physick is the book’s word for medicine: “the true medicine (or Physick)” [CH2.1]. The chapter looks for it in steps: the kernel of natural things, the Spirits in them, the Word of God, and last the honour due to God and to the Physitian.

The twofold nature of lower things

What God created is perfect, “as the heaven” [CH2.1]. But whatever is in “these sublunary inferiour things” has two natures: “a perfect and an imperfect, that is, a fift Essence and the dreggs may be separated one from the other by fire” [CH2.1]. So the medicine lies covered. It is “wrapt up in rindes, barks, matrixes, receptacles, husks, garment and cottages, as Almonds and all kernells are covered with bark and rind” [CH2.1]. It therefore “must be seperated from the impure Elements by the artificiall Anotomy of Chymists before we can come at the pure Medicine” [CH2.1]. That way “the bonds are loosed by art and industry, and the faculties of healing set at liberty” [CH2.1].

Reading: Stated. The author gives the likeness himself, kernel within rind, and says what it stands for [CH2.1]. The margin adds more likenesses, “nor can we have meale without bran, neither is hony without a sting”, and speaks of “Such Secrets” [N132] (note-only).

The specific virtue in the seeds

In “those three Families of Nature, the Vegetable, Animall, and Minerall” [CH2.2] “there is contained that True and specifix Physick of materiall distempers” [CH2.2]. It does not lie in “the four outward, naked, superficiary and Relollaceous qualities” [CH2.2], and the author names an authority for this: “as Theophrastus learnedly discourseth” [CH2.2]. It is “a certaine specifix vertue concealed in the very Seeds, naturally proceeding without art” [CH2.2]. The Creator planted it at the beginning “by vertue of that omnipotent Word” [CH2.2].

The virtue is “like the soule in mans body from the beginning of the Creation” [CH2.3] and “is not from without” [CH2.3]. Other sources are rejected. It is not “infused into them by a momomentaneous position of the Stars, nor made up of a fortuitous meeting of Atomes” [CH2.3]. Nor is it from the body, “for then it could not be separated without the destruction and corruption of the body and forme” [CH2.3]. The rule is that “the actions of Cures proceed from the Spirits and vitall Tinctures of the spirits, not from the bodies or dead Relloceous qualities” [CH2.3]. The margin agrees: “Heaven is the framer of the externall edifices, not of the Secrets, Wonders and mysteries that dwell in them.” [N133] (note-only).

Man the Microcosm and the three shops of cures

The author builds on what is “granted by the Interpreters of Secret Nature” [CH2.4]. These are other people’s words, which he accepts. They hold that “there is nothing in the whole Universe, every particular whereof is not also in that Microcosm Man” [CH2.4], and that “the seeds of all things lye hid in him” [CH2.4]. If “the internall Heart of the Microcosm or Man should be sick” [CH2.4], it has been the part of True Physitians to enquire how they might borrow remedies from “his Parent or the externall Heart of the Microcosm which is one like the other” [CH2.4]. The two Hearts are alike “if not in outward, yet in inward figure and forme” [CH2.4]. The remedies come from “those three shops of Cures” [CH2.4]; the margin beside them divides inferiour Nature into “three principall parts, i. e. Vegetable, Animall, and Minerall” [N134].

Several readings (wording-dependent). Both Hearts are printed as the Heart of the Microcosm [CH2.4]. (1) The outer Heart belongs to the world outside man. It is called his Parent and external [CH2.4], and later the outer brain is “The external Macrocosmicall Braine” [CH2.7]. (2) The words stand as printed: the outer Heart too is of the Microcosm.

Minerals: gold and what is withheld

Gold comes first among Mineralls, with an aside: it is gold “which cheareth up the Spirits when a man hath enough of it in his purse” [CH2.5].

Here the chapter first says that some knowledge is open only to some men. Mineralls bear “Magicall and Hieroglyphicall Characters, which sage Antiquity hath without rashnesse or superstition attributed to them” [CH2.5]. These “doe sufficiently insinuate to Inlightned men, and Magically disclose their hidden vertue to those more secret Phylosophers that are instructed therein” [CH2.5]. Reading: Stated. The choicest things, “by Natures just decree, with-hold their benigne and vitall Element from those that possesse them” [CH2.5]. The margin says the same of gold: “As he that hath much Gold may know nothing of its medicinall vertue.” [N135] (note-only).

Gold “would discover most Divine actions if it were rightly refined, and had its power reduced into act by a Naturall and due Resolution” [CH2.5]. A parenthesis adds that “there is but one way to Resolve and to compound things, because Art and Nature, like mother and daughter, consent to each other” [CH2.5]. Yet “few men have this gift of God bestowed on them, so as to make solid and massy Gold potable, that it may be drunk” [CH2.5]. The chapter calls this a gift given to few.

Vegetables, animals, and the Spirits that direct

A list of Vegetables follows, “with six hundred of the like” [CH2.6]. Then come animal remedies, such as horns and the bone in a stag’s heart [CH2.7]. Rightly prepared, they cure the diseases of the Heart, “but by an Internall, proper, specifick, harmonious, similer virtue” [CH2.7]. All healing lies in the Spirits: “the earthy part is altogether dead, husks and rinds beget nothing, the Spirits onely in the bodies of things doe all” [CH2.7]. The author glosses his own terms: “not the body, but the Astrum or hidden Heaven gives all the direction” [CH2.7]. These Astra, the Formes of medicines, are “separated by Alchymie from their bodies” [CH2.7]. Reading: Stated.

The Spirits “onely know how and where to find out the disease” [CH2.7], and the medicine finds its way by likeness: “every medicine striveth to get to its place, and seeketh after that member that is like unto it” [CH2.7]. What draws it is a magnetick vertue “which may well be called the inexpressible property” [CH2.7].

Next comes “Celsus the Roman Physitian confesseth of all Arts” [CH2.7], followed by “where by many good old country women have gone beyond great learned Physitians” [CH2.7]. Voice: UNCLEAR, between Celsus and the author. The margin beside it reads: “Experiment, as also judgement, without knowledge is but fallacious difficult and fortuitous, but with Science it is true and infallible.” [N138]. Several readings (note-only). (1) The note limits the praise of the country women. (2) The note backs the rule of “the true Phylosophicall Phisitians” [CH2.7], which rests on long Experience.

The same is said of “the other six principall members and parts of mans body” [CH2.7]. Two are named. “The external Macrocosmicall Braine is the Oyle of Silver, Water of the Sophire Emerold, Mosse, Vitrioll” [CH2.7]. And “The flower or CHIBUR or Brimstone are a Balsam for the lungs and the whole breast.” [CH2.7].

Every disease has its medicine

Even “Chronicall, Astrall and fixed diseases” [CH2.8] can be cured. The common sort of Physitians, who “know not the seat, seeds, nativities, roots, and centres of diseases” [CH2.8], judge them incurable. The author reports their view in order to reject it. His rule: “there is no disease, as it is a disease, but there is a medicine for it” [CH2.8]. The one exception is disease “from the anger of the Divine Majesty by secret predestination” [CH2.8], whose cure was given “to the Apostles, who by true faith in Christ healed all Diseases” [CH2.8]. The margin sets out two origins of disease: “the Caelestiall is cured by the Word of the Lord, the Naturall is recovered by naturall means” [N139].

He rejects the view he gives to Pliny, of Nature “as an unjust Step-mother” [CH2.8]. In his own words, “she is the benigne Parent of all Things” [CH2.8]. So “It were therefore great folly and sottishnesse to think that the great Creator would hide these things from men” [CH2.8].

Interpreted (mine). This stands beside the withholding in CH2.5 and the Word “not known to every one” [CH2.20]. The margin shows how the two may fit: “There is no cause to complaine of the want of medicines but onely of our ignorance of them.” [N139]. On the narrowest reading, God does not hide the remedies, and men fail to know them.

The beasts and their herbs

“Every Creature knoweth his proper remedy.” [N140]. “The Stork having eaten a Serpent, is cured with Organy.” [CH2.9]. From the Toad the author draws a rule: “therefore it is not good to eat Sage before it be washed” [CH2.9]. No man in his right wits, then, will think the Father would “neglect his own children, which he created after his own Image, and prefer the Beasts before them” [CH2.10].

Medicines work by the gift of God

“The most High Author of Nature ath created Medicines out of the Earth” [CH2.11]. The margin points to Eccl. 38 [N141]. These medicines are “not defectives but perfect” [CH2.11], yet they work “not of it selfe, but by the gift of God” [CH2.11]: “unlesse God be present and infuse virtue into the Hearbs what good can Dittany doe” [CH2.11].

Power comes down a chain: “All these inferiour things, as living creatures, hearbs, stones, mettalls have their force by subministration from the Heaven, and the Heaven from the Intelligences, and these from the great Worker” [CH2.12]. So “we must not trust so much to them as to God” [CH2.12]. The example is “Asa dyed because he trusted more to the Physitians then to God.” [CH2.12], with 2 Chro. 16.12 in the margin [N143].

Several readings. The virtue was earlier “not from without” and not infused by the Stars [CH2.3]. Here God infuses it [CH2.11], and force comes from the Heaven [CH2.12]. (1) CH2.3 denies only a momentary or fortuitous cause, the Stars’ position or the Atomes, while CH2.12 describes a lasting chain that ends in God. (2) The two passages stand against each other as they are.

The Word, the heavenly medicine

“The Caelestiall Medicine onely, or the WORD of God” [CH2.13] “is that which healeth all things” [CH2.13]. The opening likeness returns: “As the bark is not the kernell, so hearbs are not the medicines, but a signe onely of the Word signified.” [CH2.13]. “Physick is two-fold in the Earth” [CH2.14]. The Visible Physick “ought not to be administred before there be a separation of the pure from the impure” [CH2.14]. The Invisible Physick is “from the Son by the Word, and is but one” [CH2.14]. And “the Hearb is not the medicine, for that is invisibly hid in God himselfe” [CH2.14].

Interpreted (mine). The medicine has now been placed in the kernel [CH2.1], the Seeds [CH2.2], the Spirits [CH2.7], the Word [CH2.13] and God [CH2.14]. CH2.14 itself holds two of these together: it speaks of “the Hearbs in which the medicine is” and of the medicine hid in God [CH2.14]. The narrowest reading assigns the medicine in the Hearbs to the Visible Physick and the medicine hid in God to the Invisible.

Healing by a word: the Name and Characters

God “could (and can) make men whole by the Prophets and True Cabalists with a word onely” [CH2.15]. The text goes on: “the NAME also of the living God is lively, and so the Letters of the living Name are also lively” [CH2.15]. Great things have been done by “True Magicians (by whom I doe not mean Nicromancers or them of the Black Art)” [CH2.16], “by a Word written and Characters or Signes” [CH2.16]. The author cites Agrippa: Characters “have no force from the Figureors Pronunciation” [CH2.16], but from what God or Nature ordained for them.

“Medicines are visible bodies; Words are invisible bodies” [CH2.17]. Both heal “by the Spirit of God made One with Nature by his Word FIAT” [CH2.17]. For cure by words “written, carved and hanged about the neck” [CH2.17], he sends the reader to Rog. Bacon [CH2.17].

The Word incarnate and the daily bread

The great cures of Physitians are done “by the efficacy of the Triune and Divine Word onely” [CH2.18]. The Saviour healed the deafe and dumb, whom no Pills or Syrups could help. “he did it not by Nature, but by himselfe; he did it by One Word, and he is that Word, to wit, the increated Mercy of God” [CH2.18]. Note (wording-dependent): the same paragraph also says “Created Word” [CH2.18]. One Spirit fills all things and “may well be called The Fulnesse” [CH2.18]. “Nothing is made out of God, for in him all things live, are moved and doe subsist.” [CH2.18].

This Word “is truly our Dayly Bread” [CH2.19], “the supercaelestiall Mummy, the supernaturall Balsome” [CH2.19]. “Man liveth not by bread alone, but by that which is in Bread” [CH2.19].

Here the author says plainly that this knowledge is not open to all. The Word “is the true medicine that healeth all things, but is not known to every one” [CH2.20]. Nor can every Scholler “treat and write of it though plunged over head and eares in the dusty learning of School-Divinity” [CH2.20]. He names instead “our friend Theophrastus Paracelsus” [CH2.20], who wrote “of the WORD of God INCARNATE which may be found in the Creatures” [CH2.20]. And “by this Word, FIAT, the seed of the whole world, were Heaven and Earth created” [CH2.20].

The Magus and the true Cabalist

What the Physitian does by herbs “working successively by the space of time” [CH2.21], “the MAGUS, the Wise man or Caelestiall Physitian performeth suddenly and much sooner by Characters and Stones with a most powerfull impression” [CH2.21]. The impression is given in covered words: “the Gamahaea of Influentiall Wedlock to the Terrestiall signe” [CH2.21]. Reading: Stated. The text explains the marriage itself: “like a stretched cord, all the Superiour vertue floweth through every inferiour thing even to the utmost” [CH2.21]. It names this tie “Divine Matrimony” [CH2.21]. The margin heads the paragraph “The explication of a common saving” and gives it: “in Hearbs Stones and Words there are great vertues” [N154] (note-only).

The True Cabalist, “whom Paracelsus calls the Naturall Divine” [CH2.22], “willeth onely what God doth” [CH2.22]. He works in “that Only Divine Name ISHUH in which all things are reckoned up and contained” [CH2.22]. The author glosses this himself: “he doth performe it in the WONDERFULL WORD by the Mind, Faith and Prayer” [CH2.22]. “The New Birth is the Field of Caelestiall Physick which healeth with a word without Externall means” [CH2.22]. “in these Three principall Poynts also consisteth the whole Foundation of the Magicall and Cabalisticall Art” [CH2.22].

Several readings. (1) The three points are Mind, Faith and Prayer, the three named earlier in the paragraph [CH2.22]. (2) They are herbs, stones and words, as in the margin [N154], matching the order of Physitian, MAGUS and Cabalist [CH2.21–22].

Honour to God, and to the Physitian

“Honour and Praise and Glory therefore belongeth to the Creator, who worketh all in all” [CH2.23]. The Physitian is “the obedient Minister of God and Nature” [CH2.23]. “there is nothing at all of his own in it besides Art and right preparation” [CH2.23].

Yet the true Physitian “is to be honoured as the Scripture commandeth” [CH2.24], for three reasons. First, “God worketh and doth his own will by him as by his Minister” [CH2.25]. Secondly, “he ought to excell all mortalls in the search and knowledge of Nature and the Light thereof” [CH2.26], as Homer required [CH2.26]. Thirdly, he shows God’s works “not onely by opening his mysteryes and hidden secrets, but also by curing the sick” [CH2.27].

So Physick is “the most excellent” of the Sciences [CH2.28], and the chapter ends: “It taketh its rise from Theology or the Light of Grace, and endeth in the Light of Nature.” [CH2.28].
