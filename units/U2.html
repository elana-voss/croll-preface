Croll's Admonitory Preface — A Reading

That One Only Chief Medicine of the Most Ancient Philosophers in Plain Terms

This page sets out what the author’s Admonitory Preface says about one unit: the Medicine named in the sixth chapter’s head, “Of that one onely chiefe Medicine of the most Ancient Phylosophers” [CH6.0]. His list of heads gives it the sixth place [OPEN.29]; he sets it apart in his opening [OPEN.6]; the fifth chapter names its limit [CH5.68]; the sixth treats it to the close of the Preface [CH6.76]. Each point is marked Stated where the text says it outright, Interpreted where the point is this page’s inference, or Several readings where the lines allow more than one. Points that rest on a margin note alone are tagged note-only; points that rest on a single English word of the translation are tagged wording-dependent.

Process or not

The division made from the readings found that the book marks a process, and that this Medicine is the second of its two units, a Medicine “wrought by Art” [CH6.38], apart from the True Physick because its result differs: one Medicine that heals all diseases of the humours [CH6.42]. For this unit it rests on the Moyst way [CH6.38], the joining the True Phylosophers require [CH6.21], the note on Gold [N263] and the Kings Highway [CH6.48]. It records that the steps of this course are covered [CH6.21; CH6.38].

In brief

  1. Stated. Nothing in Nature is more choice than the medicines the author offers, except this one, which the most ancient Philosophers had from the beginning of the world [OPEN.6; OPEN.29].
  2. Stated. Against the appointed term of life it is of no effect [CH5.68; CH6.38; CH6.42].
  3. Stated. The author never read of a greater gift of wisdom given to man; by it all things may be done [CH6.1].
  4. Stated. Few or none find it; God gives it to whom he will [CH6.18; CH6.44].
  5. Stated. Those who knew it swore never to write it plainly [CH6.21; N281].
  6. Stated. The True Phylosophers hold that it is reached only when the fat or blood of the Sun and the Dew of the Lunary are joined into one body, if God please [CH6.21].
  7. Stated. One drop of it raised a man at the point of death to full health in one night [CH6.38].
  8. Stated. It heals all diseases of the humours, except at the appointed term or where God sends the disease as punishment [CH6.42].
  9. Stated. All the virtues of the Creation are heaped together into one mass by ingenuity and Art [CH6.48–49].
  10. Stated. The Phylosophers of Hermes school say it may be the Fift Essence; the author says God created this Fift Essence as the root of life [CH6.50–51].
  11. Stated. Holy men used it before the flood, and Adam had it, as Lactantius witnesseth [CH6.71].
  12. Stated. At the close the author says the Ancient, True and Philosophical Medicine is not yet fully known, and may be restored [CH6.76].

Set apart from what the author offers

Stated. In all Nature “there doth not lye hid any more choyce and efficacious, then these chief secrets of the more hidden Medicines here offered” [OPEN.6], “except only that universall and most famous Medicine which the most ancient wise Philosophers had from the beginning of the world” [OPEN.6]. His list of heads: “Of the universall and chiefe Medicine or Phisick of the most Ancient Philosophers, commended of many, possessed, yea seen, or believed of very few.” [OPEN.29]

Interpreted. This page reads the exception as a ranking: his own medicines are not this Medicine, and he puts it above them. [OPEN.6]

Its limit: the term of life

Stated. “No Medicine can preserve the corruptible body from death, the wages and punishment of sin; but there is one thing that hindereth corruption, reneweth youth, and prolongeth the short life, as in the Patriarchs” [CH5.68]; yet “in this case the universall and chiefest medicine is of no effect, because no industry or skill of Artist or Nature can perpetuate the wedlock of life and death” [CH5.68].

Several readings. (a) The “one thing” [CH5.68] is this Medicine: the sixth chapter says “Many holy men used this universall Medicine before the flood” [CH6.71]. (b) It is something else: the same paragraph names “the universall and chiefest medicine” [CH5.68] separately. [CH5.68; CH6.71]

Stated. A weak Naturall Heat must be strengthened by “the macrocosmicall Heat of Sun and Moon, if it could be got, even the most simple matter which the most Great God generated of the spirit of the world” [CH5.44].

Interpreted. The sixth chapter says almost the same of this Medicine: “it is the most simple Matter which the Best and Greatest Lord generated out of the spirit of this world” [CH6.51]. On that likeness this page reads the fifth chapter’s “most simple matter” [CH5.44] as this Medicine, and “if it could be got” [CH5.44] as saying it is hard to come by. [CH5.44; CH6.51]

The greatest gift of wisdom

Stated. The sixth chapter opens on “that chief and universal Phylosophical Phisick, than which a greater gift of wisdom we never read that divine bounty bestowed on man” [CH6.1]: “this most secret Secret of secret Secrets, by which even wonders, yea all things may be done” [CH6.1]. A margin note: “This occult Minerva of Phylosophy & this only most precious Jewell is inestimable.” [N253] (note-only).

Stated. It works first on “the Plannets of inferior Astronomy, whose imperfection and drosse it causeth to vanish by piercing them with its most powerfull impressions” [CH6.1], “for it segregateth all extraneous Sulphureity and impure Terrestreity from metallick and Humane bodies” [CH6.1]; second on “the languishing and lost health of the Human body, by its fiery vigour” [CH6.1].

Interpreted. The bracket speaks of “metallick and Humane bodies” [CH6.1], and the second field is the human body; this page reads “the Plannets of inferior Astronomy” [CH6.1] as a covered name for metallic bodies. The chapter elsewhere names “the metamorphosis of Mettalls” [CH6.50]. [CH6.1; CH6.50]

Stated. A third use he passes over: “to say nothing now of the Magicall and super Celestial use” [CH6.1], “when the Gonetick influence of the beames of the Sun and Moon and the fourth revolution is finisht upon its native Earth” [CH6.1].

Interpreted. This page reads the finished “fourth revolution” [CH6.1] as a stage; the narrowest reading is that more than one turn goes before this use. [CH6.1]

Stated. As witnesses he gives the consent of learned men, “the ocular inspectation and certification of many of our time” [CH6.1], and their writings, “which are woven of so many Hieroglyphicall, Magicall, and Mathematicall Coverings” [CH6.1].

Who finds it

Stated. “among so many thousands that seek, there is not one to whom the doors of Nature are unlockt” [CH6.18], since God “giveth his gifts to whom he will, and withdraweth them from whom be pleaseth” [CH6.18]. A margin note: “The one only and true way to all Secrets is to have recourse to God the author of all good” [N261].

Several readings. Men “seek the Kingdome of God, and study to finde out the Celestiall Stone in the last place” [CH6.18]. (a) The Celestiall Stone is this Medicine, to be sought after the Kingdom of God; a note says “in this Stone the universal Nature of things shineth forth” [N283]. (b) Men put both last, and that is why none finds the doors unlocked [N261]. A later note uses the name for what purifies the mind: “By the access of this beam, ray, or celestiall Stone all drosse is purified” [N298] (note-only). [CH6.18; N283; N298]

The sworn secret

Stated. “This is that Secret laid up and buryed in the most secret Treasury of the Mind and Memory” [CH6.21], which the more occult Phylosophers “have in a wise and ingenious craft covenanted and sworn they would never write it plainly in any Book” [CH6.21], nor made known “to any but such who had an understanding allegorically.” [CH6.21] Being Lords of Things, “they should also have power to dispence or give Names at their pleasure” [CH6.21].

Interpreted. This page reads the freedom to “give Names at their pleasure” [CH6.21] as a warning that the names of this Medicine and its matter cannot be taken at their face. [CH6.21]

What must be joined

Stated. The True Phylosophers (QUOTED: True Phylosophers) affirm “that none can attaine the desired end” [CH6.21] “before the fat or blood of the Sun and the Dew of the Lunary, be joyned into one body by the circular wheel of the Elements with the help of Art and Nature in the form of an Hexagon” [CH6.21], “which can never be done except the most High God please” [CH6.21]; “they sleight all particulars, which naturally doe altogether want the virtue of tincturing, unlesse they proceed from the first fountaine” [CH6.21].

Interpreted. The narrowest reading: the making needs two things joined into one body, by both Art and Nature; and the “particulars” [CH6.21] lack the virtue of tincturing unless they come from the first fountain. [CH6.21]

Stated. A margin note beside it: “On this the hinge of the whole busines turneth” [N263], “as Gold animated by the Salt of Niture is made the chief subject of the Phylosophers metallick Physicks” [N263] (note-only).

Several readings. (a) The note names the chief subject of this Medicine. (b) Its “metallick Physicks” [N263], in the plural, may be a class of works of which this is one. (c) Its “Gold” [N263] may be a plain name, or one given “at their pleasure” [CH6.21] (note-only, wording-dependent). [N263; CH6.21]

The wonder seen: one drop

Stated. The paragraph tells of “the greatest miracle of Nature wrought by Art” [CH6.38], among “the various and manifold metamorphosis of the Inferior Astronomy made in the cold” [CH6.38], “the Moyst way of the Ancients not as yet intensly exalted to the eye of the Basilisk” [CH6.38]: “by giving one onely drop of that Latex or liquor” [CH6.38], “a man desperately sick and at the point of death was recovered” [CH6.38], “restoring him to firme and perfect health in one night” [CH6.38]. In that Latex “the whole world was centred” [CH6.38].

Interpreted. This page reads the Moyst way, “made in the cold” [CH6.38] and “not as yet intensly exalted” [CH6.38], as marks of the course of making: a moist way, worked cold, with a further exalting to come. A note calls something “The Physophycall Basilisk” [N274] that “like Lightning suddenly and unawares burneth up any imperfect mettall whatsoever” [N274], and the Medicine works “like as in Lightning and the eye of a Basilisk” [CH6.50]; so the Basilisk’s eye may name the Medicine at its height of power. [CH6.38; N274; CH6.50]

Stated. “this Kingly medicine, and the Empresse of all other, causeth Humane Bodies immediately and as it were miraculously to rise againe from what desperate diseases soever” [CH6.38], “if God hath not otherwise decreed” [CH6.38]; “this whole new regenerate world is able by vertue of its Regeneration to renew the little old corruptible world Man” [CH6.38], “till the appointed Terme of death imposed on mortalls for their sin” [CH6.38].

Interpreted. The great world made new renews the little world; the narrowest reading is that the Medicine holds the whole world in small, as the Latex in which “the whole world was centred” [CH6.38]. [CH6.38]

All diseases of the humours

Stated. “All diseases proceeding from the corruption of humors, how great and desperate soever their cures be, are healed by this universall medicine” [CH6.42], “except the party be at the utmost Terme appointed him by the supream Being; or the disease be inflicted by God, besides Nature, as a punishment and speciall affliction” [CH6.42].

The Artist and God

Stated. The author approves what Lullius “rightly concluded” [CH6.44] (QUOTED: Lullius): “between the Artist and God the first cause, there ought to be an agreement without contrariety” [CH6.44]. He warns that “two publick Phylosophers of divers Nations in our time abusing the Gifts of God” [CH6.44] “came to dolefull and lamentable end” [CH6.44].

Several readings. A margin note: “The originall of the Philosophicall Magistery.” [N278] (a) The originall is the first way of keeping it, “the first Hapocraticall silence” [CH6.44], which most now break by boasting [N278]. (b) It is its source, God moving the Artist [CH6.44]. (note-only) [N278; CH6.44]

The road and the one mass

Stated. Many “have been perswaded that they should never attaine the same, either by other means or any Arts” [CH6.48], “but by a wonderfull and most abstruse comprehending of all the vertues of the whole Creation flowing and running together in one certaine masse” [CH6.48], which “in this Rode or Kings Highway and Phylosophical Reason is to be accomplished” [CH6.48]. The voice of this persuasion is UNCLEAR. The next paragraph: “All these spirituall virtues and active qualities being by great help of ingenuity and Art like the lesser world heaped up together and concentred into one masse” [CH6.49] bring “the knowledge of all Naturall things and Heavenly seerets, and a perfect operation” [CH6.49]. A margin note: “The defect of Nature must be supplyed by the industry of Art” [N280].

Interpreted. Read with the Latex, whose virtues were “by a wonderfull artifice invisibly gathered together in an heap” [CH6.38], these lines give the making as a gathering of all virtues into one mass by Art, where Nature falls short. [CH6.48–49; N280]

The Reason given by Hermes school

Stated. The Phylosophers “train’d up in Hermes school” [CH6.50], though “altogether dumb in keeping close the secret of the Art by a constant Taciturnity” [CH6.50], give this Reason (QUOTED: the Phylosophers of Hermes school): “the greatest Medicine, being artificially prepared with the help of wise Nature the Mistresse of Science, should be (or is) the Life and inlightning Light” [CH6.50]; “it may be the Essence of our Life: the Fift Essence compounded of the four Elements, in which are all the Elements actually” [CH6.50]; “This is so in respect of the four qualities of the body as the Heaven is incorruptible in respect of the four Elements.” [CH6.50]

Interpreted. In their words the Medicine stands to the body’s four qualities as the Heaven to the four Elements. Their hedge, “should be (or is)” [CH6.50] and “it may be” [CH6.50], this page reads as theirs, not the author’s doubt. [CH6.50]

The Fifth Essence and its names

Stated. The author: “The most High created this Fift Essence as the root of life in Nature for the preservation of the four qualities of the Humane body” [CH6.51], “as he did the Heaven for the preservation of the universe” [CH6.51]. He names it “the Glasse of God’s Mysteries, and the Miracle of universall Nature; the Fift Essence of the whole frame of the world, and the whole world Regenerated” [CH6.51], and “the Son of the Sun and Moon, who by his ascending into Heaven and descending into the Earth hath obtained all power of superior and inferior things” [CH6.51].

Stated. The paragraph goes on: “yea that it is truly the spirit of Life which pierceth through all other spirits” [CH6.51]; “there is need of the Balsom of life as a means to be sought from Externalls, by which the internall is restored” [CH6.51]; it “hath been altogether unknown to the Physitians of our time” [CH6.51]. From “yea that it is” [CH6.51] the voice is UNCLEAR: the author’s own, or the Reason of Hermes school continued.

Interpreted. “the Son of the Sun and Moon” [CH6.51] answers the joining of “the fat or blood of the Sun and the Dew of the Lunary” [CH6.21]: the narrowest reading is that the Medicine is named as the offspring of the two things joined. [CH6.51; CH6.21]

Stated. Margin notes add names: “The Tincture is the Fife Essence of the Microcosm” [N283]; Paracelsus (QUOTED: Paracelsus) calls it “the Defensive of old age, the Universall medicament, which like the invisible Fire consumeth all diseases” [N283] (note-only).

Interpreted. This page reads Stone, Tincture, Fift Essence, Magisterie and universall Medicine as names for one thing (note-only). [N283; CH6.51; CH6.49; CH6.42]

Long life, up to the term

Stated. The author speaks “Phylosophically of the Naturall death, which is onely the wasting of the Naturall Moysture and Heat, as may be seen in a burning lamp” [CH6.70], “not Theologically of the Fatall death and utmost Terme which God hath prefixt to every one” [CH6.70]. “God hath created the aforesaid Medicine for the preservation of life” [CH6.71]; it can “chase away from us all diseases which cause the naturall death, untill ultimate death the most Terrible of all Terrible things” [CH6.71]. “Many holy men used this universall Medicine before the flood, which Adam also had in his Family, as Lactantius witnesseth” [CH6.71]. A margin note, beside “Archid. of the Elixirs” [N307], in an UNCLEAR voice: “It is the conservation of the Human body from all accidentall corruption.” [N307] (note-only).

Interpreted. The fifth chapter denies any help against the term [CH5.68]; the sixth says this Medicine preserves life [CH6.71]. This page reads them as agreeing once the two deaths are kept apart: the Medicine holds off the natural death [CH6.70] up to the fixed term, and can do nothing against the term itself [CH6.42]. [CH5.68; CH6.70–71]

Not yet fully known

Stated. The author has imparted “so far as was lawfull, and so much as was allowable by God” [CH6.76], and hopes that “that Ancient, True, & Philosophical Medicin” [CH6.76] “is not yet fully known, may in a shorttime by the heavenly assistance, be restored to its lost lustre & ancient splendor” [CH6.76]. A margin note: “I have not spoken of so many things, but there yet more left to be spaken.” [N317]

Interpreted. “Ancient, True, & Philosophical” [CH6.76] answers the head’s “most Ancient Phylosophers” [CH6.0]; this page reads the closing words as about this Medicine, lost to his age and still to be restored. [CH6.76; CH6.0]

Open questions

  • Whether the Celestiall Stone sought “in the last place” [CH6.18] is this Medicine [N283; N298].
  • Whether the “one thing” [CH5.68] that prolongs life is this Medicine [CH6.71].
  • What the Sun’s fat or blood, the Dew of the Lunary, the wheel and the Hexagon name [CH6.21].
  • Whether the note on Gold [N263] speaks of this Medicine or of metallic works in general.
  • What the four revolutions and the Magicall use are [CH6.1].
  • What the Moyst way is, what its exalting adds, and who saw the one drop [CH6.38].
  • Whose persuasion the Kings Highway is [CH6.48].
  • Whether the originall of the Magistery [N278] is the first silence or its source in God [CH6.44].
  • Whose voice speaks from “yea that it is truly the spirit of Life” [CH6.51] onward.
