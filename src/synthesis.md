# The Preface as a Whole

## What the Preface is

The heading addresses Prince Christian Anhaltin [OPEN.0], named at the end as the author’s patron [CH6.76]; the first words address the “COURTEOUS READER” [OPEN.1]. The aim is to break an old silence about secrets and to “set before thee two of the most excellent Spagyrik Mysteries” [OPEN.2; OPEN.3]. Before the Remedies, the author lists what is “to be explained by a Philosophicall Phisitian” [OPEN.23]: six numbered heads and an epilogue [OPEN.24–30]. Our reading is that the chapters follow this list: head III, that Phisick is fetched out by fire, matches Chapter III’s heading [OPEN.26; CH3.0].

Its sections, in order:

- The opening, with no chapter number: why the silence is broken, the critics expected, the heads to come [OPEN.2; OPEN.10; OPEN.23].
- Chapter I, Of the True Physick: this Physick is God’s gift and rests on the agreement of the great world with man [CH1.1; CH1.4].
- The Microcosm, or Little World, Man: man’s generation, his parts and his invisible, sydereal side [MICRO.0; MICRO.14; MICRO.31].
- Chapter II: the true Physick lies wrapped like a kernel in rinds, in the Spirits of things, and last in the Word of God [CH2.1; CH2.7; CH2.13].
- Chapter III: how it is got out of its bark and rind and prepared by Fire [CH3.0].
- Chapter IV: how Physick works on the body and cures [CH4.0].
- Chapter V, “The Duty of Natures Minister, the Physitian” [CH5.0]: what manner of physician this Physick requires [OPEN.28].
- Chapter VI: the one chief Medicine of the most ancient philosophers; it closes the Preface [CH6.0; CH6.75].

## The argument in its own order

### The opening: the Admonitory Preface

The author reports the ancient Philosophers’ rule against those who “prophane the Secrets” only to set himself against it [OPEN.2]. His ground is God, who “communicateth his light, without respect or envy, to the good and bad” [OPEN.2], so he will no longer hide his Talent “by an impious silence” [OPEN.2]. What he offers he says he proved by his own hand and trial, unlike writers who wrote from others’ report, for “no man is further to be believed, then as every one findeth by his own proper experience” [OPEN.3; OPEN.4; OPEN.5]. One thing he sets above all he offers: “that universall and most famous Medicine which the most ancient wise Philosophers had from the beginning of the world” [OPEN.6].

He then foresees how each sort will take his publishing. The Mysticall Hermetick Philosophers will accuse him of “breaking open the seale of Chymistry” [OPEN.10]; he answers that better Treasures lie in the true Cabala, to be shown to all when in the third Age “Elias the Artist” comes [OPEN.10]. The spurious Theophrasteans will accuse him, since they lose their cheating trade [OPEN.11]. The milder Galenists will embrace this light, the dull ones scorn it, and the Scholastic Doctors hold titles got by flattery or bribery [OPEN.12; OPEN.13; OPEN.14; OPEN.15]. Some old physicians become scholars of Chymistry, lest they be buried in “the dead shadows of meer outsides” [OPEN.17; OPEN.20]. The list of heads follows [OPEN.23–30].

### Chapter I. Of the True Physic

The true Physick “is the mear gift of the most high God” [CH1.1], to be learned from God, not from the Heathens [CH1.1]. Its foundation “is according to the agreement of the lesser World Man with the greater and externall world” [CH1.4]: the outer world is a glass in which man is seen, the two one in essence and inward form [CH1.5]. Everything is fourfold, so there is a fourfold Man and a fourfold Medicine [CH1.6; CH1.12]. The Astrum is “the secret Forger” of the form of things [CH1.15]; each thing carries its own, and the outer stars neither infuse virtue into things nor necessitate man [CH1.16].

All compound bodies resolve into Three Principles, Mercury, Sulphur and Salt [CH1.19]. The first life of medicines must die so that a second life is reached through Putrefaction and Regeneration [CH1.37]. Diseases come not from the four humours but from the Seed and the Three First [CH1.39; CH1.40], and are cured not by contraries but by the Arcana, which the Chymist draws out of the last matter into the first [CH1.43].

### The Microcosm, or Little World, Man

The section opens on the Divine lesson “KNOW THY SELFE” [MICRO.1]. The notion of all things is created in us, and the mind is perfected “not by study and paines; but by patience and submission” [MICRO.3]. True philosophy should be grounded on the Scriptures, not on the Heathen [MICRO.8]. Man is “the bond or buckle of the world” [MICRO.11], made of the Quintessence of all created things [MICRO.12], the centre and circle of the creatures [MICRO.13]. He has three parts, a mortal Body, a Sydereal Spirit and an immortal Soul [MICRO.14]; the body from the Firmament is the soul’s vehicle, knitting soul and earthy body together [MICRO.17].

The invisible, sydereal man is also called the imagination [MICRO.32], “the Gate of Wonders”, which breeds the Plague and “brings sicknesse and health” [MICRO.33]. The Gabalisticall part of Magick rests on three Pillars: prayer in sacred silence, natural faith and a strongly exalted imagination [MICRO.37–40]. The author breaks off, since these matters “require a larger narration then can be made of them at this time” [MICRO.44], and names those who cleared this foundation, Paracelsus first and Severinus next [MICRO.45].

### Chapter II. Where that true Physick is to be found

Lower things have two natures, “a fift Essence and the dreggs”, which fire can separate [CH2.1]; the medicine lies as a kernel in its rind, got out by the Chymists’ art [CH2.1]. It is a specific virtue planted in the Seeds by the Word [CH2.2]; cures come from Spirits, not bodies [CH2.3; CH2.7]. Remedies come from the three shops, Vegetable, Animal and Mineral [CH2.4; N134], among them gold, which few can make potable [CH2.5]. Every disease has its medicine, except disease from God’s anger [CH2.8].

Herbs are “but a signe onely of the Word signified” [CH2.13]; the Word of God “is that which healeth all things” [CH2.13]. The Magus heals by Characters and Stones, the true Cabalist by Mind, Faith and Prayer [CH2.21; CH2.22]. The medicine is placed in kernel, Seeds, Spirits, Word and God [CH2.1; CH2.2; CH2.7; CH2.13; CH2.14]. Our reading is, narrowest, that the medicine in herbs belongs to the Visible Physick and that hid in God to the Invisible [CH2.14]. The chapter ends by honouring God, and the Physitian as his minister [CH2.23–28].

### Chapter III. How the True Physic is got out and prepared by Fire

Nothing below is so noble that it has no poison, yet where there is venom there is virtue [CH3.1]; the margin names God’s severe Justice the venom and his Mercy the Physick [N163]. Physicians are ministers “to seperate, not masters to compose” [CH3.6]; “Physick and Chymistry cannot be separated” [CH3.7]. In each of Animals, Vegetables and Minerals the Three First are sought [CH3.9]. Since the fall, impurity is joined to the pure roots, and Nature gives nothing pure [CH3.11; CH3.12].

The Separatory art parts the kernel and Quint-Essence from the husks [CH3.14]; medicines so prepared bring many advantages [CH3.16–19], and prepared minerals and stones excel vegetables [CH3.21]. All things come from one seed and Principle, quickened by one universall Spirit [CH3.28–31]. Two readings stand of where the Elixer’s matter lies: in man [CH3.35–37], or in every thing, man among them [CH3.34; CH3.38]. Galen lacked the art of separation; Theophrastus would have shown more had his failings been borne with [CH3.40; CH3.41]. The chapter judges that “this wicked generation is not worthy of such medicines” [CH3.42] and closes with a prayer [CH3.46].

### Chapter IV. How Physick works upon the body

Physicians dispute whether contraries are cured by contraries, as Hippocrates holds, or like by like, as Paracelsus holds [CH4.1]. The author answers that both may be received; Paracelsus faults not Hippocrates but Galen, who referred cures to bare qualities [CH4.2]. Nature cures, and “the Physitian is but the minister or servant” [CH4.3]; the medicine helps the body’s Balsom “like a coadjutour or privy counsellour” [CH4.3]. Contrary qualities do not cure, because “Nature is not a quality but a vertue” [CH4.6]. The worst diseases spring from the Seed or Astra’s, not the humours [CH4.6], and “Physick is a Spirit (not a body)” [CH4.6].

Man’s Salt, Sulphur and Mercury are preserved by those of the great world, and diseases are sorted by these three [CH4.7–10]. Paracelsus cures ulcers from Salts by Salts [CH4.9], the next paragraph all ulcers by Mercury [CH4.10]; two readings stand: the rules disagree, or, wording-dependent, two words name one remedy [CH4.9–10]. The chapter ends with two Prague stories, of a swallowed knife and swallowed flints [CH4.13; CH4.14].

### Chapter V. The Duty of Nature’s Minister, the Physician

The physician must be “free from every kind of Sect” [CH5.1], yet all sects may be admitted [CH5.3]. Secrets remain for wise men to find [CH5.5], so neither the Ancients nor Theophrastus is wholly rejected or embraced [CH5.7]. The physician is first an interpreter of Nature [CH5.8], second a Spagirus who separates pure from impure [CH5.12]. Paracelsus is praised as “the true Monarch of Physicke” [CH5.12] and defended against the charge, which the author reports to reject, that his cures were the Devil’s [CH5.15]. Every true theologian is a philosopher, and every true philosopher a theologian [CH5.19].

Tartar, the excrement of meat and drink coagulated in man [CH5.35; CH5.44], explains the first of Wimpeneus’s three respects in which the Paracelsians cure the greatest diseases [CH5.31; CH5.32]. Paracelsus is to be read, not as an oracle [CH5.51]; he wrote in a magical style for the children of Wisdom [CH5.54]. Physician and surgeon should be one [CH5.56], and Old and New Physick may be reconciled [CH5.61]. Eight reasons are given why cures fail [CH5.68–81]; diseases from God’s wrath yield only to a celestial and spiritual cure, by repentance and faith [CH5.82].

### Chapter VI. Of that one only chief Medicine

The chief Medicine is “this most secret Secret of secret Secrets” [CH6.1]. To pious and holy philosophers it assures renewed youth and perpetual health [CH6.2]; its seekers must want wisdom, not wealth [CH6.6; CH6.12]. Few reach it, and only by God’s will [CH6.17; CH6.18]. The philosophers swore never to write it plainly, and spoke only to those who understood allegorically [CH6.21]. The author attacks impostors with golden promises [CH6.22–25] and was granted to “see and taste it, at some Great mans house” [CH6.37]; one drop of a liquor restored a dying man in one night [CH6.38].

The Medicine is named a Fift Essence, “the root of life in Nature” [CH6.51]. It consumes the disease, brings the Humors to their true Temperament, and can keep a man sound to his natural death [CH6.53; CH6.54]. Paracelsus is cleared of the scoff that he sought bodily immortality [CH6.62]. The true long life is also union with God, the Centre [CH6.63; CH6.64]. The chapter weighs how life is prolonged and shortened [CH6.69–74] and ends with a prayer to the Triunity [CH6.77–78].

## Process or not, and what the Preface carries

The division made from the readings finds that the Preface does mark a process. This is its finding, not something the book says of itself. Its basis is the heading of Chapter III and head III of the opening list: Physick got out and prepared by fire [CH3.0; OPEN.26]. It adds the Separatory art [CH3.14] and “The Chymicall way of Subtiliating Extracting, and Separating” [CH3.40]. Its end is “that pure and Christiline matter may be administred to our bodies” [CH3.7]. For the chief Medicine, the division cites “the greatest miracle of Nature wrought by Art” and the “Moyst way of the Ancients” [CH6.38]. It also cites the joining of Sun and Lunary “with the help of Art and Nature”, in a matter the philosophers swore never to write plainly [CH6.21], and a margin note on Gold animated by the Salt of Niture, which it interprets as the chief Medicine’s work (note-only) [N263]. It counts two units.

**The True Physic.** This is Physick of the kind got out and prepared by Fire [CH3.0]. Chapter I treats it as God’s gift, resting on the agreement of the great and little worlds [CH1.1; CH1.4]. Chapter II places it covered as a kernel in rind, in the Seeds and in the Spirits of things [CH2.1; CH2.2; CH2.7], and Chapter III tells how Chymistry frees it, the trial being made by Fire [CH3.7; CH3.14]. “By its onely power all diseases are cured”, the author says, if it be “rightly prepared, and separated from all impurity” [CH1.3]. Chapter IV says it works by helping Nature’s own Balsom, not by contrary qualities [CH4.3; CH4.6]. See [the True Physic in Plain Terms](units/U1.md) and [the True Physic in Plain Language](units/U1_plain.md).

**That one only chief Medicine of the most ancient Philosophers.** The opening sets it above every medicine the author offers [OPEN.6], and the list of heads calls it “commended of many, possessed, yea seen, or believed of very few” [OPEN.29]. Chapter VI says it purges impurity from metallic and human bodies and restores health [CH6.1]. It “reduceth to the true Temperament by making equall the unequall”, and keeps the fore-qualified man who uses it prudently sound to his natural death [CH6.54]. The author says he saw a dying man saved “by giving one onely drop of that Latex or liquor” [CH6.37; CH6.38]. It is named a Fift Essence, “altogether one and the same with the spirit of our body” [CH6.51]. See [that one only chief Medicine of the most ancient Philosophers in Plain Terms](units/U2.md) and [that one only chief Medicine of the most ancient Philosophers in Plain Language](units/U2_plain.md).

## What is covered, and how

The Preface begins by breaking a rule of silence [OPEN.2], yet keeps silences of its own and says where. The author breaks off contemplations that “require a larger narration then can be made of them at this time” [MICRO.44]. In Chapter V he says “nor may I in this unfit place openly speak more of these Mysteries” [CH5.24]. Of the chief Medicine he withholds “the Magicall and super Celestial use” [CH6.1]. Of what he saw at a great man’s house he writes: “Which I forbear to English” [CH6.37]. He has imparted “so far as was lawfull, and so much as was allowable by God”, and “some tedious things should be left obscure to young Schollers and for intelligent & wise men to find out” [CH6.76].

Covered speech is named and defended. Chapter I speaks of difficulties “wrapt up in aenigmaticall obscurity” [CH1.12]. The writings that witness to the chief Medicine are “woven of so many Hieroglyphicall, Magicall, and Mathematicall Coverings” [CH6.1]. Of Paracelsus the author says: “He concealed his mysteries under vulgar and various names”, and warns against taking the similitudes for the truth itself [CH5.55]. A verse runs: “Arts wrapt in Riddles safe doe lye” [CH5.26]. The true philosophers made their knowledge known only to “such who had an understanding allegorically”, and may give names at their pleasure [CH6.21]. Some figures the text explains itself: the kernel and rind [CH2.1], the Spagirus [CH5.12], the bolted Gate [CH6.19]. Others the pages read only as figures, such as Vulcan for fire [OPEN.3]. The joining of the blood of the Sun and the Dew of the Lunary stays in covered words [CH6.21].

To whom are things given? The opening says the Gates of Wisdome “ought alwayes to stand open to ingenious men” [OPEN.2], and the margin says “Only they that are worthy shall understand these things” [N018]. Mineral virtues are disclosed to enlightened men [CH2.5], Physick is discerned by the wise man only [CH4.6], and the Hermetic philosophers left their secrets to sworn disciples [CH5.28]. The chief Medicine is promised to the pious [CH6.2], and even one who holds the key may be refused, “because the Gate was bolted, that is, the Divine Grace and Benediction was denyed and did resist” [CH6.19]. The slothful will never finger these secrets, and there is little danger of the profane grasping them [OPEN.10].

The pages record strains in the book here. Chapter V praises Paracelsus as one who “knew and publisht” hidden secrets [CH5.12], yet asks whether any man with his knowledge would tell it to every man [CH5.27]. It says “A Secret indeed is to be concealed, it belongs onely to God to reveale it” [CH5.28], and later calls foolish the belief “that a Secret looseth its vertue when tis made known” [CH5.62]. Our reading is, with that page, that the later passage rejects the belief about lost virtue, not the dangers of disclosure [CH5.28; CH5.62]. Chapter II calls it folly to think the Creator would hide remedies from men [CH2.8], beside the gift of making Gold potable, which few men have [CH2.5]. The page’s narrowest reading is that God does not hide the remedies, and men fail to know them [N139].

Our reading is that the Preface also speaks of time in two ways. The opening says the Treasures are “to be manifest indifferently to all in due time by the light of Grace and Nature” [OPEN.10]. Chapter V says secrets are ordained for times and nations [CH5.5]. Chapter III says God “will never suffer those Secrets to be known, especially in these corrupt and perilous times” [CH3.42].

## What the Preface leaves open

- Whether the Preface’s gift is to all or to some. It defends giving like the Father who shines on good and bad, and also says only the worthy will understand [OPEN.2; OPEN.10; N018].
- What “mingled together in a fit place” means: Mysteries hidden among other matter so the unworthy pass them by, or simply set in their proper place [OPEN.10].
- What the Light of Nature is: an inward teacher, the likeness of world and man, or a gift from above. The pages give all three as defensible, and Chapter V sets it beside the liberty of human judgement [CH1.1; CH1.5; CH1.18; CH5.1].
- Whether the medicine’s virtue comes from within or is infused from Heaven. Chapter II says it is not from without, then that God infuses it and Heaven gives force [CH2.3; CH2.11; CH2.12]. The page gives two readings: the passages deny different causes, or they stand against each other.
- Where the matter of the philosophers lies: in man, or in every thing [CH3.36; CH3.38; N180].
- Whether the three points of “the Magicall and Cabalisticall Art” are Mind, Faith and Prayer, or herbs, stones and words [CH2.22; N154].
- How the rules for ulcers fit, by Salts in one place and by Mercury in the next [CH4.9–10].
- What the “one thing” is that prolongs life in Chapter V: the universal medicine, or the Quint-Essence of Gold [CH5.68; CH5.36].
- What the Gold animated by Salt of Niture is: the road the seekers leave to the greedy, or a covered speech [CH6.11; N263].
- What the capitals in the words the author forbears to English are: hidden letters, or misprints [CH6.37].
- Whether Nothing names two things, the world left behind and the self given up, or one word used of unlike things [CH6.68].
- What the Magical and super Celestial use of the chief Medicine is, which the author leaves unsaid [CH6.1].
