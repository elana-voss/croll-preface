# Chapter I. Of the True Physick

The chapter treats one thing, named in its first words: “THE true Physick whereof by the Devine assistance I intend here to treat” [CH1.1]. It runs from where that Physick comes from, through the agreement of the great world with man, the Elements, the Astra's and the three Principles, to the cause and cure of diseases.

## Physick comes from God

In his own voice the author says the true Physick “is the mear gift of the most high God; it is not to be sought for or learned from the Heathens, but from God alone” [CH1.1]; the margin sends the reader to “Paracels. his Labyrinth of Physitians” [N036]. Hence “no mortall Master or dead letter can ever teach it so well as he” [CH1.1].

The Physitian “should be born out of the Light of Grace and Nature of the inward and invisible Man” [CH1.1], a teacher also called “the internall Angell, the Light of Nature” [CH1.1]. Physick rests on Nature, “upon which God hath written with his own sacred finger in sublunary things, but especially in perfect Mettalls” [CH1.1]: “God therefore is the true Foundation thereof” [CH1.1]. A margin note adds that teaching is vain for a scholar “whose Nativity is not disposed to that Art” [N037].

Stated, a definition: “Physick is nothing else but the created and incarnate Mercy of our Heavenly Father, bestowed upon poor afflicted Mortalls” [CH1.2], so that the sick may feel the Creator's love and God be glorified. The margin denies that “University books are” its foundation [N038], and, Stated, note-only: “This Physicall essence in Phisick is called Gold.” [N038] Physick is also “written in the book of Nature” [N038], to be read “by Chyromancy and phisiognomy” [N038].

## The Medicine and where it lies

“this Medicine, as naturall Mummy and kernell of Nature, is contained in the vitall Sulphur, as in the treasure of Nature” [CH1.3], and is founded in “the Balsam of Vegetables, Mineralls and Animalls” [CH1.3]. “By its onely power all diseases are cured” [CH1.3], if it be “rightly prepared, and separated from all impurity” [CH1.3] and given by “a Godly skilfull Phisitian” [CH1.3]. How, he promises for later: “as shall be shewed anon” [CH1.3].

## The great world and the little world

The ground of all: “The Foundation of this Physick is according to the agreement of the lesser World Man with the greater and externall world” [CH1.4]. Philosophy teaches of Earth and Water, Astronomy of Firmament and Aire; together they “make up an internall and perfect Phylosopher” [CH1.4]. The great world is to the little “as of a parent to the little World as to the Son” [CH1.4], and one must “duly compare the Anotomy of the World with the Anotomy of Man” [CH1.4].

“The outward World is a speculative Anotomy, wherein we may see, as in a glasse, the lesser World Man” [CH1.5], since what a Physitian must know of man “cannot be understood from the man himselfe” [CH1.5]. They differ in outward form, but “in essence and internall form they are altogether one and the same thing” [CH1.5]. “Heaven and Earth are Man's Parents” [CH1.5], so he who “can Anotomize them, hath attained the true knowledge of their child Man” [CH1.5]. Interpreted: Anotomy, the book's word, means here knowing a thing's parts and powers in its nature; CH1.38 sets it against the “Locall Anotomy of a man and dead corpses” [CH1.38].

Stated, under the margin head “What the Light of Nature is.” [N039]: “the Light of Nature, which is nothing else but a divine Analogy of this visible world with the body of man” [CH1.5]. The phrase is used in more than one way, and several readings are defensible: an inward teacher, beside “the internall Angell” [CH1.1]; the likeness of world and man [CH1.5]; or a gift from above, “that singular inspiration of God” [CH1.18], with “the Firmament is the Light of Nature” [CH1.41].

“Man is an hidden world, because visible things in him are invisible, and when they are made visible then they are diseases, not health” [CH1.6]. He may be known “visibly and invisibly or magically” [CH1.6]; Interpreted, wording-dependent: the or sets magically beside invisibly as its other name. Nature “is its own medicine, disease, and Physitian” [CH1.6]; “Nature, not man, maketh a Physitian.” [CH1.6] Because “the Matter of Man is the Extract of the four Elements” [CH1.6], man must have “a familiarity with all the Elements and their fruits” [CH1.6]; God created the Elements “that they might sustaine and preserve Man with food and Physick” [CH1.6].

## Everything fourfold, and the inhabitants of the Elements

The Elements differ only in form: “in the foure Elements there is but one Anatomy essence and matter, all the differenc being onely in the Form” [CH1.6]. “There are Foure of every thing, one in the Firmament or Heavenly Element” [CH1.6], one each in Aire, Water and Earth: “So there is a fourfold Man” [CH1.6].

The next account is given as Paracelsus's: “Paracelsus faith, that to avoyd an Emptiness in all the four Elements, he created living creatures” [CH1.7] (faith so printed), “inanimate, that is to say, without an Intellectuall Soule” [CH1.7], unlike “Men created after the Image of God” [CH1.7]. Four lists follow with no speaker named, so their voice is unclear: Nimphs, Undens, Melosyns for the Water, with Syrens their Monsters [CH1.8]; Gnoms, Lemurs, Sylphs, Montans, Zonnets for the Earth, with Pigmyes [CH1.9]; Umbratils, Satyrs and others for the Aire, with Gyants [CH1.10]; Vulcanals, Pennats, Salamanders, Superi for “the Fire or the Firmament” [CH1.11], with Zundell. The Flagae are Theophrastus's, “which Theophrastus in his works affirmeth are in many thousands of severall forts incorporated to the Soul of the World” [CH1.11].

## A fourfold Medicine; Astrology and Chymiology

“Thus also there is a fourfold Medicine” [CH1.12]: the fourfold Heart of the Macrocosm answers to the Heart of man, and so every member; “He that knows not this Fundamentall cannot be a good Phisitian” [CH1.12]. On the word of “ancient Records” [CH1.12], “Astrologers and Chymiologers were very near of kin” [CH1.12]; the Caelestiall Astronomy and the inferiour both “have their own Heaven, their own Sun, their own Moon” [CH1.12]. Interpreted: Sun and Moon are used of the inferiour too.

Here understanding is tied to some only. “Those Chymists who by the assistance of divine Grace have attained the Mind” [CH1.12] can unfold “all Phylosophicall difficulties that have been wrapt up in aenigmaticall obscurity” [CH1.12]. Stated: these matters have been wrapt up. Interpreted, narrowest and wording-dependent: the Mind is something reached by Grace, named together with knowing how to fit the properties of upper bodies to lower. Such men “need not travell to India or America to get the knowledge of Phylosophy” [CH1.12].

Given as Severinus's: “as P. Severinus the Dane doth learnedly observe” [CH1.13], the constellation of the seasons is “contained in the Earth, Water, Aire” [CH1.13]. Rejected as a mistake of others: that to the Firmament alone “many of the common Phylosophers by a great mistake have ascribed all Astronomy” [CH1.13]. The margin: “The Astrum of Man and Heaven is but one.” [N046]

## The twofold Heaven and each thing's own Astrum

“There is a twofold Heaven” [CH1.14]: outer, the bodies of the stars; inner, Stated, “the Astrum or invisible and insensible body in all the Stars of Heaven” [CH1.14], which “is the Spirit of the World, or Nature, as Paracelsus calls it, the Hylech” [CH1.14]. Several defensible readings: “as Paracelsus calls it” names Paracelsus's word for Nature, or for the Hylech, or for both. Man's inner heaven is “the Olimpick spirit” [CH1.14], and the invisible Man is “one and the same thing with the Spirit of the world, as whitenesse is with snow” [CH1.14].

So all things, “even stones and mettalls” [CH1.15], have “a syderiall spirit, which is called Heaven or the Astrum, the secret Forger, from which every Formation, Figure and Colour of things proceedeth” [CH1.15]. Man's own is “The Sun of the Microcosm” [CH1.15], which Paracelsus is said to call “the Ens or Being of the seed” [CH1.15].

The author narrows his words, Stated: form comes not from “the visible coales of Heaven, nor of the invisible body of the Astra's in the Firmament, but of every things own proper Astrum” [CH1.16]. He rejects “the false Philosophers” who think “the stars of the Firmament do infuse virtue into herbs and trees; no in no wise” [CH1.16]. The upper stars “excite and stir up the growth of inferiour things” [CH1.16] and provide dew, rain and seasons, but “do not infuse the internall Astrum into things that grow” [CH1.16].

“the externall stars do neither incline nor necessitate Man” [CH1.16]; “Man rather inclines the Stars, and by his Magicall imagination infecteth them” [CH1.16]. Our conditions come “from the hand of God through the breathing in of the breath of life” [CH1.16], and “Mans Reason ought to rule the externall Stars” [CH1.16]; if “the children of Adam” [CH1.16] did not “provoke our Father with our sins” [CH1.16], “we should alwayes find him meek and gentle towards us” [CH1.16]. “The course of the externall Firmament is free with its constellations, and is governed by none” [CH1.17], and so the Firmament in man is “not at all governed by the outward Firmament” [CH1.17]. As “the Aire or Sun cannot set an apple or pear upon the tree” [CH1.17], the outer heaven gives no virtue to growing things.

## The golden chain

The fruits of all the Astra's help one another, and that help is named, Stated: “This visible and invisible fellowship of Nature is that golden chaine so much commended, this is the marriage of heaven and riches, these are Plato's rings” [CH1.18]; in the margin, “a Divine Series and Order serving Providence” [N051]. It is “that dark and close Phylosophy so hard to be known in the most inward and secret parts of Natare” [CH1.18]. For it Democritus, Pythagoras, Plato, Apollonius and others travelled to the Indies and “to Hermes his Pillars in Aegypt” [CH1.18].

## The three Principles

“there are THREE Principles of all things which are found in every compound body” [CH1.19]: “No body compos'd by Nature can by any dissolving skill be parted into more or lesse then Three, viz. Into Mercury or liquor, Sulphur or Oyle, and Salt” [CH1.19]. The second ground: “the Holy Triunity when it spake that Triune word FIAT created all things Triune” [CH1.19], seen in a Spagiricall resolution, glossed “A seperation of purity from impurity, or truth from falshood.” [N052] The world is “nothing else but a fume or smoak coagulated or curded together of the three substances” [CH1.19]. Green wood shows them: a watery moisture and an oily one, which the fire consumes, and the Salt that “remains in the ashes” [CH1.19].

“The Salt by coagulation gives Solidity, Colour and Tast to all bodies” [CH1.19]; the Sulphur “gives the Body Substance and Transmutation” [CH1.19]; Mercury, “like the Elixir giveth the vertues” [CH1.19] and cherishes the two others. A further view is others': “Some Theophrastaeans, who have more narrowly and exactly searched out the causes of hidden things doe add a Fourth” [CH1.21], the Spirit, answering to Aire [CH1.21].

## The Elements, out of Severinus

“we shall add a few things concerning them out of that short Treatise of Severinus” [CH1.22], the author says, closing later: “Thus much out of Severinus” [CH1.25]. Between these marks the matter is given as Severinus's.

“The true and purely spirituall Elements are the keepers, nurses, places, Mines, wombs and receptacles of the whole Creation” [CH1.22], in two Globes like an egg [CH1.22]. In them the Creator planted “the Light and Seminall causes of all things” [CH1.23]. “The Seeds and Astra's, those bonds of things, lay hid in the invisible Treasures of the Elements from the beginning of the Creation” [CH1.23], and “The knowledge of the Elements cannot be attained unto without these Seeds” [CH1.23].

Some bodies have “onely properties without Arcane or hidden secrets” [CH1.24], “in which there is no vertue for curing diseases” [CH1.24]; in others “a seperation made of the strong from the weak, of the pure from the impure” [CH1.25] is possible. What is said of Fire, Aire, Water, Earth and of their likeness in man is “to be understood of Elementated Elements” [CH1.25], “for the true Elements are Spirituall” [CH1.25]: Element has two senses. Then, in the author's voice: “least that which he hath said should seem obscure to the inconsiderate Reader, we will now speak more clearly of the Elements” [CH1.25].

## The Elements more clearly

He that would know “those four Pillars of the World” [CH1.26] shall “understand himselfe and his own Originall” [CH1.26]. “The body of the Element is a dead and dark thing; the Spirit is the life, and is divided into Astra's” [CH1.27], and “no visible body is of it selfe and from it selfe, but from its own invisible Element and Astrum” [CH1.27]. Of the stars, “Nostoch saith, they feed on fire” [CH1.28]. “Our visible Elements are but the bodies and houses of others” [CH1.28], and things joined in a visible body choke “the force, power and operation of the inner Spirit” [CH1.28].

Each Element is twofold. Of the Earth: “The Externall is not the Element, but the body of the Element, and is the Sulphur, Mercury, Salt” [CH1.29]. Of the Water: its fruits “are digged out of the Earth” [CH1.30], for “the Astrum of every Element brings forth and bayes its fruits in a strange region or matrix” [CH1.30]. The Aire “is the Balsam of all created things” [CH1.32]. The stars grew out of Heaven and abide there [CH1.33], and “the seven Rulers of the world are nothing else but the fruits of Fire” [CH1.34]. Stated, of what Sun properly means: “not the body of the Sun, but the Spirit in the body, is the Sun properly; the like also may be said of Man” [CH1.34].

## Seed, death and the second life

“the four Astra's of the said Elements are the Seeds in the four matrices or wombs” [CH1.35]. “Angels cannot increase themselves because they want a body” [CH1.35]. When the Seed rots, the Astrum passes into a new body, “as Christ himselfe sets it forth by a similitude and example in a graine of Wheat” [CH1.35; N068].

“Putrefaction consumeth and separateth the old Nature, and bringeth new fruit.” [CH1.36] So of eternal life, “death is the cause of the glorifying of the body” [CH1.36]; so in the work: “'Tis necessary that the first life of hearbs and medicines should die that the second life by the Chymists help may be attained through Putrefaction and Regeneration” [CH1.37]. Then “the Three First discover themselves with their hidden vertues” [CH1.37], and “without Regeneration no hid Secret of Physick can be attained to” [CH1.37]. Interpreted: Regeneration is used of the Chymist's work beside the words of the glorified body; the chapter lays the two together.

Man has “the Physicall body of Man, which is nourished from the Earth, and Sydereall body which liveth by the Firmament” [CH1.37]; growing things come “out of the invisible Seed” [CH1.37], not from the four humors.

## The Anotomy of disease and cure

“It is not the Locall Anotomy of a man and dead corpses, but the Essentiated and Elemented Anotomy of the World and man that discovereth the disease and cure” [CH1.38]; “the Anotomy of the diseases and of the medicines is but one” [CH1.38]. The chief thing, Stated, is the Concordance of Nature: “how he may make the Astrum of the Physick or of the magicall Heaven agree with the internall Astrum and Olimpus of Man” [CH1.38].

Diseases “doe not proceed from the four fictitious humours or qualities, but rather from the Seed” [CH1.39]. “the Three First are the cause of all diseases” [CH1.40]: united, the body is sound; where not, “sicknesse and the root of the first death hath taken footing” [CH1.40]. Some diseases are “Astrall or Firmamentall” [CH1.40], from the Firmament in man. “Heaven is the Father and teacher of all Arts, except Divinity and holy Righteousnesse” [CH1.40], and “all Believers and Regenerate men are hid from, and unknown to Astronomers” [CH1.40]; for this the author sends the reader to “the Sage and deep Phylosophy of Paracelsus” [CH1.40]. The margin: “The Galenists doe know nothing of these Astral cures” [N074].

## Not cured by contraries: the Arcana

The Astra's or Elements, “which are Spirits” [CH1.42], are “without any quality neither hot, nor cold nor dry, nor moyst” [CH1.42], but what is produced out of them has qualities; Snow, Raine, Thunder and the like proceed “out of the Three First, i. e. Mercury, Salt, and Sulphur” [CH1.42]. Hence the conclusion: “diseases are not cured by contraries, as if heat were to expell cold” [CH1.43], but “by the secret things or Astra's which the Chymist can reduce out of the last matter into the first” [CH1.43]. Stated: “These Arcana or hid things are actually neither cold nor hot” [CH1.43]. “Of this sort are the Fift Essences, Magisteryes, and the like.” [CH1.43]
