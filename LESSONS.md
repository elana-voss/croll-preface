# Lessons

What changed from the original plan while the work ran, why, and what the next book project should do from the start. Newest last.

1. **Plan review before the first milestone (2026-11-20).** A Fable review of the plan found the estimates at about half of Geber's measured rates (8M against about 17M), facts counted from a first extraction that dropped verse, notes' gaps and italics, no consumer of the readings tried before the costliest milestone, and no answer for a book that marks no process. Next time: estimate from the last book's measured rates, count facts from the XML, and pilot every consumer of the reading format before the readings run.
2. **The paired pages present what the book carries (2026-11-20, the owner).** The plan made the Plain Terms and Plain Language pages depend on a process, so a book without one would have had no primary result. Rewritten (Fable medium, `work/plan_review/review2.md`): the pairs are always present, one per unit the book's own marking sets apart, a process or whatever the book has instead. Next time: define the paired deliverable by what the book carries from the start, never by a process.
3. **Extraction review (M1, 2026-11-20).** The first test compared the text with every space removed, so it could not see the XML file's own indentation, which the code had kept inside words. Two cases: an expanded abbreviation ("per que" for "perque") and gaps beside a line-end break (11 notes). The review agent found them. It also found that a quote check matching part of a word passed, and that apostrophes and "&" were miscounted. The plan's figures from an earlier count (368 paragraphs, 7,487 note words) were corrected to the extraction's. Next time: from the start, treat whitespace next to a line-end break or inside an expansion as layout, test words and spacing (not only signs) against the XML, and make quote matching whole-word.
4. **Pilot scripts review (M2, 2026-11-20).** The review agent found the scripts trusted agent output: a malformed `from`, a blank reference, a quote of only a gap, an unclosed quotation mark or a run written `OPEN.3–OPEN.7` passed or crashed instead of being reported. Readers and reconcilers that ran the quote check and the trial on their own output before handing back came back clean. Next time: write each checker against malformed agent output from the start, and have every agent run the checks on its own output until they pass.
5. **Second reader and headword lines (M2 pilot, 2026-11-20).** One reader alone found 28.6 % of the reconciled observations (almost all reader B), so the second reader stays. The top headword stands in 61 paragraphs, far over one bundle: the lexicon writer gets a spread sample of its lines (24 here) and only the candidate observations that name it. The pilot sample left out six paragraphs its candidates cite, so the writer could not use them. Next time: plan each headword's share of lines before the lexicon bundles are built, and always include the paragraphs its candidates cite.
6. **Readings (M3, 2026-11-20).** All 18 chunks read twice and reconciled; one reader alone found 21.5 % of the reconciled observations, almost all reader B (Fable), so the second reader earned its cost. Each finished agent woke the main session, and the editor demands a visible line in every turn, so the owner got a status line per agent. Next time: a turn with nothing for the owner writes only "·" from the start, and each finished chunk's readings are committed as it completes.
7. **Section pages (M4, 2026-11-20).** Sections over the agent's bundle limit (CH5, CH6) were written and corrected in halves and joined by script; the joins passed both checks. Writers named the author by a name the book never prints, and correctors split on it (two kept it because the voice label is `CROLL`); the core rules now say the label is only a label and pages say "the author". A possessive-only name in the book (Noe's) was flagged as an outside word until the check learnt stems. Next time: state from the start how pages name the author, and plan halves for long sections before the first writer runs.
8. **Paired pages (M5, 2026-11-21).** The division settled two units: the True Physick (CH1–CH5) and the one chief Medicine of the most ancient philosophers (CH6). The first reviewer bundle dropped every observation cited by a margin note's id, because notes were filtered as if they were paragraphs; the script now maps a note to its paragraph. A unit's observations ran to 47,000 tokens even with notes cut, so their quotes are cut to 50-character locators (the bundle gives the line in full) and the lines get 36,000 tokens. Correctors given only the shared rules undid parts of the page's own form: the Plain mark, the last heading and the opening's pointer to the other page; a corrector's bundle should carry the writer's form rules from the start. Writers ran over length (5,896 words against about 4,000); a length line in the scripts' findings had the corrector cut it. A shell `grep` for curly quotation marks matched en dashes byte by byte: such checks go by Python.
9. **Synthesis (M6, 2026-11-21).** The page written from the section pages cited lines worth 95,000 tokens, over the corrector's limit, so it was corrected in three parts of 900–1,450 words, each with the lines it cites, and joined by script; the parts' corrections were real (wrong voices, claims wider than the lines). The outside-word check counted link targets (`md`) as words; it now skips them. Next time: plan a page that cites the whole book to be corrected in parts from the start, and tell a part's corrector to leave out the closing lines.
10. **Word list (M7, 2026-11-21).** 137 headwords read at two or more places, after merging variant spellings, went into 9 batches by script (lines per batch within the bundle limit), each written and then corrected; every corrector made real narrowings (claims no quote shows, Stated made Interpreted, wrong voices, quotes in another's voice). The first corrector, given only the shared rules, set a headword back to the book's spelling; the corrector's prompt now carries the writer's form rule (headwords in today's spelling). The outside-word check flags such headwords (spagyric, olympic, philosophists) though the book spells them otherwise; these flags are accepted when the headword is the today's form of a book word. Next time: give the corrector the writer's form rules from the start, and let the outside check accept a headword whose listed spellings are in the book.
11. **Lorebook subjects and trigger test (M8, 2026-11-21).** The code review found that SillyTavern matches a key of several words anywhere in the text (no whole-word check), that a selective entry with no secondary keys fires ungated, and that recursion scans the chat with the inserted content; the model of World Info now does the same. The first trigger test, with placeholder content, missed 66 wanted subjects on 40 book lines: 44 of 75 subjects are gated on everyday words and the 27 context words rarely stood in a natural line. Letting every distinctive word (context words, keys of ungated subjects, others' open keys) open a gated subject halved the misses; what remains are lines made only of everyday words, which gating is meant to pass over; 29 of 33 core subjects fired, no everyday line fired anything. Next time: build the gate from all distinctive keys from the start, and judge the trigger test per subject (fires at least once) as well as per line.
12. **Lorebook texts and acceptance (M8, 2026-11-21).** Three writers (Opus) wrote 75 texts, 9,099 words, from the word-list entries; all passed the script check at once, and three checkers (Opus) still made 15–25 corrections each, nearly all statements stronger than the entries (a reading given as certain, an added reason, rank or name). The trigger test gave the same failures with the texts as with placeholders, so the texts change only recursion (at most 6 entries on a line). LOREBOOK says the report goes to the reviewer with the build, but no prompt existed for it: an acceptance prompt was written at the end (Fable mended keys: quicksilver, mumia, Word of God). The key check refused an open key that holds the subject's own gated key; that is now allowed and tested. Three core subjects (centre, mind, the Word on its own) still fire on no line made only of everyday words, by design of the gate. Next time: write the acceptance prompt with the others, and let open keys contain gated keys from the start.
