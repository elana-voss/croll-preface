CH3.22 | position | CROLL | “First, Because so great vertue and strong operations cannot be stampt upon such soft matter, as is that of Plants and living things” | First reason: the soft matter of plants and living things cannot hold so strong an impression.

CH3.23 | position | CROLL | “there being scarce any Oyntment prepared that is not made of an imperfect mettall or minerall” | imperfect mettalls | Second reason: surgical ointments, nearly all made of an imperfect metal or mineral, show such virtue.
CH3.23 | position | CROLL | “perfect things doubtlesse have received of the Omnipotent far greater force and vertues” | Drawn from the imperfect to the perfect: perfect things have far greater force.
CH3.24 | position | CROLL | “could by no means so contemper the mixture of those bodies that they should attaine to such admirable power and admit of the solid and stable Nature of a Balsom” | Balsom | Third reason: Nature makes living things and plants for many uses, so their mixture cannot reach the solid, stable nature of a Balsom.
CH3.25 | position | CROLL | “Nature therefore by length of time can adorn precious stones and other metallary bodies with a greater faculty of working” | Fourth reason: the long time of generation gives stones and metallary bodies a greater faculty.
