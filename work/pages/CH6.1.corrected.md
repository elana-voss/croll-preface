# Chap. VI. Of That One Only Chief Medicine of the Most Ancient Philosophers

The head names one single chief Medicine of the most ancient philosophers [CH6.0]. This half praises it, says who may seek it, why its holders hide it, attacks those who falsely claim it, and gives the author's own witness and the older philosophers' account of it [CH6.1–50].

## The greatest gift

The author calls this physick the gift “than which a greater gift of wisdom we never read that divine bounty bestowed on man”, and “this most secret Secret of secret Secrets” [CH6.1]; the margin calls it an occult Minerva and a Jewel beyond price [N253]. It purges the “Plannets of inferior Astronomy”, for “it segregateth all extraneous Sulphureity and impure Terrestreity from metallick and Humane bodies”, and it restores human health [CH6.1]. Interpreted: since the bracket names metallic bodies, these planets most narrowly stand for metals.

A further use he withholds: “to say nothing now of the Magicall and super Celestial use” [CH6.1]. He gives only its condition, the influence of the Sun and Moon and a fourth revolution finished upon its native earth [CH6.1; N254].

He rests all this on antiquity and on eye-witnesses of his time. As God is wonderful in all his works, “so doth he usually hide his wonderfull Gifts in wonderfull men”, whose writings are “woven of so many Hieroglyphicall, Magicall, and Mathematicall Coverings” [CH6.1]. Stated: the truth comes under covers. It promises “a removall of old age and renewing of youth, perpetuall health, and honest food and rayment”, but only “to the Pious and holy Phylosophers dignified by Nature and Education” [CH6.2].

## Neither denial nor boast

The author will not deny these wonders, nor revile them, as most do, “as if they were but the speculations of idle men” [CH6.3], a view reported to be rejected. Revilers, “whom the Phylosophers call fools”, are shut out [CH6.3]. Some wait to hear whether he will claim the preparation, as “greedy Phylosophists use to doe” [CH6.4]. His margin answers: “Here I would be taken as an Index, or as one standing at the door to direct others the way they should goe” [N255]. Having promised “I would set down at least those things which I had made tryall of”, he will falsify nothing [CH6.5].

## Who may seek it

The Art is “not of Sophisters, but Phylosophers”, though “none aboundeth with so many Imposters as this” [CH6.6]. Its true seekers are furnished, since “it cannot be that a poor or covetous man should spend his time in the study of Phylosophy”, and, after Solomon's example, pray “not for wealth, but wisdome” [CH6.6]. They love Heaven, secrets and Nature, and refuse no honest labour of the hands [CH6.7–9; N256]. They desire to use the gift for the end the text names itself, “to wit, the Praise and Honour of God, and the good of his needy neighbor”, “in a constant Taciturnity, without pride” [CH6.10].

They are “the Children of the golden doctrine (whose gold is the Omnipotent God)” [CH6.11]. Stated: the text glosses its own word golden. They seek it for men's health, “leaving the mettalls to those who with an impious hunger” long to be rich [CH6.11; N257]. “A Phylosopher must covet Nothing but Wisdome” [CH6.12]. The gift comes “either by the immediate inspiration of the most High, or by the expert ocular demonstration of a wise Master in this Art” [CH6.12].

## Few reach it, and only by God's will

Few reach “the mount of Chymistry” [CH6.13]. None helps another without long proof of his “pure and Harpocraticall Faith” [CH6.14]. The margin asks where such a Phaenix is found, and says entrance is only by revelation or from one “that hath the Secret, & teacheth it while he liveth” [N258]. Voice unclear: Paracel. is cited beside these words.

The seeker “must not be a slave to other mens judgment, nor live upon their purse, and must retire himselfe” [CH6.15], for “Too much acquantance hinder them / Who labour to find out this Gem.” [CH6.16]. The verse's voice is unclear [CH6.16; N259].

The author gives the agreed word of reverend antiquity: none found the secret by wit or experience, for “it is somthing Divine and hid, and that which is above Reason” [CH6.17]; God “hath reavealed and made it plaine to his faithfull wise ones” [CH6.17; N260]. So among thousands “there is not one to whom the doors of Nature are unlockt” [CH6.18], since “it is not expedient for all to their salvation, that they should have Honors & riches with their health” [CH6.18; N261]. This holds “when men seek the Kingdome of God, and study to finde out the Celestiall Stone in the last place” [CH6.18]. Several readings: the Stone is to be sought only after the Kingdom of God; or men put both last, and fail.

Some get “the key of the Phylosophicall Garden (as I know some that have)”, yet cannot enter “because the Gate was bolted, that is, the Divine Grace and Benediction was denyed and did resist” [CH6.19]. Stated: the text glosses its gate. Impostors holding a true ferment “prepar'd by others” could not multiply it, “by reason of the ignorance of the principle or beginning” [CH6.19].

## Why the philosophers write in covers

The work is “hid under the robe of the Phylosophicall Virgin, which even one brother would not teach another” [CH6.20].

The genuine philosophers “doe study and strive to be harsh and rugged” [CH6.21], have “covenanted and sworn they would never write it plainly in any Book”, and none has made it known except to “such who had an understanding allegorically” [CH6.21]. “God would not manifest this knowledg to all, to avoyd offence.” [N262]. They have “power to dispence or give Names at their pleasure” [CH6.21]. Interpreted: their names are not to be read at face value.

What they affirm: none reaches the end “before the fat or blood of the Sun and the Dew of the Lunary, be joyned into one body by the circular wheel of the Elements with the help of Art and Nature in the form of an Hexagon” [CH6.21]. Interpreted: these are covered words [CH6.21; N264]. The margin says the whole business turns as “Gold animated by the Salt of Niture is made the chief subject of the Phylosophers metallick Physicks” [N263]. Several readings beside CH6.11: the seekers leave metals only as a road to wealth, or the note speaks covertly. And all hangs on grace, “for the Spirit proceedeth from Grace, who inspireth whom he will” [CH6.21].

## Against the impostors

Since “all mans endeavor is but vaine except God prosper it” [CH6.22], the author wonders that great men trust “the golden promises of circumforaneous, vitious, and most lying Impostors” [CH6.22; N266]. These Gymnosophists “inrich the eares of credulous men that they may make their pockets poor” [CH6.23], and deal in “painting white and red, and pargetting womens faces” [CH6.24]. Several readings: cosmetics, or a figure for false colour. Alphidius foretold that “instead of the Tincture they should find only the colour, instead of the Hermetick Stone a pibble or glasse” [CH6.25]. The margin adds Lully's curse: “He that publisheth this Art, let him die an evill death, saith Lully” [N268].

## The adept's silence and the last day

The adept, who “hath happily labored like Hermes more then twenty years”, “will be lesse afraid to die an hundred most cruell deaths” than publish the treasure to the unworthy, for he must account for “the Talent committed to him” [CH6.27]. At the last day “the heat of the Centre united to the heat of Sun shall set all corruption of the Elements on fire” [CH6.28], and what is pure remains “like a pure, cleare, incorruptible and fixed Essence in a serene resplendent Chrystalin Earth” [CH6.29]. “The property of fire is to seperate the impurity of the Elements.” [N269]. Interpreted: the last day is drawn in the terms of the medicine's purging.

## A wish for princes

The author wishes princes would set skilled men to the Three Families of Nature, “viz. Animalls, Vegetables, and Mineralls”, “separating them by Fire into their Three First (things.)” [CH6.30]. This would show “the TRIUNE naked DIANA” with “a most limpid or clear MERCURIAL, a most garnished SULPHUREAN or oyly, and a most bright pure SALINE form” [CH6.31]. Stated: the three forms are named as hers. Francis the First meant to try one, “but was prevented by death” [CH6.32]. With “the sole fountaine being reserved to it selfe for its own uses”, the spare streams would serve the poor [CH6.33]. God gives “by asking, seeking, and knocking” [CH6.34; N271].

## The King's highway

“For this onely is the Kings high-way”, to Nature's secrets and to God, “alterity being swallowed up of unity” [CH6.35]. “There is no recovering or returning to what we have lost but by the Crosse and Death” [CH6.35; N272].

## The author's own witness

The author sought it “not to the end that I might make Gold and Silver” and, hindered by envy, says “I comfort my selfe with the cogitation onely” [CH6.36; N273]. Yet he was granted “that I should see and taste it, at some Great mans house” [CH6.37]. Words he does not English follow, with odd capitals, and then: “Which I forbear to English.” [CH6.37]. Several readings: the capitals hide letters to be read apart, or are misprints.

The metamorphoses he saw were made in the cold, “the Moyst way of the Ancients not as yet intensly exalted to the eye of the Basilisk” [CH6.38]. A dying man was saved “by giving one onely drop of that Latex or liquor”, a storehouse “in which the whole world was centred”, in one night [CH6.38]. But “many diseases are a Divine innate punishment, for which there is no cure in Nature” [CH6.38], and “There is no Physitian can cure death, but Christ.” [N275]. The margin's Basilisk “like Lightning suddenly and unawares burneth up any imperfect mettall whatsoever” [N274].

## Metals and bodies alike

Metals are cleansed like men's bodies and “restored to their True Health, which is aureity or goldness”, “for mettals doe not differ in specie but onely in accidents” [CH6.39]. This is “justly not to be made known (though it be truer then true) to our Vulgar sort” [CH6.40]. Their disbelief is a gain, protecting true students [N276].

## Paracelsus and Lullius

The Astral physician needs this to cure “the four Monarchs of all diseases, the Epilepsie, Gout, Dropsie, Leprosie” [CH6.41]. Paracelsus cured them “through the help of Christ, not of the Devill”, “proving in some of his works that God taught him the medicine” [CH6.41; N277]. It heals “as a Carpenter that squareth all kind of timber” [CH6.42]. Lullius, approved, held that “between the Artist and God the first cause, there ought to be an agreement without contrariety” [CH6.44]. Some eminent men before, and “two publick Phylosophers of divers Nations in our time”, came to a wretched end, “answerable to the unworthy publishing of this most True Phylosophicall Art” [CH6.44].

## The ancient philosophers and their silence

The ancients were “these private not publick Secretaries of Nature” who rejoiced “in Quiet Silence” [CH6.46; N279]. Paracelsus is given as calling Phylosophy Adept “Long Life and free from all infirmity, even till the Naturall death” [CH6.47]. Its virtues are “concentred into one masse” [CH6.49]. Those of Hermes school were “altogether dumb in keeping close the secret of the Art by a constant Taciturnity” [CH6.50], speaking only “in a picture of Aenigmaticall words” [N281]. Yet the reason they give is quoted: the Medicine is “the Life and inlightning Light”, “the Fift Essence compounded of the four Elements” [CH6.50].
