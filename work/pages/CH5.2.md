## A secret kept, and handed only to sworn disciples

Croll says plainly: “A Secret indeed is to be concealed, it belongs onely to God to reveale it”, for disclosure “commonly it exposeth to death, or prison, or reproach” [CH5.28]. “The true Hermetick Phylosophers took an Oath” not to “defile the virginity of Nature”, yet “have they bequeathed their secrets to their sworn disciples”, and even these “are with much sweat to be searched out” [CH5.28].

Stated: the secret passes only to sworn disciples [CH5.28]. Interpreted: “the virginity of Nature” is a figure; the narrowest reading is Nature's hidden working, which the oath keeps from being laid open [CH5.28].

## Whom Paracelsus condemns, and what true Method is

Men of shallow judgement think Paracelsus would be “the sole and onely Physitian in all the world”; Croll reports this to reject it [CH5.29]. In his own voice: “he only condemns the common rable of Physitians who are altogether ignorant both of Theory & Practice” [CH5.29].

The “fallacious Medodists” charge “the genuine Chymists that they have lost the right method”; Croll calls it false, holding that “Paracelsus observed onely that method that was agreeable to Reason and Experience” and that “we are not so much to regard by whom, as what it is that is spoken” [CH5.30]. He defines: “True Method consisteth in the knowledg of the Disease, and the Cure of it” [CH5.31].

## Three respects; the first opens on Tartar

Wimpeneus “learnedly sheweth that greatest and most grievous Diseases now a dayes are in three respects cured by the Paracelsians” [CH5.31]. The three follow as First, Secondly, Thirdly; the text does not mark where his account ends and Croll's begins [CH5.32; CH5.45; CH5.46].

First, “the Diseases are more perfectly known”: while all were reduced to the four humours, diseases from Tartar could not be cured [CH5.32]. Croll turns aside, “since we are here fallen upon Tartar”, and closes the turn with “So much for Tartar.” [CH5.32; CH5.44].

## How food is separated

“The first Ens or Being toward life” arises “by the Archaeus, or digestion of the Stomack” and is reduced to “Sulphur, Mercury, and Salt, as is plain in the three chief Emunctories”: urine, entrails, sweat [CH5.33]. A margin note beside it says “The first digestion of the stomack is not digestion” [N217]; the lines given do not reconcile the two.

The Archaeus is Stated: the stomach is “the instrument of Mans Archaeus, or the Internal, innate Chymist which God hath planted in Man” [CH5.34]. All food holds a Tartar “hurtful to our Health”; a weak stomach sends the impure to the liver, which “seperateth the pure from the impure, viz. the Rubie from the Chrystall” [CH5.34]. The stones are Stated: “The Rubie is the nourishment of all the members”; the Christall becomes urine [CH5.34]. If the liver fails, the Spirit of the Salt coagulates the matter in the kidneys “into Sand or Tartar” [CH5.34].

The definition: “Tartar therefore is the excrement of meat and drink, which by the spirit of Salt is coagulated in Man” [CH5.35].

## Kinds of Tartar, and how they are known

“Paracelsus distinguisheth Tartar into that which is strange or forraine proceeding from meat and drink, and that which is innate of the cruor or hereditary blood” [CH5.36]. The innate kind yields “onely by the Quint-Essence of Gold which reneweth the whole body”; whose claim this is the sentence does not show [CH5.36]. Tartar “is the mother of almost all diseases of all coagulate bodies” [CH5.37].

Four sorts spring “from the fourfold fruits of the Elements”: Earth, Water, the flesh of beasts and fowls, and the Firmament [CH5.38–42]. From the last come “Astrall diseases”, which “the universall medicine powerfull expelleth” [CH5.42]. This four is counted by source, that of CH5.35 by form in the body; the lines given do not relate them [CH5.35; CH5.38].

The kinds “shew themselves in the Urine, and are judged of by the art of separation”, and “The disease is cured by the same Element that was the cause of it.” [CH5.43].

## The heat that separates

What parts Tartar from nourishment is “our Naturall Heat, or the microcosmicall heat of the Sun and Moon that is in us” [CH5.44]. Weak, it must be strengthened “by the macrocosmicall Heat of Sun and Moon, if it could be got”, that is, “the most simple matter which the most Great God generated of the spirit of the world” [CH5.44]. Stated: that spirit is “one and the same with the spirit of our body” [CH5.44; N220]. Interpreted: the matter is not named; the narrowest reading is one substance left unnamed, with “if it could be got” marking it as hard to obtain [CH5.44].

Hence “the Archaeus of the stomack must destroy the Tartar of the Elements least it be turned into the Tartar of Man”, for “the spirit of the Sun which is Lord and Master of coagulation in various subjects will breed the Stone” [CH5.44]. In CH5.35 the spirit of Salt coagulates; the lines given do not relate the two [CH5.35; CH5.44].

## The second and third respects

“Secondly, Because we have more perfect medicines now then heretofore, that is to say, of Mineralls”, found by “the Sons of Cadmus”, a name left unexplained; Paracelsus “would have a steely medicine applied as proper for an iron disease” [CH5.45]. Thirdly, “the Harmony between the great and little world” shows which medicine suits which member: Gold, Pearles, Saffron for the heart [CH5.46]. The little world is man, whose “frame” the author of diseases destroys [CH5.44].

## Physicians quarrel among themselves

“Whereas Paracelsus offendeth Galen, Galen did the same to others” [CH5.47]. Five disputes are named, such as Argenterius against Fernelius, with no words of theirs [CH5.48]. Croll asks how many millions “will still perish” before they agree [CH5.49].

## Read Paracelsus, but not as an oracle

He exhorts “all Students of Hippocraticall and Galenicall Physick” to read “this new Phylosopher and Physitian” with diligence, like bees “segregating the best from the worst” [CH5.50]; not as “Gospel-Oracles”, since “he himselfe hath recanted something of his over hasty writings” [CH5.51]. To those who “throw away the kernell and pick up onely the husks”, he says: pray “for the spirit, that ye may understand the dead letter of Theophrastus” [CH5.53]. Stated, in a figure: the writings have a husk and a kernel, and the spirit to read them is asked of God [CH5.53].

## Why Paracelsus wrote in a magical style

Against the charge of contradiction: “he wrote in a Magicall stile; not for the vulgar sort, but for himselfe and the understanding children of Wisdome” [CH5.54]. Had he written plainly, vulgar Alchymists would have “prostituted the Art” [CH5.54].

Stated: “He concealed his mysteries under vulgar and various names”, so “we must not take the similitudes for the truth it selfe” [CH5.55]. Reading them needs “a Delian swimmer, a most acute and sharp wit, a Magicall Understanding” [CH5.55]. Magick, a word that stands at a gap in the print, is Stated: not witchcraft, but “the plenary knowledge of occult Nature”, working “by a fit application of Agents and Patients, observing the consent and dissent of things” [CH5.55].

## Physician and surgeon in one, working by hand

“every Surgeon should be a Physician, and every Physitian a Chyrurgion, that there may be a sound Bridgroom for a sound Bride” [CH5.56]. The physician prepares his own medicines, “to reduce them to their pure simplicity”, for “neither is Sugar free from faeculent impurity” [CH5.56]. “judgement without practice is altogether barren and fruitlesse”; Nature hands remedies “to her Servant to make them up” [CH5.57]. The Rationall Physitians, who cry out against the Chymists “as if they were no Physitians”, stand helpless at the bed “because they can prepare their medicines but onely with their Reason, not at all with their hand” [CH5.57].

## False followers

Croll will not defend those who claim Paracelsus but “neither acknowledge the minde of the Author in his Theory, nor doe any thing praise-worthy in Practice” [CH5.58]. The “false and lying Theophrasteans” cure “the party and his disease at once with a turse of the Church-yard” [CH5.59]. Interpreted (wording-dependent): the cure is the patient's death. Though they “doe sometime exceed many Galenists”, “no conscientious man will ever approve” their practice [CH5.60].

## Old and New Physick reconciled

Since “the Paracelsian Theory of Chymicall Physick as yet is wrapt up in perplexities and some obscurities”, students should learn of the Chymists to prepare medicines but “observe the method of Hippocrates in curing diseases”, so “these two Schools of Old and New Physick may be reconcil'd without contradiction” [CH5.61].

## Prayer, and Physick made common

What an honest physician gives “is to be accounted of as the hand of God” [CH5.62]. Some withhold Physick lest, made common, “or as they say Prophaned, they should lose their gaines” [CH5.62]. Croll rejects the vulgar belief “that a Secret looseth its vertue when tis made known”, since “neither God nor Nature hath made every man a Physitian” [CH5.62]. The margin: “Paracelsus would not have Physick be concealed.” [N227].

Interpreted: this does not overturn CH5.28, where a secret is hidden for the danger to the teller; here Croll rejects only the fear that telling destroys its virtue [CH5.28; CH5.62]. Whether the secret of the one place is the Physick of the other the lines do not say.

Physicians should imitate the Hermetick Physitians, “who always use to pray for a blessing upon their endeavours”; to use a creature without a blessing God counts “theft and rapine” [CH5.62]. Pray before, give thanks after; their neglect is “the chief cause of thier ill successe” [CH5.63–65].

## Eight causes why the best medicine fails

First, the term of life. “No Medicine can preserve the corruptible body from death”, yet “there is one thing that hindereth corruption, reneweth youth, and prolongeth the short life, as in the Patriarchs”; even “the universall and chiefest medicine is of no effect” against the appointed end [CH5.68]. Several readings of the unnamed “one thing”: the universal medicine of the same paragraph [CH5.68]; or the Quint-Essence of Gold, “which reneweth the whole body” [CH5.36]. The text names neither.

Second, unskilful physicians have made the disease incurable; those who let a patient perish rather than yield credit men “commonly (and justly) call Honest Hangmen” [CH5.69]. Third, “the Physitian is sent for too late” [CH5.70]. Fourth, the sick breaks “the Golden law of the Locrians” [CH5.71]. Fifth, hard natures and adverse season: “A ripe pear or apple will fall of its own accord” [CH5.72]. Sixth, “what Nature hath once taken away the Physitian can by no means restore” [CH5.73]. Seventh, the patient's meanness and doubt; “There is a twofold Physick; Visible or created, and Invisible even the WORD of GOD” [CH5.74].

## Faith and imagination

Within the seventh cause: Stated, “Naturall Faith (I speak not of the Faith of Grace by Christ) inborn of God the Father in the first Creation, which is our IMAGINATION” can cause and cure diseases [CH5.75]. In plague, fear “breedeth that Basilisk of Heaven”, and “the Iliaster or Evestrum of the Sun” punishes sin; the lines given do not explain these names [CH5.75].

“Faith giveth the Imagination”, and “the Imagination giveth a Sydus or Constellation” [CH5.76]. The Spirit that helps is Stated: “I mean the Spirit of Nature or the Sydus of the medicine” [CH5.77]. A new spring in Misnia and Bohemia cures by “the constant excessive affection of them that use it” [CH5.77]. Avicen is quoted: “Nature obeyeth the cogitations and vehement desires of the Soul” [CH5.78]. “God will doe nothing without Man, as his Organ or Instument” [CH5.80].

Eighth, lest the healed sin more: “every sicknesse is as it were an expiation or propitiatory of sacrifice” [CH5.81].

## Diseases from the wrath of God

Here no bodily medicine helps, “because God hath shut the doors of Nature”; the cure is repentance through “the Celestiall Physitian of our Soules” [CH5.82]. Paracelsus calls them “Deal diseases, which are from the Ens of God”: “the Cure, saith he, is by Faith” [CH5.82]. The margin: “A Physitian beginneth in appeasing Gods wrath.” [N252].
