# The Preface as a Whole

## What the Preface is

The heading addresses the Preface to Prince Christian Anhaltin [OPEN.0], whom the author names again at the end as his patron [CH6.76]. Its first words, though, speak to the reader: “COURTEOUS READER” [OPEN.1]. Its aim is to break an old silence about secrets and to “set before thee two of the most excellent Spagyrik Mysteries” [OPEN.3]. The author says he proved their preparations by his own hand [OPEN.3]. Before he comes to the Remedies, he lists what is “to be explained by a Philosophicall Phisitian” [OPEN.23]: six numbered heads and an epilogue [OPEN.24–30]. Our reading is that the chapters follow this list, since head II asks where true Phisick lies hid, head III says it is fetched out by fire, and Chapter III’s heading speaks of the True Physick got out and prepared by Fire [OPEN.25; OPEN.26; CH3.0].

Its sections, in order:

- The opening, with no chapter number, defends breaking the silence, names the critics the author expects, and lists the heads to come [OPEN.2; OPEN.10; OPEN.23].
- Chapter I, Of the True Physic, says this Physick is God’s gift and rests on the agreement of the great world with man [CH1.1; CH1.4].
- A section on the Microcosm, or Little World, Man treats man’s making, his parts and his inner powers [MICRO.0].
- Chapter II says where the true Physick lies: in the kernel of things, in their Spirits, and last in the Word of God [CH2.1; CH2.7; CH2.13].
- Chapter III says how it is got out of its bark and rind and prepared by Fire [CH3.0].
- Chapter IV says how Physick works on the body and cures [CH4.0].
- Chapter V, “The Duty of Natures Minister, the Physitian.” [CH5.0], says what the physician must be and know.
- Chapter VI treats the one chief Medicine of the most ancient philosophers and closes the Preface [CH6.0; CH6.75].

## The argument in its own order

### The opening: the Admonitory Preface

The author begins with the case against himself. The ancient Philosophers forbade men “to prophane the Secrets” [OPEN.2]; that rule is theirs, and he reports it in order to set himself against it [OPEN.2]. His ground is God, who “communicateth his light, without respect or envy, to the good and bad” [OPEN.2], and so he will no longer hide his Talent “by an impious silence” [OPEN.2]. What he offers he says he tested by fire and by his own hand, unlike writers who wrote from others’ report, for “no man is further to be believed, then as every one findeth by his own proper experience” [OPEN.3; OPEN.4; OPEN.5]. He sets one thing apart, above all he offers: “that universall and most famous Medicine which the most ancient wise Philosophers had from the beginning of the world” [OPEN.6].

He then numbers the opponents he foresees. The Mystical Hermetic Philosophers will accuse him of “breaking open the seale of Chymistry” [OPEN.10]; he answers that better Treasures lie in the true Cabala and will be shown to all in a third Age, when “Elias the Artist” comes [OPEN.10]. The Theophrasteans lose their cheating trade by his publishing [OPEN.11]. The Galenists, mild or dull, and the Scholastic Doctors with their bought titles follow [OPEN.12; OPEN.13; OPEN.14; OPEN.15]. Some old physicians leave “the dead shadows of meer outsides” and become scholars of Chymistry [OPEN.17; OPEN.20]. The section ends with the list of heads [OPEN.23–30].

### Chapter I. Of the True Physic

The true Physick “is the mear gift of the most high God” [CH1.1], learned from God and the Light of Nature, not from the Heathens [CH1.1]. Its foundation “is according to the agreement of the lesser World Man with the greater and externall world” [CH1.4]: the outer world is a glass in which man is seen, and the two are one in essence and inward form [CH1.5]. Everything is fourfold, so there is a fourfold Man and a fourfold Medicine [CH1.6; CH1.12]. Each thing has its own Astrum, “the secret Forger” of its form [CH1.15], and the outer stars neither infuse virtue into things nor necessitate man [CH1.16].

All compound bodies resolve into Three Principles, Mercury, Sulphur and Salt [CH1.19]. The first life of medicines must die so that a second life is reached through Putrefaction and Regeneration [CH1.37]. Diseases come not from the four humours but from the Seed and the Three First [CH1.39; CH1.40]. Hence diseases are cured not by contraries but by the Arcana, the secret things the Chymist draws out of the last matter into the first [CH1.43].

### The Microcosm, or Little World, Man

This section opens on self-knowledge, the lesson printed as “KNOW THY SELFE” [MICRO.1]. The notion of all things is created in us, and the mind is perfected “not by study and paines; but by patience and submission” [MICRO.3]. True philosophy should be grounded on the Scriptures, not on the Heathen [MICRO.8]. Man is “the bond or buckle of the world” [MICRO.11], made of the Quintessence of all created things [MICRO.12], and the centre and circle of the creatures [MICRO.13]. He has three parts, a mortal Body, a Sydereal Spirit and an immortal Soul [MICRO.14], and the body from the Firmament is the soul’s vehicle between the other two [MICRO.17].

The invisible, sydereal man works through Imagination, “the Gate of Wonders” [MICRO.33], which can breed and cure disease [MICRO.33]. A Gabalistical Magick rests on three Pillars: silent prayer, natural faith and a strongly exalted imagination [MICRO.37–40]. The author then breaks off, since these matters “require a larger narration then can be made of them at this time” [MICRO.44]. He ends by naming those who cleared this foundation, Paracelsus first and Severinus next [MICRO.45].

### Chapter II. Where that true Physick is to be found

Lower things have two natures, “a fift Essence and the dreggs”, which fire can separate [CH2.1]; the medicine lies wrapped as a kernel in its rind and must be got out by the Chymists’ art [CH2.1]. It is a specific virtue planted in the Seeds by the Word [CH2.2], and cures come from Spirits, not bodies [CH2.3; CH2.7]. Remedies are drawn from the three shops of Vegetable, Animal and Mineral [CH2.4], with gold first, though its virtue is disclosed to few [CH2.5]. Every disease has its medicine, except disease sent by God’s anger [CH2.8].

The chapter then rises: herbs are “but a signe onely of the Word signified” [CH2.13], and the Word of God alone “is that which healeth all things” [CH2.13]. The Magus heals by Characters and Stones, the true Cabalist by the Word through Mind, Faith and Prayer [CH2.21; CH2.22]. The page records that the medicine is placed in the kernel, the Seeds, the Spirits, the Word and God; our reading is, narrowest, that the medicine in the herbs belongs to the Visible Physick and the medicine hid in God to the Invisible [CH2.14]. The chapter ends by giving honour to God and to the Physitian as his minister [CH2.23–28].

### Chapter III. How the True Physic is got out and prepared by Fire

Nothing in the lower world is so noble that it has no poison, yet where there is venom there is virtue [CH3.1]. The margin names the two: God’s severe Justice is the venom, and his Mercy the Physick [N163]. Physicians are ministers “to seperate, not masters to compose” [CH3.6], and “Physick and Chymistry cannot be separated” [CH3.7]. Bodies are sorted into Animals, Vegetables and Minerals, and the Three First sought in each [CH3.9]. Impurity came into things with the fall, so Nature now gives nothing pure [CH3.11; CH3.12].

The Separatory art parts the kernel and Quint-Essence from the husks, which are enemies to man’s body [CH3.14]. Three advantages follow from medicines so prepared [CH3.16–19], and minerals and stones, rightly prepared, excel vegetables [CH3.21]. All things come from one seed and one Spirit of the world [CH3.28–31]. Witnesses say where the matter lies, and the page gives two readings: in man, or in every thing with man as one case [CH3.34–38]. Galen lacked the art of separation, while Paracelsus would have shown more had he been borne with [CH3.40; CH3.41]. The chapter closes on the judgement that “this wicked generation is not worthy of such medicines” [CH3.42], and a prayer [CH3.46].

### Chapter IV. How Physick works upon the body

The chapter opens with a dispute: should contraries be cured by contraries, as Hippocrates holds, or like by like, as Paracelsus holds [CH4.1]? The author answers that both maxims may be received [CH4.2]; his quarrel is with Galen, who referred cures to bare qualities [CH4.2]. Nature does the cure, and “the Physitian is but the minister or servant” [CH4.3]; the medicine helps the body’s own Balsom “like a coadjutour or privy counsellour” [CH4.3]. The ground is that “Nature is not a quality but a vertue” [CH4.6]. Diseases spring from the Seed or Astra’s, not from the humours [CH4.6], and “Physick is a Spirit (not a body) which the Magician or wise man only can discerne” [CH4.6].

The Salt, Sulphur and Mercury of man are preserved by those of the great world [CH4.7], and diseases are sorted by these three substances [CH4.7–10]. The page gives two readings where ulcers are cured by Salts in one place and by Mercury in the next: the rules disagree, or, wording-dependent, they name one remedy under two words [CH4.9–10]. The chapter ends with two stories from Prague, of a swallowed knife and of swallowed flints [CH4.13; CH4.14].

### Chapter V. The Duty of Nature’s Minister, the Physician

The physician must be “free from every kind of Sect” [CH5.1], yet no sect is to be slighted [CH5.3]. More secrets remain concealed, to be found by wise men in their times [CH5.5], so neither the Ancients nor Theophrastus is wholly to be rejected or embraced [CH5.7]. The physician is first an interpreter of Nature [CH5.8] and second a good Spagirus, one who separates the pure from the impure [CH5.12]. Paracelsus is praised above all [CH5.12; CH5.14] and defended against the charge that his cures were the Devil’s, a charge the author reports to reject [CH5.15]. The Light of Grace and the Light of Nature make one man both theologian and philosopher [CH5.19].

A long turn on Tartar, the excrement of food coagulated in man, explains the first of three respects in which the Paracelsians cure better [CH5.31; CH5.32; CH5.35; CH5.44]. Paracelsus is to be read, but not as an oracle [CH5.51]; he wrote in a magical style for the children of Wisdom [CH5.54]. Physician and surgeon should be one [CH5.56], and the Old and New Physick may be reconciled [CH5.61]. Eight causes are given why even the best medicine fails [CH5.68–81], and diseases from God’s wrath are cured only by repentance [CH5.82].

### Chapter VI. Of that one only chief Medicine

The author calls the chief Medicine “this most secret Secret of secret Secrets” [CH6.1]. It is promised only to the pious philosophers [CH6.2], and seekers must want wisdom, not wealth [CH6.6; CH6.12]. Few reach it, and only by God’s will [CH6.17; CH6.18]. The philosophers write of it in covers and swore never to write it plainly [CH6.21]. The author attacks impostors who promise gold [CH6.22–25] and says he once saw and tasted the Medicine at a great man’s house [CH6.37]. One drop of a liquor saved a dying man in one night [CH6.38].

The Medicine is then named a Fift Essence, “the root of life in Nature” [CH6.51]. It cures by bringing the Three First back to equality [CH6.53; CH6.54], and it can keep a man sound to his natural death [CH6.54]. Paracelsus is cleared of the scoff that he sought bodily immortality [CH6.62]. The true long life is then given a second sense: union with God, the Centre [CH6.63; CH6.64]. The chapter weighs how life is prolonged and shortened [CH6.69–74] and ends the Preface with a prayer to the Triunity [CH6.77].

## Process or not, and what the Preface carries

The division made from the readings finds that the Preface does mark a process. This is its finding, not something the book says of itself. Its basis is the heading of Chapter III and head III of the opening list, which both speak of the true Physick got out and prepared by fire [CH3.0; OPEN.26]. It adds the Separatory art, which names the operation [CH3.14], and the words “The Chymicall way of Subtiliating Extracting, and Separating” [CH3.40], which call it a way. Its end is “that pure and Christiline matter may be administred to our bodies” [CH3.7]. For the chief Medicine, the division cites “the greatest miracle of Nature wrought by Art” [CH6.38] and a named “Moyst way of the Ancients” whose steps the lines do not give [CH6.38]. It also cites the philosophers’ covered joining of Sun and Lunary with the help of Art, words they report under an oath never to write plainly [CH6.21]. Finally it cites a margin note on Gold animated by the Salt of Niture, which it interprets as the chief Medicine’s work (note-only) [N263]. It counts two units.

**The True Physic.** This is Physick of the kind got out and prepared by Fire [CH3.0]. Chapter I treats it as God’s gift, resting on the agreement of the great and little worlds [CH1.1; CH1.4]. Chapter II places it covered as a kernel in rind, in the Seeds and in the Spirits of things [CH2.1; CH2.2; CH2.7], and Chapter III tells how Fire frees it [CH3.7; CH3.14]. “By its onely power all diseases are cured”, the author says, if it be “rightly prepared, and separated from all impurity” [CH1.3]. Chapter IV says it works by helping Nature’s own Balsom, not by contrary qualities [CH4.3; CH4.6]. See [the True Physic in Plain Terms](units/U1.md) and [the True Physic in Plain Language](units/U1_plain.md).

**That one only chief Medicine of the most ancient Philosophers.** This Medicine is wrought by Art [CH6.38]. The opening sets it above everything the author offers [OPEN.6], and the list of heads calls it “commended of many, possessed, yea seen, or believed of very few” [OPEN.29]. Chapter VI treats it in full: it purges impurity from metallic and human bodies and restores health [CH6.1]. It “reduceth to the true Temperament by making equall the unequall” [CH6.54], and it keeps its user sound to his natural death [CH6.54]. The author says he saw a dying man saved “by giving one onely drop of that Latex or liquor” [CH6.38]. Its matter is named as a Fift Essence, one with the spirit of our body [CH6.51]. See [that one only chief Medicine of the most ancient Philosophers in Plain Terms](units/U2.md) and [that one only chief Medicine of the most ancient Philosophers in Plain Language](units/U2_plain.md).

## What is covered, and how

The Preface begins by breaking a rule of silence [OPEN.2], yet it keeps silences of its own and says where. The author breaks off his account of man because it needs a larger narration [MICRO.44]. In Chapter V he says “nor may I in this unfit place openly speak more of these Mysteries” [CH5.24]. Of the chief Medicine he withholds a further use, “to say nothing now of the Magicall and super Celestial use” [CH6.1]. Of what he saw at a great man’s house he gives words he will not translate: “Which I forbear to English.” [CH6.37]. He closes by saying he has imparted “so far as was lawfull, and so much as was allowable by God” [CH6.76]. He adds: “It is provided for by the Philosophical law that some tedious things should be left obscure to young Schollers and for intelligent & wise men to find out” [CH6.76].

Covered speech is named and defended. Chapter I speaks of difficulties “wrapt up in aenigmaticall obscurity” [CH1.12]. Wonderful gifts come in writings “woven of so many Hieroglyphicall, Magicall, and Mathematicall Coverings” [CH6.1]. Of Paracelsus the pages state: “He concealed his mysteries under vulgar and various names” [CH5.55]. The reader is warned not to take the likenesses for the truth itself [CH5.55]. Plato is quoted: “Arts wrapt in Riddles safe doe lye” [CH5.26]. The true philosophers made known their work only to “such who had an understanding allegorically” [CH6.21], and they may give names at their pleasure [CH6.21]. Some figures the text explains itself: the kernel and rind [CH2.1], the Spagirus [CH5.12], the bolted Gate [CH6.19]. Others the pages read only as figures, such as Vulcan for fire [OPEN.3]. The joining of the blood of the Sun and the Dew of the Lunary stays in covered words [CH6.21].

To whom are things given? The opening says the gates of wisdom stand open “to ingenious men” [OPEN.2], and the margin says “Only they that are worthy shall understand these things” [N018]. Mineral virtues are disclosed to enlightened men [CH2.5], Physick is discerned by the wise man only [CH4.6], and the Hermetic philosophers left their secrets to sworn disciples [CH5.28]. The chief Medicine is promised to the pious [CH6.2], and even one who holds the key may be refused, “because the Gate was bolted, that is, the Divine Grace and Benediction was denyed and did resist” [CH6.19]. The book says the secrets also guard themselves, since the slothful and the profane fail to grasp them [OPEN.10].

The pages record strains within the book on this theme. Chapter V praises Paracelsus as one who “knew and publisht” hidden secrets [CH5.12], yet asks whether any man with his knowledge would tell it to every man [CH5.27]; the page records this without judging it. Chapter V says “A Secret indeed is to be concealed, it belongs onely to God to reveale it” [CH5.28]. Later it calls foolish the belief “that a Secret looseth its vertue when tis made known” [CH5.62]. Our reading is, with that page, that the later passage rejects the belief about lost virtue, not the dangers of disclosure [CH5.28; CH5.62]. Chapter II calls it folly to think the Creator would hide remedies from men [CH2.8], beside gifts withheld from most [CH2.5]. The page’s narrowest reading is that God does not hide the remedies, and men fail to know them [N139].

Our reading is that the Preface also speaks of time in two ways. The opening says the Treasures will be shown “to be manifest indifferently to all in due time by the light of Grace and Nature” [OPEN.10]. Chapter V says secrets are ordained for times and nations [CH5.5]. Chapter III says God “will never suffer those Secrets to be known, especially in these corrupt and perilous times” [CH3.42]. No page sets these passages beside each other; the link between them is ours.

## What the Preface leaves open

- Whether the Preface’s gift is to all or to some. It defends giving like the Father who shines on good and bad, and also says only the worthy will understand [OPEN.2; OPEN.10; N018].
- What “mingled together in a fit place” means: Mysteries hidden among other matter so the unworthy pass them by, or simply set in their proper place [OPEN.10].
- What the Light of Nature is: an inward teacher, the likeness of world and man, or a gift from above. The pages give all three as defensible, and Chapter V sets it beside the liberty of human judgement [CH1.1; CH1.5; CH1.18; CH5.1].
- Whether the medicine’s virtue comes from within or is infused from Heaven. Chapter II says it is not from without, then that God infuses it and Heaven gives force [CH2.3; CH2.11; CH2.12]. The page gives two readings: the passages deny different causes, or they stand against each other.
- Where the matter of the philosophers lies: in man, or in every thing [CH3.36; CH3.38; N180].
- Whether the three points of the Magical and Cabalistical Art are Mind, Faith and Prayer, or herbs, stones and words [CH2.22; N154].
- How the rules for ulcers fit, by Salts in one place and by Mercury in the next [CH4.9–10].
- What the “one thing” is that prolongs life in Chapter V: the universal medicine, or the Quint-Essence of Gold [CH5.68; CH5.36].
- What the Gold animated by Salt of Niture is: the road the seekers leave to the greedy, or a covered speech [CH6.11; N263].
- What the capitals in the words the author forbears to English are: hidden letters, or misprints [CH6.37].
- Whether Nothing names two things, the world left behind and the self given up, or one word used of unlike things [CH6.68].
- What the Magical and super Celestial use of the chief Medicine is, which the author leaves unsaid [CH6.1].
