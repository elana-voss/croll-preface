# The Preface as a Whole

## What the Preface is

The heading addresses Prince Christian Anhaltin [OPEN.0], named at the end as the author’s patron [CH6.76]; the first words address the “COURTEOUS READER” [OPEN.1]. The aim is to break an old silence about secrets and to “set before thee two of the most excellent Spagyrik Mysteries” [OPEN.2; OPEN.3]. Before the Remedies, the author lists what is “to be explained by a Philosophicall Phisitian” [OPEN.23]: six numbered heads and an epilogue [OPEN.24–30]. Our reading is that the chapters follow this list: head III, that Phisick is fetched out by fire, matches Chapter III’s heading [OPEN.26; CH3.0].

Its sections, in order:

- The opening, with no chapter number: why the silence is broken, the critics expected, the heads to come [OPEN.2; OPEN.10; OPEN.23].
- Chapter I, Of the True Physick: this Physick is God’s gift and rests on the agreement of the great world with man [CH1.1; CH1.4].
- The Microcosm, or Little World, Man: man’s generation, his parts and his invisible, sydereal side [MICRO.0; MICRO.14; MICRO.31].
- Chapter II: the true Physick lies wrapped like a kernel in rinds, in the Spirits of things, and last in the Word of God [CH2.1; CH2.7; CH2.13].
- Chapter III: how it is got out of its bark and rind and prepared by Fire [CH3.0].
- Chapter IV: how Physick works on the body and cures [CH4.0].
- Chapter V, “The Duty of Natures Minister, the Physitian” [CH5.0]: what manner of physician this Physick requires [OPEN.28].
- Chapter VI: the one chief Medicine of the most ancient philosophers; it closes the Preface [CH6.0; CH6.75].

## The argument in its own order

### The opening: the Admonitory Preface

The author reports the ancient Philosophers’ rule against those who “prophane the Secrets” only to set himself against it [OPEN.2]. His ground is God, who “communicateth his light, without respect or envy, to the good and bad” [OPEN.2], so he will no longer hide his Talent “by an impious silence” [OPEN.2]. What he offers he says he proved by his own hand and trial, unlike writers who wrote from others’ report, for “no man is further to be believed, then as every one findeth by his own proper experience” [OPEN.3; OPEN.4; OPEN.5]. One thing he sets above all he offers: “that universall and most famous Medicine which the most ancient wise Philosophers had from the beginning of the world” [OPEN.6].

He then foresees how each sort will take his publishing. The Mysticall Hermetick Philosophers will accuse him of “breaking open the seale of Chymistry” [OPEN.10]; he answers that better Treasures lie in the true Cabala, to be shown to all when in the third Age “Elias the Artist” comes [OPEN.10]. The spurious Theophrasteans will accuse him, since they lose their cheating trade [OPEN.11]. The milder Galenists will embrace this light, the dull ones scorn it, and the Scholastic Doctors hold titles got by flattery or bribery [OPEN.12; OPEN.13; OPEN.14; OPEN.15]. Some old physicians become scholars of Chymistry, lest they be buried in “the dead shadows of meer outsides” [OPEN.17; OPEN.20]. The list of heads follows [OPEN.23–30].

### Chapter I. Of the True Physic

The true Physick “is the mear gift of the most high God” [CH1.1], to be learned from God, not from the Heathens [CH1.1]. Its foundation “is according to the agreement of the lesser World Man with the greater and externall world” [CH1.4]: the outer world is a glass in which man is seen, the two one in essence and inward form [CH1.5]. Everything is fourfold, so there is a fourfold Man and a fourfold Medicine [CH1.6; CH1.12]. The Astrum is “the secret Forger” of the form of things [CH1.15]; each thing carries its own, and the outer stars neither infuse virtue into things nor necessitate man [CH1.16].

All compound bodies resolve into Three Principles, Mercury, Sulphur and Salt [CH1.19]. The first life of medicines must die so that a second life is reached through Putrefaction and Regeneration [CH1.37]. Diseases come not from the four humours but from the Seed and the Three First [CH1.39; CH1.40], and are cured not by contraries but by the Arcana, which the Chymist draws out of the last matter into the first [CH1.43].

### The Microcosm, or Little World, Man

The section opens on the Divine lesson “KNOW THY SELFE” [MICRO.1]. The notion of all things is created in us, and the mind is perfected “not by study and paines; but by patience and submission” [MICRO.3]. True philosophy should be grounded on the Scriptures, not on the Heathen [MICRO.8]. Man is “the bond or buckle of the world” [MICRO.11], made of the Quintessence of all created things [MICRO.12], the centre and circle of the creatures [MICRO.13]. He has three parts, a mortal Body, a Sydereal Spirit and an immortal Soul [MICRO.14]; the body from the Firmament is the soul’s vehicle, knitting soul and earthy body together [MICRO.17].

The invisible, sydereal man is also called the imagination [MICRO.32], “the Gate of Wonders”, which breeds the Plague and “brings sicknesse and health” [MICRO.33]. The Gabalisticall part of Magick rests on three Pillars: prayer in sacred silence, natural faith and a strongly exalted imagination [MICRO.37–40]. The author breaks off, since these matters “require a larger narration then can be made of them at this time” [MICRO.44], and names those who cleared this foundation, Paracelsus first and Severinus next [MICRO.45].
