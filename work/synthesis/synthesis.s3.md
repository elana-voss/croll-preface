## Process or not, and what the Preface carries

The division made from the readings finds that the Preface does mark a process. This is its finding, not something the book says of itself. Its basis is the heading of Chapter III and head III of the opening list, which both speak of the true Physick got out and prepared by fire [CH3.0; OPEN.26]. It adds the Separatory art, which names the operation [CH3.14], and the words “The Chymicall way of Subtiliating Extracting, and Separating” [CH3.40], which call it a way. Its end is “that pure and Christiline matter may be administred to our bodies” [CH3.7]. For the chief Medicine, the division cites “the greatest miracle of Nature wrought by Art” [CH6.38] and a named “Moyst way of the Ancients” whose steps the lines do not give [CH6.38]. It also cites the philosophers’ covered joining of Sun and Lunary with the help of Art, words they report under an oath never to write plainly [CH6.21]. Finally it cites a margin note on Gold animated by the Salt of Niture, which it interprets as the chief Medicine’s work (note-only) [N263]. It counts two units.

**The True Physic.** This is Physick of the kind got out and prepared by Fire [CH3.0]. Chapter I treats it as God’s gift, resting on the agreement of the great and little worlds [CH1.1; CH1.4]. Chapter II places it covered as a kernel in rind, in the Seeds and in the Spirits of things [CH2.1; CH2.2; CH2.7], and Chapter III tells how Fire frees it [CH3.7; CH3.14]. “By its onely power all diseases are cured”, the author says, if it be “rightly prepared, and separated from all impurity” [CH1.3]. Chapter IV says it works by helping Nature’s own Balsom, not by contrary qualities [CH4.3; CH4.6]. See [the True Physic in Plain Terms](units/U1.md) and [the True Physic in Plain Language](units/U1_plain.md).

**That one only chief Medicine of the most ancient Philosophers.** This Medicine is wrought by Art [CH6.38]. The opening sets it above everything the author offers [OPEN.6], and the list of heads calls it “commended of many, possessed, yea seen, or believed of very few” [OPEN.29]. Chapter VI treats it in full: it purges impurity from metallic and human bodies and restores health [CH6.1]. It “reduceth to the true Temperament by making equall the unequall” [CH6.54], and it keeps its user sound to his natural death [CH6.54]. The author says he saw a dying man saved “by giving one onely drop of that Latex or liquor” [CH6.38]. Its matter is named as a Fift Essence, one with the spirit of our body [CH6.51]. See [that one only chief Medicine of the most ancient Philosophers in Plain Terms](units/U2.md) and [that one only chief Medicine of the most ancient Philosophers in Plain Language](units/U2_plain.md).

## What is covered, and how

The Preface begins by breaking a rule of silence [OPEN.2], yet it keeps silences of its own and says where. The author breaks off his account of man because it needs a larger narration [MICRO.44]. In Chapter V he says “nor may I in this unfit place openly speak more of these Mysteries” [CH5.24]. Of the chief Medicine he withholds a further use, “to say nothing now of the Magicall and super Celestial use” [CH6.1]. Of what he saw at a great man’s house he gives words he will not translate: “Which I forbear to English.” [CH6.37]. He closes by saying he has imparted “so far as was lawfull, and so much as was allowable by God” [CH6.76]. He adds: “It is provided for by the Philosophical law that some tedious things should be left obscure to young Schollers and for intelligent & wise men to find out” [CH6.76].

Covered speech is named and defended. Chapter I speaks of difficulties “wrapt up in aenigmaticall obscurity” [CH1.12]. Wonderful gifts come in writings “woven of so many Hieroglyphicall, Magicall, and Mathematicall Coverings” [CH6.1]. Of Paracelsus the pages state: “He concealed his mysteries under vulgar and various names” [CH5.55]. The reader is warned not to take the likenesses for the truth itself [CH5.55]. Plato is quoted: “Arts wrapt in Riddles safe doe lye” [CH5.26]. The true philosophers made known their work only to “such who had an understanding allegorically” [CH6.21], and they may give names at their pleasure [CH6.21]. Some figures the text explains itself: the kernel and rind [CH2.1], the Spagirus [CH5.12], the bolted Gate [CH6.19]. Others the pages read only as figures, such as Vulcan for fire [OPEN.3]. The joining of the blood of the Sun and the Dew of the Lunary stays in covered words [CH6.21].

To whom are things given? The opening says the gates of wisdom stand open “to ingenious men” [OPEN.2], and the margin says “Only they that are worthy shall understand these things” [N018]. Mineral virtues are disclosed to enlightened men [CH2.5], Physick is discerned by the wise man only [CH4.6], and the Hermetic philosophers left their secrets to sworn disciples [CH5.28]. The chief Medicine is promised to the pious [CH6.2], and even one who holds the key may be refused, “because the Gate was bolted, that is, the Divine Grace and Benediction was denyed and did resist” [CH6.19]. The book says the secrets also guard themselves, since the slothful and the profane fail to grasp them [OPEN.10].

The pages record strains within the book on this theme. Chapter V praises Paracelsus as one who “knew and publisht” hidden secrets [CH5.12], yet asks whether any man with his knowledge would tell it to every man [CH5.27]; the page records this without judging it. Chapter V says “A Secret indeed is to be concealed, it belongs onely to God to reveale it” [CH5.28]. Later it calls foolish the belief “that a Secret looseth its vertue when tis made known” [CH5.62]. Our reading is, with that page, that the later passage rejects the belief about lost virtue, not the dangers of disclosure [CH5.28; CH5.62]. Chapter II calls it folly to think the Creator would hide remedies from men [CH2.8], beside gifts withheld from most [CH2.5]. The page’s narrowest reading is that God does not hide the remedies, and men fail to know them [N139].

Our reading is that the Preface also speaks of time in two ways. The opening says the Treasures will be shown “to be manifest indifferently to all in due time by the light of Grace and Nature” [OPEN.10]. Chapter V says secrets are ordained for times and nations [CH5.5]. Chapter III says God “will never suffer those Secrets to be known, especially in these corrupt and perilous times” [CH3.42]. No page sets these passages beside each other; the link between them is ours.

## What the Preface leaves open

- Whether the Preface’s gift is to all or to some. It defends giving like the Father who shines on good and bad, and also says only the worthy will understand [OPEN.2; OPEN.10; N018].
- What “mingled together in a fit place” means: Mysteries hidden among other matter so the unworthy pass them by, or simply set in their proper place [OPEN.10].
- What the Light of Nature is: an inward teacher, the likeness of world and man, or a gift from above. The pages give all three as defensible, and Chapter V sets it beside the liberty of human judgement [CH1.1; CH1.5; CH1.18; CH5.1].
- Whether the medicine’s virtue comes from within or is infused from Heaven. Chapter II says it is not from without, then that God infuses it and Heaven gives force [CH2.3; CH2.11; CH2.12]. The page gives two readings: the passages deny different causes, or they stand against each other.
- Where the matter of the philosophers lies: in man, or in every thing [CH3.36; CH3.38; N180].
- Whether the three points of the Magical and Cabalistical Art are Mind, Faith and Prayer, or herbs, stones and words [CH2.22; N154].
- How the rules for ulcers fit, by Salts in one place and by Mercury in the next [CH4.9–10].
- What the “one thing” is that prolongs life in Chapter V: the universal medicine, or the Quint-Essence of Gold [CH5.68; CH5.36].
- What the Gold animated by Salt of Niture is: the road the seekers leave to the greedy, or a covered speech [CH6.11; N263].
- What the capitals in the words the author forbears to English are: hidden letters, or misprints [CH6.37].
- Whether Nothing names two things, the world left behind and the self given up, or one word used of unlike things [CH6.68].
- What the Magical and super Celestial use of the chief Medicine is, which the author leaves unsaid [CH6.1].
