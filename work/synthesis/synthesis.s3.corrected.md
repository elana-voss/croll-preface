## Process or not, and what the Preface carries

The division made from the readings finds that the Preface does mark a process. This is its finding, not something the book says of itself. Its basis is the heading of Chapter III and head III of the opening list: Physick got out and prepared by fire [CH3.0; OPEN.26]. It adds the Separatory art [CH3.14] and “The Chymicall way of Subtiliating Extracting, and Separating” [CH3.40]. Its end is “that pure and Christiline matter may be administred to our bodies” [CH3.7]. For the chief Medicine, the division cites “the greatest miracle of Nature wrought by Art” and the “Moyst way of the Ancients” [CH6.38]. It also cites the joining of Sun and Lunary “with the help of Art and Nature”, in a matter the philosophers swore never to write plainly [CH6.21], and a margin note on Gold animated by the Salt of Niture, which it interprets as the chief Medicine’s work (note-only) [N263]. It counts two units.

**The True Physic.** This is Physick of the kind got out and prepared by Fire [CH3.0]. Chapter I treats it as God’s gift, resting on the agreement of the great and little worlds [CH1.1; CH1.4]. Chapter II places it covered as a kernel in rind, in the Seeds and in the Spirits of things [CH2.1; CH2.2; CH2.7], and Chapter III tells how Chymistry frees it, the trial being made by Fire [CH3.7; CH3.14]. “By its onely power all diseases are cured”, the author says, if it be “rightly prepared, and separated from all impurity” [CH1.3]. Chapter IV says it works by helping Nature’s own Balsom, not by contrary qualities [CH4.3; CH4.6]. See [the True Physic in Plain Terms](units/U1.md) and [the True Physic in Plain Language](units/U1_plain.md).

**That one only chief Medicine of the most ancient Philosophers.** The opening sets it above every medicine the author offers [OPEN.6], and the list of heads calls it “commended of many, possessed, yea seen, or believed of very few” [OPEN.29]. Chapter VI says it purges impurity from metallic and human bodies and restores health [CH6.1]. It “reduceth to the true Temperament by making equall the unequall”, and keeps the fore-qualified man who uses it prudently sound to his natural death [CH6.54]. The author says he saw a dying man saved “by giving one onely drop of that Latex or liquor” [CH6.37; CH6.38]. It is named a Fift Essence, “altogether one and the same with the spirit of our body” [CH6.51]. See [that one only chief Medicine of the most ancient Philosophers in Plain Terms](units/U2.md) and [that one only chief Medicine of the most ancient Philosophers in Plain Language](units/U2_plain.md).

## What is covered, and how

The Preface begins by breaking a rule of silence [OPEN.2], yet keeps silences of its own and says where. The author breaks off contemplations that “require a larger narration then can be made of them at this time” [MICRO.44]. In Chapter V he says “nor may I in this unfit place openly speak more of these Mysteries” [CH5.24]. Of the chief Medicine he withholds “the Magicall and super Celestial use” [CH6.1]. Of what he saw at a great man’s house he writes: “Which I forbear to English” [CH6.37]. He has imparted “so far as was lawfull, and so much as was allowable by God”, and “some tedious things should be left obscure to young Schollers and for intelligent & wise men to find out” [CH6.76].

Covered speech is named and defended. Chapter I speaks of difficulties “wrapt up in aenigmaticall obscurity” [CH1.12]. The writings that witness to the chief Medicine are “woven of so many Hieroglyphicall, Magicall, and Mathematicall Coverings” [CH6.1]. Of Paracelsus the author says: “He concealed his mysteries under vulgar and various names”, and warns against taking the similitudes for the truth itself [CH5.55]. A verse runs: “Arts wrapt in Riddles safe doe lye” [CH5.26]. The true philosophers made their knowledge known only to “such who had an understanding allegorically”, and may give names at their pleasure [CH6.21]. Some figures the text explains itself: the kernel and rind [CH2.1], the Spagirus [CH5.12], the bolted Gate [CH6.19]. Others the pages read only as figures, such as Vulcan for fire [OPEN.3]. The joining of the blood of the Sun and the Dew of the Lunary stays in covered words [CH6.21].

To whom are things given? The opening says the Gates of Wisdome “ought alwayes to stand open to ingenious men” [OPEN.2], and the margin says “Only they that are worthy shall understand these things” [N018]. Mineral virtues are disclosed to enlightened men [CH2.5], Physick is discerned by the wise man only [CH4.6], and the Hermetic philosophers left their secrets to sworn disciples [CH5.28]. The chief Medicine is promised to the pious [CH6.2], and even one who holds the key may be refused, “because the Gate was bolted, that is, the Divine Grace and Benediction was denyed and did resist” [CH6.19]. The slothful will never finger these secrets, and there is little danger of the profane grasping them [OPEN.10].

The pages record strains in the book here. Chapter V praises Paracelsus as one who “knew and publisht” hidden secrets [CH5.12], yet asks whether any man with his knowledge would tell it to every man [CH5.27]. It says “A Secret indeed is to be concealed, it belongs onely to God to reveale it” [CH5.28], and later calls foolish the belief “that a Secret looseth its vertue when tis made known” [CH5.62]. Our reading is, with that page, that the later passage rejects the belief about lost virtue, not the dangers of disclosure [CH5.28; CH5.62]. Chapter II calls it folly to think the Creator would hide remedies from men [CH2.8], beside the gift of making Gold potable, which few men have [CH2.5]. The page’s narrowest reading is that God does not hide the remedies, and men fail to know them [N139].

Our reading is that the Preface also speaks of time in two ways. The opening says the Treasures are “to be manifest indifferently to all in due time by the light of Grace and Nature” [OPEN.10]. Chapter V says secrets are ordained for times and nations [CH5.5]. Chapter III says God “will never suffer those Secrets to be known, especially in these corrupt and perilous times” [CH3.42].

## What the Preface leaves open

- Whether the Preface’s gift is to all or to some. It defends giving like the Father who shines on good and bad, and also says only the worthy will understand [OPEN.2; OPEN.10; N018].
- What “mingled together in a fit place” means: Mysteries hidden among other matter so the unworthy pass them by, or simply set in their proper place [OPEN.10].
- What the Light of Nature is: an inward teacher, the likeness of world and man, or a gift from above. The pages give all three as defensible, and Chapter V sets it beside the liberty of human judgement [CH1.1; CH1.5; CH1.18; CH5.1].
- Whether the medicine’s virtue comes from within or is infused from Heaven. Chapter II says it is not from without, then that God infuses it and Heaven gives force [CH2.3; CH2.11; CH2.12]. The page gives two readings: the passages deny different causes, or they stand against each other.
- Where the matter of the philosophers lies: in man, or in every thing [CH3.36; CH3.38; N180].
- Whether the three points of “the Magicall and Cabalisticall Art” are Mind, Faith and Prayer, or herbs, stones and words [CH2.22; N154].
- How the rules for ulcers fit, by Salts in one place and by Mercury in the next [CH4.9–10].
- What the “one thing” is that prolongs life in Chapter V: the universal medicine, or the Quint-Essence of Gold [CH5.68; CH5.36].
- What the Gold animated by Salt of Niture is: the road the seekers leave to the greedy, or a covered speech [CH6.11; N263].
- What the capitals in the words the author forbears to English are: hidden letters, or misprints [CH6.37].
- Whether Nothing names two things, the world left behind and the self given up, or one word used of unlike things [CH6.68].
- What the Magical and super Celestial use of the chief Medicine is, which the author leaves unsaid [CH6.1].
