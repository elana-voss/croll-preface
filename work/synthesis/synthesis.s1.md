# The Preface as a Whole

## What the Preface is

The heading addresses the Preface to Prince Christian Anhaltin [OPEN.0], whom the author names again at the end as his patron [CH6.76]. Its first words, though, speak to the reader: “COURTEOUS READER” [OPEN.1]. Its aim is to break an old silence about secrets and to “set before thee two of the most excellent Spagyrik Mysteries” [OPEN.3]. The author says he proved their preparations by his own hand [OPEN.3]. Before he comes to the Remedies, he lists what is “to be explained by a Philosophicall Phisitian” [OPEN.23]: six numbered heads and an epilogue [OPEN.24–30]. Our reading is that the chapters follow this list, since head II asks where true Phisick lies hid, head III says it is fetched out by fire, and Chapter III’s heading speaks of the True Physick got out and prepared by Fire [OPEN.25; OPEN.26; CH3.0].

Its sections, in order:

- The opening, with no chapter number, defends breaking the silence, names the critics the author expects, and lists the heads to come [OPEN.2; OPEN.10; OPEN.23].
- Chapter I, Of the True Physic, says this Physick is God’s gift and rests on the agreement of the great world with man [CH1.1; CH1.4].
- A section on the Microcosm, or Little World, Man treats man’s making, his parts and his inner powers [MICRO.0].
- Chapter II says where the true Physick lies: in the kernel of things, in their Spirits, and last in the Word of God [CH2.1; CH2.7; CH2.13].
- Chapter III says how it is got out of its bark and rind and prepared by Fire [CH3.0].
- Chapter IV says how Physick works on the body and cures [CH4.0].
- Chapter V, “The Duty of Natures Minister, the Physitian.” [CH5.0], says what the physician must be and know.
- Chapter VI treats the one chief Medicine of the most ancient philosophers and closes the Preface [CH6.0; CH6.75].

## The argument in its own order

### The opening: the Admonitory Preface

The author begins with the case against himself. The ancient Philosophers forbade men “to prophane the Secrets” [OPEN.2]; that rule is theirs, and he reports it in order to set himself against it [OPEN.2]. His ground is God, who “communicateth his light, without respect or envy, to the good and bad” [OPEN.2], and so he will no longer hide his Talent “by an impious silence” [OPEN.2]. What he offers he says he tested by fire and by his own hand, unlike writers who wrote from others’ report, for “no man is further to be believed, then as every one findeth by his own proper experience” [OPEN.3; OPEN.4; OPEN.5]. He sets one thing apart, above all he offers: “that universall and most famous Medicine which the most ancient wise Philosophers had from the beginning of the world” [OPEN.6].

He then numbers the opponents he foresees. The Mystical Hermetic Philosophers will accuse him of “breaking open the seale of Chymistry” [OPEN.10]; he answers that better Treasures lie in the true Cabala and will be shown to all in a third Age, when “Elias the Artist” comes [OPEN.10]. The Theophrasteans lose their cheating trade by his publishing [OPEN.11]. The Galenists, mild or dull, and the Scholastic Doctors with their bought titles follow [OPEN.12; OPEN.13; OPEN.14; OPEN.15]. Some old physicians leave “the dead shadows of meer outsides” and become scholars of Chymistry [OPEN.17; OPEN.20]. The section ends with the list of heads [OPEN.23–30].

### Chapter I. Of the True Physic

The true Physick “is the mear gift of the most high God” [CH1.1], learned from God and the Light of Nature, not from the Heathens [CH1.1]. Its foundation “is according to the agreement of the lesser World Man with the greater and externall world” [CH1.4]: the outer world is a glass in which man is seen, and the two are one in essence and inward form [CH1.5]. Everything is fourfold, so there is a fourfold Man and a fourfold Medicine [CH1.6; CH1.12]. Each thing has its own Astrum, “the secret Forger” of its form [CH1.15], and the outer stars neither infuse virtue into things nor necessitate man [CH1.16].

All compound bodies resolve into Three Principles, Mercury, Sulphur and Salt [CH1.19]. The first life of medicines must die so that a second life is reached through Putrefaction and Regeneration [CH1.37]. Diseases come not from the four humours but from the Seed and the Three First [CH1.39; CH1.40]. Hence diseases are cured not by contraries but by the Arcana, the secret things the Chymist draws out of the last matter into the first [CH1.43].

### The Microcosm, or Little World, Man

This section opens on self-knowledge, the lesson printed as “KNOW THY SELFE” [MICRO.1]. The notion of all things is created in us, and the mind is perfected “not by study and paines; but by patience and submission” [MICRO.3]. True philosophy should be grounded on the Scriptures, not on the Heathen [MICRO.8]. Man is “the bond or buckle of the world” [MICRO.11], made of the Quintessence of all created things [MICRO.12], and the centre and circle of the creatures [MICRO.13]. He has three parts, a mortal Body, a Sydereal Spirit and an immortal Soul [MICRO.14], and the body from the Firmament is the soul’s vehicle between the other two [MICRO.17].

The invisible, sydereal man works through Imagination, “the Gate of Wonders” [MICRO.33], which can breed and cure disease [MICRO.33]. A Gabalistical Magick rests on three Pillars: silent prayer, natural faith and a strongly exalted imagination [MICRO.37–40]. The author then breaks off, since these matters “require a larger narration then can be made of them at this time” [MICRO.44]. He ends by naming those who cleared this foundation, Paracelsus first and Severinus next [MICRO.45].
