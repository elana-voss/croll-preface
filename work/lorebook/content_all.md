## microcosm

Man is the microcosm, the little or lesser world, into whom the whole great world is gathered; the great visible world was made man. He answers to the macrocosm as a child to its parent, and is one and the same with the great world in essence and inner form, though not in outward form. He is called a little world because the whole world preserves, nourishes and heals him. The nature of both worlds is its own medicine, disease and physician; man's inborn heat and moisture are sustained by the heat and moisture of sun and moon, and the whole microcosm is to be brought into a true temper. The ancients wisely gave man this name, and the interpreters of secret nature hold that every particular of the universe is also in him. The little world is also the body, in which strife lurks without end. Some sayings seem to give the name to the outer elements themselves, which mirror the whole man, though they may still mean man, with outer things tied to him as his likeness. The lesser world also seems to serve as a likeness for spiritual virtues and active qualities heaped together by art into one mass.

## macrocosm

The macrocosm is the great world, set over against man, the little world, as parent to child. Its parts answer to man's parts, and man must always answer to its fourfold members. Man's parts are restored by like fruits of the great world, and his salt, sulphur and mercury are preserved by its salt, sulphur and mercury, which agree with his by analogy. Where the philosopher comes to a stand in the natural light of the great world, the physician begins to move, following the agreement between that light and the natural light within man.

## Paracelsus

Paracelsus is an author cited and followed as an authority in physic and philosophy, whose philosophy is called sage and deep. He is owed his due place in practice and in preparations, and only the physician who walks according to his mind is able to open nature's book. He says that like things preserve their like and are destroyed by their contraries, and all that is earthy is called his salt. He seems to be the same man named Theophrastus, defended against envious physicians who are incensed against him and cast a serpent's eye on him, while those who have medicines prepared after his mind have the true ones. Still, neither the physic of the ancients nor his is wholly to be rejected, nor wholly embraced.

## astrum

The astrum is the invisible, insensible body or living spirit in every thing: its inward heaven and seed, the secret forger from which its form and growth proceed. There is no body without an astrum. The seven visible coals of heaven are only the bodies of the astra, and the stars are said to be their rods. The astra of the elements are the seeds in their wombs, invisible, and shown visibly in the highest element. In man the astrum is the sun of the little world, which Paracelsus calls the being of the seed; from it man is generated, and the astrum of man and of heaven is one. The sidereal body, called also the imagination, holds all the astra, which are also named the powers of the angels. In medicine the body of a simple is cast away and its astrum alone taken, to agree with man's inner astrum; the worst diseases spring not from the humours but from invisible astra. Astral diseases come from the firmament in man or from the stars' impressions and have astral remedies, and the astral spirit holds human reason, sense and wisdom. The chief medicine seems to have recovered a dying man by its astral nature, and the astral physician seems to be one who must know the philosophical secret.

## archaeus

The archaeus is the inward worker in man that digests food and separates the pure from the impure. It is called the inborn chemist whom God planted in man, and the stomach is its instrument; the stomach, the liver and the kidneys each have their own. From the last matter of food it raises what nourishes the body, and the archaeus of the stomach must destroy the tartar of the elements, lest it turn into man's own tartar. It is also called the art of nature, and sometimes the best medicine is none at all, leaving the work to the archaeus alone. It is said to be one spirit with the astrum and Vulcanus, without reason, and as diverse as the forms of things are diverse.

## balsam

Man's inward balsam is nature within the body, also called the inner mummy, which keeps the body from all corruption and expels whatever is contrary to it. True medicine seems to rest on the balsam of plants, minerals and animals, where every action in nature begins; the air is called the balsam of all created things, and the spirit the life and balsam of all natural things. Outward things, even venomous ones, seem to hold a balsam agreeable to man's nature that restores the inner one through sympathy; the quintessence is called our bodies' true and friendly balsam. The philosophers of Hermes' school say the greatest medicine quickens our balsam, the spirit, the invisible heavenly vapour of life. Paracelsus calls the tincture the perfect, perpetual balsam of physicians and the defence of old age. The word of God is called a supernatural balsam, comforting mortals more than their own.

## basilisk

The basilisk is a creature the weasel fights, eating rue before the battle. The name is also taken to mean something in the work: a basilisk that, like lightning, suddenly burns up any imperfect metal and at once brings forth a new form. Its eye seems to be a degree to which the work is raised, for the moist way of the ancients is said not yet to have been exalted to the eye of the basilisk.

## celestial stone

The celestial stone is what seekers study to find in the last place, a gift God gives to whom he will. It is also called a beam or ray: by its coming all dross is purified and the darkness of ignorance is driven away. Of those who seek, it seems that there is not one to whom the doors of nature are unlocked.

## centre

The centre is the place of rest and truth, set against the dark labyrinth and circumference of toil and error. God is the centre and circle of all things: all flows from him, and the centre or one is his unchangeable will, against the circumference and plurality of creatures. Man, in imitation of his Creator, is the centre of the creatures, where all things of the universe meet. The word is used also of the one point to which Paracelsus laboured to call back the harmony of the sciences and muses from the whole circle, which seems to mean their unity; of the centre of diseases, seemingly one with their root and seed, which must be plucked up for the cure; and, in a saying of Paracelsus, of the place where the wise lived together keeping wisdom among themselves, taken to be the circle of those who hold it.

## chemistry

Chemistry is the art of fire that separates the good from the bad. It cannot be separated from physic, and its knowledge should be learned from the cradle; it is not the craft mountebanks use to paint faces white and red. The mystical Hermetic philosophers complain that its seal has been broken open and its truth, held captive till now in a prison of shadows and envy, brought out, which seems to mean knowledge kept hidden. It is also pictured as a mount whose top few reach, the top seeming to be the height of the art.

## chemists

Chemists are practitioners who prepare and make up medicines, and they are held to be the most skilful of any, though others cry out that they are no physicians; Paracelsus and other chemists are named as those from whom to learn to prepare and make up medicines. Yet most chemists betake themselves to court and are turned from the truth by its glitter. Those chemists who by divine grace have attained the mind can easily and truly unfold every philosophical difficulty that has been wrapped in riddling obscurity.

## earth

Earth is first the whole frame of the world, which man is to till and manage by wonder, search and knowledge of both the visible and the invisible earth, in fear of God and love of his neighbour, recovering what he has lost. He who has learned to know God and himself seems to be one who has ordered the earth with good husbandry. Earth is also the element from which man's visible, tangible body comes, one of the four in the little world. In a simple, the body or earth is cast away and only its virtue taken. Earth seems also to be a clear, crystalline earth in which what is pure in the elements shall remain like a fixed essence, at rest for ever with the saved.

## elements

The four elements are fire, air, water and earth. Man's matter is their extract, so he must have in himself a familiarity with all of them and their fruits; God made them for their fruits' sake, to sustain man with food and physic, and knowing them shows every disease in man and its cure. The three principles of the first matter were afterwards separated into these four bodies. An element is the invisible life and spirit within its visible body, in which its astra lie: the visible earth is not the element but its body, dead in itself yet the element of a hidden life. Severinus names the true elements, purely spiritual, the keepers, wombs and receptacles of all creation, and the elemented elements as they are to us, fire or the firmament, air, water and earth, which conceive, bring forth and take back all things.

## ens

Paracelsus calls the inner astrum, the sun of the little world, the ens or being of the seed, from which man is generated, formed and governed. Agrippa gives the name to the universal spirit, a being the senses cannot grasp. The first ens toward life rises by the archaeus from the last matter of food and nourishes the body. Paracelsus speaks of the ens of God, the source of diseases whose causes are unsearchable and whose cure must be sought in faith, not in nature. Life is also shortened through the ens by bodily diseases, whose cure is to be sought from natural causes and means; mind and being together cut life short. Paracelsus names a twofold death, from the iliad or first principle and from the ens: the first the physician may hinder, the second not. That ens may be the ens of God, or the bodily being.

## essence

Essence is the healing part of a thing, its physic, lying close to its poison, for nothing in this lower world is so noble that it holds no poison; got out from its husks and impurities, the quickening essence, loosed from its chains, mounts higher and quicker. The one nature is the essence and matter of all things. The physical essence in physic is called gold, and the being of the seed, from which all men after the first proceed, is also named essence. What is pure in the elements seems likened to a pure, clear, incorruptible and fixed essence that shall remain, and the philosophers of Hermes' school say, with caution, that the greatest medicine may be the essence of our life.

## faith

Faith is first faith in God: by it alone eternal things are seen, and faith in God, with love of one's neighbour, is the best foundation of physic. The faith of grace through Christ is distinct from natural faith, an inborn faith or wisdom that God the Father gave equally to all men at the creation. Natural faith is our imagination and is so powerful that it can both cause and cure diseases; its efficacy showed in the woman with the issue of blood and in the centurion, and through God it can do all things naturally. Faith is also a working power: what we do visibly by the body we do by faith in the manner of spirits, and without faith we can do nothing. It gives the imagination, and every creature obeys the faith of men who are innocent and taught in the law of the Lord. Against invisible diseases of the mind no bodily guard avails, only faith, which is the work of him in whom we believe. Last, a firm faith, hope and trust toward the physician and his physic help much toward health.

## fiat

Fiat, “let there be”, is the creating word of God. By it God produced the first matter; the triune God, speaking this triune word, created all things threefold; and by it man was made from the clay or dust of the earth. The word fiat is the seed of the whole world, by which heaven and earth were made, and it works in all creatures; it is the true medicine that heals all things, and by it the spirit of God is made one with nature. Man has two spirits: the first from the limbus, the greater world, the second from the word fiat.

## fifth essence

The fifth essence is the perfect part of a thing below the moon, which fire can separate from the dregs, its imperfect part. As the chief medicine it results from the purer essence of the four elements, purified and incorruptible, and is compared to heaven: God created it as the root of life to keep the four qualities of the human body, as he made heaven to keep the universe. It has the nature and heat of heaven, binds the elements or humours in peaceful harmony, and is the fifth essence of the whole world, the world regenerated; the tincture is the fifth essence of man. The philosophers of Hermes' school say it is compounded of the four elements, all actually present in it. Occult qualities that the soul of the world puts into things to make them fruitful are commonly called fifth essences. God took the quintessence out of all created things and fashioned man of it; man is the quintessence of the greater world, and so may not only imitate heaven but rule it. In medicine the quintessence is the soul dwelling in a remedy, its super-elemental mystery and the true inner balsam of our bodies, which fire parts from the body; the quintessence of gold is said to renew the whole body.

## fire

Fire tests and separates: as all things are proved by fire, so is the knowledge of physic. It parts the false from the true and the quintessence from the body, divides things into their three first things, and reveals their agreement, operation and difference and ever new remedies. Fire is also an element, whose fruits are the stars, the zodiac's signs and the seven rulers of the world. What Paracelsus calls the element of fire is an essential fire of life working within the body, perhaps the greatest secret against all diseases. Life may be prolonged by remedies as a fire by laying on wood. The root of a disease seems to be the fire that must be quenched, not its smoke. Temptation and tribulation are a fire man must pass through to blessedness, and the Holy Spirit is taken to renew by fire to make hidden things manifest.

## firmament

The firmament is the heaven with its fixed stars and planets, whose course is free and governed by none; it is the second natural book, and the school of physic is roofed not with tile but with the firmament. It is also the element of fire, the heavenly element; it is twofold, with an element dwelling in it that holds all astra and seeds, and it sends out the bodies of the stars, sun, moon and planets. Severinus counts fire or the firmament first of the elemented elements. Man has a firmament within him with its own stars and course, not governed by the outer one, and the sidereal man is one with the firmament of the astra. There seems also to be a lower firmament with a sun to which the element of fire is likened, and the word of God appears to be called the firmament of all physic.

## first matter

The first matter is what God first produced by the word fiat: threefold, holding the three principles, a shapeless, confused essence that philosophers call the chaos and hyle, mother of the world, and Paracelsus the limbus. Its earth was the word of the Lord, and it was the seed of all things to be created: God drew every creature out of it, and in respect of it all things were created perfect. The first matter was made a world, and the great world was made man.

## globes

Two globes, the upper and the lower, are explained by astronomy and philosophy; the properties of the bodies in the upper globe are seen in the astra and bodies of the lower. Severinus divides the places of the elements into these two: the upper holds fire or the firmament and air, the lower water and earth, like the yolk of an egg. Beneath the globe of the moon God seems to have created all metallic, mineral and vegetable forms, and there too the more incorruptible substance is found.

## gold

Gold is above all a metal of medicine: rightly refined it would show most divine actions, yet few have God's gift to make solid gold drinkable. It is set down for the heart and thickens the excessive sublimation of mercury, though a man may have much gold and know nothing of its healing virtue. The physical essence in physic is called gold. Gold is also money, which cheers the spirits when the purse is full, but Croll says he did not seek to make gold and silver.

## Hermes

Hermes is the ancient philosopher called most wise and most pious, of reverend antiquity, the foremost of natural philosophers and the first prophet; his pillars stood in Egypt. One adept is said to have laboured happily like Hermes for more than twenty years. Hermes' school seems to be the line of earlier philosophers trained in the secret art, who kept its secret by silence.

## imagination

The imagination is a name of man's sidereal body: it encloses and is all the astra, holds the same course, nature and power as heaven, and is one with the astra of the firmament; being all the astra, it works many operations. It is a working power: it marks the unborn child without touch, draws other men's fancy like a lodestone, infects the stars, breeds the plague, and with true “Gabalia” can produce growing things and metals. It is natural faith, which can both cause and cure diseases. The firmament also has an imagination, but without reason, as man has it with reason, and the whole heaven is called nothing but imagination. Man's reason, used as God wills, is a chain binding the five spirits of the elements, whose imagination is also in him, so that they rest in his imagination. Imagination is also counted among the invisible diseases of the mind.

## light of grace

The light of grace is one of the two lights, within which are all things and without which is nothing; it is named with theology and set beside the light of nature. It begets a true theologian, though not without philosophy, and physic takes its rise from it and ends in the light of nature. By its heavenly help we may, with Christ and all the elect, possess eternal life, and by it the intimate vision and intuitive knowledge of God come to the separated soul even in this world. By it and the light of nature certain treasures are to be made manifest to all in due time.

## light of nature

The light of nature is a divine analogy of the visible world with the body of man. It is the firmament, the great world, which supplies man naturally with all things. Within man it is an inward teacher, named with the inward invisible man and the inner angel, that teaches like a sound doctor; a physician should be born of it and of the light of grace. It is also each man's freedom and power to discern and judge, which no one may take from another and which was never confined to Galen alone. Paired with the light of grace as one of the two lights, it is God's treasury confirmed in scripture and makes a true philosopher, as grace makes a theologian; by it things are shown and men become skilled in chemistry and truly philosophize.

## limus terrae

Limus terrae is the slime, clay or dust of the earth from which man was taken. It is explained as the visible frame of the whole world, heaven and its spheres with the earth: the great world with all its creatures, a hidden secret thing, twofold, visible and invisible. Man himself is called the limus terrae, as made up of all bodies and created things. It also means the earthly mind, after which the friends of God do not live, for they live after the image and inspiration of the Most High.

## lodestone

The lodestone is the drawing stone, used in drawing plasters and taken as a likeness for anything that attracts or works unseen. In man, the sidereal spirit, born of the astra together with him and still united to them, is the lodestone and has a magnetic nature; the drawing body and spirit of man are the lodestone of the microcosm, attracting to them the force of the astra. The earthly lodestone is a spirit in its body and draws to itself, and the imagination of man is like the lodestone in its nature, attracting the fancy of other men. The antipathic lodestone has wonderful virtues and works as if bewitching, spiritually and invisibly.

## magic

True and lawful magic, set apart from witchcraft, is the perfection of all philosophy, the chief wisdom of God's works and full knowledge of hidden nature. It does things beyond nature's usual order by fitly applying agents and patients, observing the consent and dissent of things; far better treasures lie in it, with the true Cabala. Natural magic has kinds: one does invisibly and spiritually, by art, what nature does visibly and bodily; another, called cabalistic, works through the imagination, so that the true magician can draw the working of stars, stones and metals into it. Magic or faith, transplanting minds, has power over all spirits. By mechanical magic the element of earth should swim upon water, which common people hardly believe. Severinus speaks of an incomprehensible magic, seemingly a hidden working, by which what God planted in the elements is ever supplied out of the treasures of divine wisdom.

## magician

The true magician is the wise man. He alone can discern physic, which is a spirit and not a body. He can draw the working of the stars, stones and metals into his imagination, and if he is a true magician he is not below the angels in any magical operation.

## magistery

Magistery means mastership, and it seems also to name the philosophers' work itself: Lully searched into it, and it is tied to the matter of the elixir. Lully speaks of God opening to the artist what is hidden in the magistery of this art, which seems to mean the work of the art. Nature too is said to make itself merry with its own magistery or mastership, rejoicing in its master, its art and its virtues. Magisteries seem also to be a kind of high prepared medicine, counted among the arcana beside fifth essences and named together with elixirs.

## matrix

A matrix is a womb: the place where a seed or astrum dies and rots and then brings forth its fruit, the astrum going forward into a new body. The four astra of the elements are the seeds in four matrices or wombs, and the astrum of each element bears its fruits in a strange region or matrix, a generating matrix, as the fish in the water. In a larger sense the matrix is the mother of a whole: water is the matrix of the world, on which the Spirit of God moved, and the great world was the matrix of Adam, the whole frame of the world being the mother of all things brought forth. Severinus says that the three first are found in every matrix, which seems to mean every place of birth.

## matter

Matter is spoken of in two states. In respect of the first matter all things were created perfect; the last matter, seemingly things as they now stand, is what the chemist perfects by fire, drawing the secret things or astra out of it into the first. It seems that in digestion, likewise, the archeus raises the first being of the body's nourishment out of the last matter of food. The one nature is the essence and matter of all things. Gross bodily matter drowns and hinders the power of a medicine, for secrets work without matter and body, and separation yields a pure, crystalline matter fit for our bodies. The chief medicine is made of the most simple and incorruptible matter under heaven, generated by God out of the spirit of this world, that is, the soul or spirit of the world. Lesser uses: the philosophers say the matter of their mystery may be had everywhere, being in every elementated thing; Morienes seems to tell King Calid, who asked after the matter of the elixir, that it is of the king himself; Lully says he got his matter out of a mean and worthless thing; the matter of the stone is said to be in everything in respect of the vegetable spirit; and Paracelsus calls the matter of diseases tartar.

## medicine

Medicine is a remedy given for disease, rightly prepared by one's own labour and used in the fear of God; sometimes the best medicine is to use none at all. Above every other stands the one chief medicine of the most ancient philosophers, universal and famous, which the wise had from the beginning of the world. God created it for the preservation of life. It is made of the most incorruptible and effective matter under heaven, the soul or spirit of the world, and is in itself the matter of our creation.

## mercury

Mercury is one of the three principles of every compound body, with sulphur and salt, and from these three all physic proceeds. It is the liquor or moisture: like the elixir it gives the virtues, and by a steady supply of vital and vegetative moisture it cherishes the other two, which by constant action grow dry and old. It is also the moisture of what nourishes, and all ulcers are healed by incarnating mercury. Mercury is also quicksilver, to which the watery, fleeing moisture of green wood is likened. Mercury may be exalted above its natural degree in three ways and so seems to cause diseases; distilled by the heat of digestion, it is said to cause all sorts of apoplexies.

## metals

Metals are fruit of the elements, like living things and plants, created not for man's food but for his physic. Some, out of simplicity, hold that metals are of no use in physic, and that view is answered. Above all in the perfect metals God has written in lower things with his own sacred finger; not subject to corruption themselves, their spirits free man's body from corruption as plants, which are corruptible, cannot. Precious stones take their colour, form and tincture from metals by the forming of the stars, for they are metals transplanted. Metals as riches seem to be left to those with an impious hunger to be rich, and the philosophers trained in the school of Hermes name the metamorphosis of metals beside the curing of diseases.

## mind

The mind is the part of man that comes from God and unites him with God: the spirit God breathed into man, God himself dwelling in us, and the steersman of the soul. When the soul turns back within itself into the mind, it draws near to God, sees all things and needs no outward teaching; the man who lives in nothing but the mind is as an angel. Embracing the mind, the image of divinity united to God by Christ, brings actual blessedness. At the last all minds, by a unanimous friendship and in a way past utterance, shall be one thing in one mind above every mind; this may be God himself, the passage going on to speak of the intimate vision of God, or simply the oneness of all minds. Against it stands the earthly mind, after which the friends of God do not live.

## mummy

Mummy is the preserving balsam of life. The true medicine, as natural mummy and kernel of nature, is held in the vital sulphur. Man has an innate mummy, named with his inward nature or balsam, the vital body and liquor of life, and with the vital spirit that has its seat in the midst of man's heart; by it nature heals its own infirmities, as balsamic mummy it keeps the body from corruption, and its destruction is ultimate death. The plague infects the mummy and sulphur of man. The Word of God is a supercelestial mummy, comforting mortals more than their own. Mummy seems also to stop bleeding in man because of like anatomy, and salt and mummy are named together as something that can be spent, after which, it seems, things breed nothing but worms.

## mystery

A mystery is a hidden thing of God or of nature: true philosophers come to understand and even imitate the greatest mysteries of nature, while impostors are unworthy of them, and divine mysteries are greater than words can set forth. In chemistry a mystery is the healing part of a thing, which the art separates into its own receptacle; the super-elemental mystery is the quintessence. The word seems also to cover the soul's turning back within itself into the mind, by which it draws near to God, a mystery the academic spirit cannot understand and which is not to be spoken of openly. The philosophers say the matter of their mystery, seemingly their work, may be had everywhere, being in every elementated thing.

## natural death

Natural death, spoken of philosophically, is only the wasting of the natural moisture and heat, as in a burning lamp: after old age the vital spirit is fully extinguished, consumed and scattered. Diseases can cause it. It differs from the fatal or ultimate death, the utmost term God has fixed for each, which belongs to theology. Yet natural death is also equated with the term of life God appointed to man for disobedience, and up to that term the medicine keeps a man sound.

## natural heat

Natural heat is the heat in man that digests nourishment. It is the heat of the sun and moon in us, the microcosmic heat by which everything is digested for sustenance and multiplication. The celestial spirit, which is one with our natural spirit, is our natural heat whenever its breathing in the body is not lessened or hindered by outward things. When this heat or fire grows weak, it can be strengthened, inflamed and increased.

## nature

Nature is the medium that joins the lowest things to the highest by harmonious consent, called animal, vegetable or mineral after its subject. It is the order and series of God's works, borrowing all its virtue from him; the one nature is the essence and matter of all things and the specific of every creature, and it is the invisible fire by which things multiply. Within the body nature is the genuine physician of all diseases, the inward mummy or balsam, a virtue and not a quality, and the physician is her servant. As she is now, she gives nothing pure but mixes everything with impurities, to move men to learn chemistry. She is a book and a sanctuary whose light teaches like a sound doctor; art and nature consent like mother and daughter, art supplying her defects. Heraclitus called nature the daughter of war. Every nature best preserves itself.

## nothing

Man's own nothing is what he falls into when he turns from God to himself or to creatures, claiming them and himself as his own; it is joined with his own evil, to his ruin. Whatever is not God is nothing and ought to be esteemed so. The most beautiful nothing is the shadow and region of darkness and death, which must be cast behind on the return to the Being of beings. Yet the friends of God, from divine love, cast themselves headlong into the fountain of the abyss and the sea of nothingness; this seems to be a nothingness sought rather than fled.

## philosophy

True philosophy is the mind's right knowledge of itself. It must be grounded on Scripture and on Christ the cornerstone and so return into God; at its end it knows God, the maker of all things, and passes into him with a full image of his likeness. Without it no one can be wholly godly, and true philosophy is theological. In a narrower sense philosophy teaches the virtues of earth and water, as astronomy does of fire and air, and the two together make a perfect philosopher. It is also a practical physic that keeps remedies ready for the physician, the mother of physicians. The philosophy of the heathen is polluted and corrupt, the spring of all error, and must not rule Christians.

## physic

Physic is the art and profession of healing, and above that the healing power God set in things: nothing else than the created and incarnate mercy of the heavenly Father, a favour given by God, placed in nature beside his justice, which is the disease and venom. This healing power is wrapped in rinds, barks, husks and coverings and must be separated from the impure elements by the chemists' art; it is a spirit and not a body, which only the wise man can discern. The work of curing is the refreshing of strength and balsam and the removing of the impurities that cause diseases. True physic is the gift of the most high God alone, not to be learned from the heathen; covered in bark and rind, it must be got out and rightly prepared by fire, and it is what was sought with exceeding love. True physic seems also to be a branch of knowledge, named beside astronomy and chemistry, without which the truly philosophical preparations of the medicines of Theophrastus cannot be understood.

## poison

Poison is what harms and kills, as in the draught by which the malicious adversaries of Paracelsus brought him to a violent death. Nothing in this lower world is so noble that it has no poison in it, set close beside its healing essence or physic; this poison, a tartarous, mucilaginous impurity, must be separated from healing medicines and balsams. Roger Bacon says that where God in mercy placed plenty of physic, there by his justice he set poison to guard it. Poison can overcome poison: nature destroys one by another, as opium, with a greater degree of poison, overcomes a pestilence, and every poisonous creature holds an antidote to its own. Paracelsus gives the name of poison and tartar to the excrements of meat and drink that for the most part breed all diseases.

## regeneration

In the chemist's work the first life of herbs and medicines must die so that a second life is reached through putrefaction and regeneration; without regeneration no hidden secret of physic is attained. The chief medicine seems to be a whole new regenerate world, which by its regeneration can renew man, the little old corruptible world. In man, regeneration is the means by which Christians have their seed from God, which the heathen lack: it overcomes a bad birth, is a celestial transplantation, and gives true philosophy and all sciences. Hermes and others of clean hearts knew it before the Word was incarnate, though they concealed it. At the end of this world a regeneration of soul, spirit and body will, it seems, sparkle out through the body, and its time is said to draw near.

## resolution

Resolution is the dissolving of a body. An artificial or spagyric resolution brings to light the properties that lay before in its composition, and by a natural and due resolution the power of gold would be brought into act so that it could exercise its virtues. In the body, resolution is one of the four ways in which salt destroys health; the salt of the urine is forced into resolution by the violence of separation; and what strengthens natural heat is turned into a simple spirit by resolution and joining with the nourishment. Paracelsus says that death from the resolution of the “Iliad” may be hindered by the industry of the physician, while death from the Ens or Being cannot.

## salt

Salt is one of the three principles of every natural body, beside mercury and sulphur. By coagulation it gives solidity, colour and taste to all bodies; when green wood burns it remains in the ashes, unctuous, slight and lasting. Separating the substances of opium shows a bitter, hot salt in it that penetrates and causes sweat, and the body's surplus of salt is separated by the urine. In one writer's wider use, salt is whatever will melt, can be resolved into a watery moisture and is then dried and hardened by fire; all earthiness is the salt of Paracelsus. Salt is also named with mummy as a thing that can be spent.

## secret

A secret is a hidden thing that lies in God and in nature, of which God is the first inventor; the man united to God has the most absolute knowledge of all secrets and is their keeper. The ancient philosophers strictly forbade such secrets to be profaned, and they have always been hidden from the common sort of those who profess philosophy, though they are now being brought to light and made common. A secret seems also to be a hidden medicine or its working power: secrets stand beside remedies, work without matter and body because diseases are not bodies, and only their knowledge and use is wanting. Highest of all is the one universal physic, the most secret of secrets, by which all things may be done: the greatest earthly treasure, which evil men would abuse to the ruin of the world.

## secrets of nature

The secrets of nature are the hidden things of nature, which true philosophers and skilled physicians search out, come to understand and at last really imitate. To find them out seems to be the chief thing in temporal matters, and the end of physic is not to scrape up money but to discover them. Cheats and impostors are called ignorant and unworthy of them, as of the mysteries of God.

## seed

A tree's seed is both its beginning and its end. The seed of grain is its astrum, an invisible body that, cast into the earth, brings forth a visible one, and each fruit is again astrum and seed. The earth, though it seems dead, holds the seeds of all things. The first, pure seeds of things carry the roots of sicknesses. The first matter was the seed of all things, the great world the seed of man, and the word Fiat the seed of the whole world; man is the last and perfect seed, able to beget another man, though not a seed like others. Severinus holds that seeds lay hid in the elements from creation. Interpreters of secret nature say all seeds lie hid in man. Truth seems to be a seed planted from God by regeneration, among the Gentiles choked for want of the sun.

## separation

Separation is the parting of purity from impurity, or truth from falsehood; it is what a spagyric resolution means. Severinus speaks of separating the strong from the weak and the pure from the impure in certain bodies. In the separation of all things, the spiritual elements seem to have set the visible bodies apart from themselves, as the soul parts its body from itself and still dwells in it. Each bodily member also seems to have its own separation, beside its own digestion, emunctory and excrement.

## sidereal body

The sidereal body is man's second, invisible body. Adam had two bodies, a visible elemental one from the earth and nourished by it, and an invisible, insensible sidereal one from the astra of the firmament, which lives by the firmament. The sidereal body is the house of the spirit's life, and it is this body that is capable of attaining philosophy. Not man alone has one: everything, sensible or insensible, has a sidereal spirit or sidereal body in it.

## sidereal spirit

The sidereal spirit is the invisible man: the life that dwells and works in the body and is properly the man, not flesh and blood. It comes from the firmament and is fed from air and fire; it is mortal, distinct from the immortal soul breathed in by God, and as it is the life of the body, so the Spirit of the Lord is the life of the soul. It draws all arts and human wisdom from the stars, carries the body wherever it imagines, and stirs each man's dexterity in forging his fortune. In a star it is the invisible astrum, the star properly, and everything, sensible or insensible, has a sidereal spirit or body in it. In proud men it seems to rule the old man. All natural things may be learned from the sidereal spirit of the firmament, perhaps man's own or the firmament's.

## spagyric

A spagyric is one who can separate the pure from the impure and discern true from false, a son of art exercised in chemical work; a physician must be a good spagyric, restoring his patient to health by a wise alchemical preparation. The word seems also to describe that art of preparing medicine and those who practise it: the spagyric profession, spagyric physicians, and a spagyric state whose sincere citizens may join together in union. Spagyric physic seems to mean physic duly prepared, which is to be preferred above the mixed compounds of the shops, and spagyric is glossed as divinely distinguishing.

## specific

A specific virtue is a thing's own inward power, set against its outward qualities, by which it cures. Things cure not by external, superficial qualities but by an internal, proper, specific and harmonious virtue, which comforts the heart and serves to cure; opium overcomes poison by its specific venomous virtue, having a greater degree of poison. As a noun, the one nature is the specific of every creature, which seems to mean that which gives each creature its own kind.

## spirit of life

The spirit of life is the life-spirit of the body: a single spirit united throughout the whole body of man, yet varied according to the part in which it dwells. The strengthening power of human and animal bodies proceeds from it; it moves and acts the native or radical humour, and it is nourished in turn by the humours of life. It is also called the spiracle, the breathing hole. The fifth essence is said to be truly the spirit of life, piercing through all other spirits and altogether one and the same with the spirit of our body.

## spirit of the world

The spirit of the world is the invisible and insensible body of the astra, also called Nature. The invisible man and the natural spirit of our body are one and the same with it, as whiteness is one with snow: the heavenly spirit and the spirit of the body are a single spirit. It is also named the soul of the world, the universal spirit that lives in and quickens the whole mass, works all in all and fills the whole world, a united life that gathers and knits all things together, whose magnetic spark lives and works effectually. Avicenna, building on Plato, the Arabians and the Chaldeans, calls it the soul of the world poured into all things. This same spirit is the matter of the chief medicine, the most incorruptible and effective matter there can be under heaven, holding the force of all heavenly and earthly things. By it imperfect and impure metals are cleansed of their infirmities as the bodies of men are, and of it the great God generated the most simple matter for restoring human nature.

## spirit

Spirit is the sidereal spirit in all things, called heaven or the astrum, an invisible, insensible living body, without reason in things that grow and with reason in man. Man has two spirits: a sidereal one from the firmament, and the intellectual soul inspired from God by the breath of life. A universal spirit lives in, quickens and fills the whole world. Spirit is also the life and balsam of natural things, a supernal fire got by art from things in which the virtue of sun and moon is most potent. Some Theophrastians add spirit to salt, sulphur and mercury as a fourth principle answering to air, got from minerals and vegetables but not from animals. Severinus speaks of the Spirit of God moving upon the waters at the creation. Four elementary spirits seem to belong to the elements; with a fifth kind they seem to make five, whose imagination is in man.

## sulphur

Sulphur, or oil, is one of the three principles of every natural body, beside mercury and salt. By a pleasant mixture it tempers the coagulation of the salt and gives the body substance and transmutation. Opium holds a sweet narcotic sulphur, and the body's surplus of sulphur is purged through the entrails. A vital sulphur seems to be meant as a life-bearing sulphur in bodies: the true medicine, as natural mummy and kernel of nature, is contained in it as in the treasure of nature, and the plague infects the mummy and sulphur of man, the microcosm.

## sun

The sun is the heavenly Father, who richly bestows his gifts on us all, shining on all alike. It is also the sun of the sky, whose beams give the heat that brings forth all kinds of flowers and herbs, and which melts snow though it cannot be touched. Properly, the sun is not its visible body but the spirit in that body. The lower world seems to have its own sun as well: the inferior astronomy has its own heaven, sun and moon, and the earthly firmament has its sun, a thing below answering to the sun above. Theophrastus seems to be the rising sun whom the ambitious physicians should have endured without gazing on his clouds.

## taciturnity

Taciturnity is constant silence. By it the philosophers kept the secret of the art closed, remaining altogether dumb about it, and the gifts of the art are to be used in the same constant silence, without pride, since pride provokes the envy of all men. Silence about the secret is thus both the way the art has been guarded and the manner of living with its gifts.

## tartar

Tartar is the excrement of food and drink, coagulated in man by the spirit of salt. Whatever we eat and drink holds a mucilaginous, clayish and sandy tartar harmful to health; the archeus of the stomach must destroy this tartar of the elements, or it turns into the tartar of man. As the natural surplus, the mucilage of salt, tartar is the mother of almost all diseases of all coagulate bodies. Paracelsus divides it into a foreign kind, coming from food and drink, and an innate kind, coming from the hereditary blood.

## three principles

The three principles are mercury, sulphur and salt, also called the three first. They are found in every compound body, and no natural body can be parted by any dissolving skill into more or fewer than these three: liquor, oil and salt. The physical body is said to be nothing but sulphur, salt and mercury. When the first life of herbs and medicines dies and the chemist's art brings them to a second, the three seem to show themselves with their hidden virtues, stripped naked and visible in a clear mercurial, an oily sulphurous and a bright saline form. Severinus seems to hold that all compound bodies come from them and are resolved into them again, and that they are found in every matrix. Natural actions proceed from spirits in which the mechanic sciences of the three have their vigour. Health is their agreement and union in a body, and they are the cause of all diseases; diseases spring from their invisible mechanic spirits and happen by their exaltation, and the best physicians judge their broken natural proportion to be the true origin of bodily disease. Lesser uses: the three first of vinegar, which Galen could not separate, glossed as qualities; and whey, butter and cheese as the three first of milk, all earthiness being salt.

## tincture

Tincture seems to be named with spirit as the source of action: natural actions, and the actions of cures, proceed from spirits and their vital tinctures, not from bodies or their dead qualities. Precious stones take colour, form and tincture from metals by the forming of the stars, as rubies and garnets have the tincture of gold. Tincture is called the fifth essence of the microcosm. By God's curse after the fall new tinctures came in, which are infinite evils. Severinus seems to speak of bodies whose tinctures hold agreeing qualities though no actions proceed from them, and of a radical tincture that with the balsam preserves the elements. Alphidius seems to set the true tincture against the mere colour that giddy seekers find instead, and Paracelsus is taken to say that the tincture so cleanses the balsam that its effect is seen to the tenth generation.

## transmutation

Transmutation is the change of a body or substance into another state. A vehement imagination can transmute not only one's own body but sometimes another's. The salt in man may be changed into all sorts of salts, and as the transmutation is, so is the disease. Things of natural composition are subject to corruption and transmutation. Sulphur, by its pleasant mixture with salt, gives a body substance and transmutation, which seems to mean its capacity to change.

## universal medicine

The universal medicine heals all diseases that proceed from the corruption of the humours, however great and desperate their cure, and powerfully expels the acute diseases that proceed from the impressions of the stars. Many holy men used it before the flood. It has no effect when a person has reached the utmost term appointed by the supreme Being, or when a disease is inflicted by God beyond nature.

## virtue

Virtue is the power in a thing to heal or harm, set apart from its quality, such as hot or cold. Nature itself is a virtue, not a quality, and without virtue a quality is dead and wholly accidental. Physic works on man's body and cures disease by virtue: things hold hidden and restrained virtues, some serve by a specific and harmonious virtue to cure and comfort the heart, opium acts by its specific venomous virtue, and the innate virtue of things that grow from the earth is caused by heavenly influence. Virtue is also moral goodness, which is never found without the marks and wounds of envy.

## Vulcan

Vulcan seems to stand for fire as the means of trial: things are searched and examined by the help of Vulcan before they are commended, for the proper trial of fire discerns and separates the false from the true. The poets praise Vulcan as the truest inventor and teacher of the mysteries of the arts. His name is set on an alchemy that serves the commonwealth and on the spagyric and Hermetic school, and seems there to mean the art of fire.

## the Word

The Word is first God's creating word, Fiat, “Let there be”, by which God produced the first matter, created heaven and earth, and brought all things out of darkness into light, the Word speaking and the Spirit cherishing. The Word of God is also the heavenly medicine that alone heals all things. In it lies hidden all force beyond natural actions, and by it all medicines become powerful; herbs are not the medicines but only a sign of the Word signified. The Word is the uncreated mercy of God; incarnate, it may be found in the creatures, yet is not known to every one; physicians have wrought great cures by the created Word, the incarnate mercy, and the true Cabalist performs his work in the Word by mind, faith and prayer. Words spoken, written, carved or hung about the neck also work cures: medicines are visible bodies, words invisible ones.

## adept

An adept is one who has attained it: a complete philosopher and keeper of God's secrets, whom it is useless to tempt with promises of reward. Only adept physicians know the mental diseases, which are healed without the help of natural physic. Paracelsus explains adept philosophy as long life, free from all infirmity until natural death.

## anatomy

Anatomy seems to mean the inward make of the great world and of man, which agree: the outward world is a speculative anatomy in which, as in a glass, the lesser world man may be seen, and whoever can anatomize it gains true knowledge of man, its child. A sound physician's knowledge proceeds from the anatomy of both worlds; this essentiated and elemented anatomy, which reveals disease and cure, is set above the local anatomy of dead bodies. Things of the same anatomy seem to answer each other, so that malady and medicine share one anatomy and like cures like, as mummy stops bleeding in man. Anatomy also seems to name the chemists' art of taking bodies apart, separating the pure medicine from the impure body. The anatomy of death is said to dwell in the whole state of our life.

## artist

The artist is the skilled worker in chemical employments, a master of his craft whom the preparations require, not a perverse or foolish one; yet no skill of artist or nature can make the wedlock of life and death perpetual. Lullius holds that the artist must agree with God, the first cause, without contrariety, and that God should move the artist's soul to true understanding. God himself is the perfect artist of all things, the most high Creator. Elias, who is to come and restore all things, is called the artist, which seems to mark him as the restorer.
