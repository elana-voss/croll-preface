## microcosm

Man is the microcosm, the little or lesser world, into whom the whole great world is gathered; the great visible world was made man. He answers to the macrocosm as a child to its parent, and is one and the same with the great world in essence and inner form, though not in outward form. He is called a little world because the whole world preserves, nourishes and heals him. The nature of both worlds is its own medicine, disease and physician; man's inborn heat and moisture are sustained by the heat and moisture of sun and moon, and the whole microcosm is to be brought into a true temper. The ancients wisely gave man this name, and the interpreters of secret nature hold that every particular of the universe is also in him. The little world is also the body, in which strife lurks without end. Some sayings seem to give the name to the outer elements themselves, which mirror the whole man, though they may still mean man, with outer things tied to him as his likeness. The lesser world also seems to serve as a likeness for spiritual virtues and active qualities heaped together by art into one mass.

## macrocosm

The macrocosm is the great world, set over against man, the little world, as parent to child. Its parts answer to man's parts, and man must always answer to its fourfold members. Man's parts are restored by like fruits of the great world, and his salt, sulphur and mercury are preserved by its salt, sulphur and mercury, which agree with his by analogy. Where the philosopher comes to a stand in the natural light of the great world, the physician begins to move, following the agreement between that light and the natural light within man.

## Paracelsus

Paracelsus is an author cited and followed as an authority in physic and philosophy, whose philosophy is called sage and deep. He is owed his due place in practice and in preparations, and only the physician who walks according to his mind is able to open nature's book. He says that like things preserve their like and are destroyed by their contraries, and all that is earthy is called his salt. He seems to be the same man named Theophrastus, defended against envious physicians who are incensed against him and cast a serpent's eye on him, while those who have medicines prepared after his mind have the true ones. Still, neither the physic of the ancients nor his is wholly to be rejected, nor wholly embraced.

## astrum

The astrum is the invisible, insensible body or living spirit in every thing: its inward heaven and seed, the secret forger from which its form and growth proceed. There is no body without an astrum. The seven visible coals of heaven are only the bodies of the astra, and the stars are said to be their rods. The astra of the elements are the seeds in their wombs, invisible, and shown visibly in the highest element. In man the astrum is the sun of the little world, which Paracelsus calls the being of the seed; from it man is generated, and the astrum of man and of heaven is one. The sidereal body, called also the imagination, holds all the astra, which are also named the powers of the angels. In medicine the body of a simple is cast away and its astrum alone taken, to agree with man's inner astrum; the worst diseases spring not from the humours but from invisible astra. Astral diseases come from the firmament in man or from the stars' impressions and have astral remedies, and the astral spirit holds human reason, sense and wisdom. The chief medicine seems to have recovered a dying man by its astral nature, and the astral physician seems to be one who must know the philosophical secret.

## archaeus

The archaeus is the inward worker in man that digests food and separates the pure from the impure. It is called the inborn chemist whom God planted in man, and the stomach is its instrument; the stomach, the liver and the kidneys each have their own. From the last matter of food it raises what nourishes the body, and the archaeus of the stomach must destroy the tartar of the elements, lest it turn into man's own tartar. It is also called the art of nature, and sometimes the best medicine is none at all, leaving the work to the archaeus alone. It is said to be one spirit with the astrum and Vulcanus, without reason, and as diverse as the forms of things are diverse.

## balsam

Man's inward balsam is nature within the body, also called the inner mummy, which keeps the body from all corruption and expels whatever is contrary to it. True medicine seems to rest on the balsam of plants, minerals and animals, where every action in nature begins; the air is called the balsam of all created things, and the spirit the life and balsam of all natural things. Outward things, even venomous ones, seem to hold a balsam agreeable to man's nature that restores the inner one through sympathy; the quintessence is called our bodies' true and friendly balsam. The philosophers of Hermes' school say the greatest medicine quickens our balsam, the spirit, the invisible heavenly vapour of life. Paracelsus calls the tincture the perfect, perpetual balsam of physicians and the defence of old age. The word of God is called a supernatural balsam, comforting mortals more than their own.

## basilisk

The basilisk is a creature the weasel fights, eating rue before the battle. The name is also taken to mean something in the work: a basilisk that, like lightning, suddenly burns up any imperfect metal and at once brings forth a new form. Its eye seems to be a degree to which the work is raised, for the moist way of the ancients is said not yet to have been exalted to the eye of the basilisk.

## celestial stone

The celestial stone is what seekers study to find in the last place, a gift God gives to whom he will. It is also called a beam or ray: by its coming all dross is purified and the darkness of ignorance is driven away. Of those who seek, it seems that there is not one to whom the doors of nature are unlocked.

## centre

The centre is the place of rest and truth, set against the dark labyrinth and circumference of toil and error. God is the centre and circle of all things: all flows from him, and the centre or one is his unchangeable will, against the circumference and plurality of creatures. Man, in imitation of his Creator, is the centre of the creatures, where all things of the universe meet. The word is used also of the one point to which Paracelsus laboured to call back the harmony of the sciences and muses from the whole circle, which seems to mean their unity; of the centre of diseases, seemingly one with their root and seed, which must be plucked up for the cure; and, in a saying of Paracelsus, of the place where the wise lived together keeping wisdom among themselves, taken to be the circle of those who hold it.

## chemistry

Chemistry is the art of fire that separates the good from the bad. It cannot be separated from physic, and its knowledge should be learned from the cradle; it is not the craft mountebanks use to paint faces white and red. The mystical Hermetic philosophers complain that its seal has been broken open and its truth, held captive till now in a prison of shadows and envy, brought out, which seems to mean knowledge kept hidden. It is also pictured as a mount whose top few reach, the top seeming to be the height of the art.

## chemists

Chemists are practitioners who prepare and make up medicines, and they are held to be the most skilful of any, though others cry out that they are no physicians; Paracelsus and other chemists are named as those from whom to learn to prepare and make up medicines. Yet most chemists betake themselves to court and are turned from the truth by its glitter. Those chemists who by divine grace have attained the mind can easily and truly unfold every philosophical difficulty that has been wrapped in riddling obscurity.

## earth

Earth is first the whole frame of the world, which man is to till and manage by wonder, search and knowledge of both the visible and the invisible earth, in fear of God and love of his neighbour, recovering what he has lost. He who has learned to know God and himself seems to be one who has ordered the earth with good husbandry. Earth is also the element from which man's visible, tangible body comes, one of the four in the little world. In a simple, the body or earth is cast away and only its virtue taken. Earth seems also to be a clear, crystalline earth in which what is pure in the elements shall remain like a fixed essence, at rest for ever with the saved.

## elements

The four elements are fire, air, water and earth. Man's matter is their extract, so he must have in himself a familiarity with all of them and their fruits; God made them for their fruits' sake, to sustain man with food and physic, and knowing them shows every disease in man and its cure. The three principles of the first matter were afterwards separated into these four bodies. An element is the invisible life and spirit within its visible body, in which its astra lie: the visible earth is not the element but its body, dead in itself yet the element of a hidden life. Severinus names the true elements, purely spiritual, the keepers, wombs and receptacles of all creation, and the elemented elements as they are to us, fire or the firmament, air, water and earth, which conceive, bring forth and take back all things.

## ens

Paracelsus calls the inner astrum, the sun of the little world, the ens or being of the seed, from which man is generated, formed and governed. Agrippa gives the name to the universal spirit, a being the senses cannot grasp. The first ens toward life rises by the archaeus from the last matter of food and nourishes the body. Paracelsus speaks of the ens of God, the source of diseases whose causes are unsearchable and whose cure must be sought in faith, not in nature. Life is also shortened through the ens by bodily diseases, whose cure is to be sought from natural causes and means; mind and being together cut life short. Paracelsus names a twofold death, from the iliad or first principle and from the ens: the first the physician may hinder, the second not. That ens may be the ens of God, or the bodily being.

## essence

Essence is the healing part of a thing, its physic, lying close to its poison, for nothing in this lower world is so noble that it holds no poison; got out from its husks and impurities, the quickening essence, loosed from its chains, mounts higher and quicker. The one nature is the essence and matter of all things. The physical essence in physic is called gold, and the being of the seed, from which all men after the first proceed, is also named essence. What is pure in the elements seems likened to a pure, clear, incorruptible and fixed essence that shall remain, and the philosophers of Hermes' school say, with caution, that the greatest medicine may be the essence of our life.

## faith

Faith is first faith in God: by it alone eternal things are seen, and faith in God, with love of one's neighbour, is the best foundation of physic. The faith of grace through Christ is distinct from natural faith, an inborn faith or wisdom that God the Father gave equally to all men at the creation. Natural faith is our imagination and is so powerful that it can both cause and cure diseases; its efficacy showed in the woman with the issue of blood and in the centurion, and through God it can do all things naturally. Faith is also a working power: what we do visibly by the body we do by faith in the manner of spirits, and without faith we can do nothing. It gives the imagination, and every creature obeys the faith of men who are innocent and taught in the law of the Lord. Against invisible diseases of the mind no bodily guard avails, only faith, which is the work of him in whom we believe. Last, a firm faith, hope and trust toward the physician and his physic help much toward health.

## fiat

Fiat, “let there be”, is the creating word of God. By it God produced the first matter; the triune God, speaking this triune word, created all things threefold; and by it man was made from the clay or dust of the earth. The word fiat is the seed of the whole world, by which heaven and earth were made, and it works in all creatures; it is the true medicine that heals all things, and by it the spirit of God is made one with nature. Man has two spirits: the first from the limbus, the greater world, the second from the word fiat.

## fifth essence

The fifth essence is the perfect part of a thing below the moon, which fire can separate from the dregs, its imperfect part. As the chief medicine it results from the purer essence of the four elements, purified and incorruptible, and is compared to heaven: God created it as the root of life to keep the four qualities of the human body, as he made heaven to keep the universe. It has the nature and heat of heaven, binds the elements or humours in peaceful harmony, and is the fifth essence of the whole world, the world regenerated; the tincture is the fifth essence of man. The philosophers of Hermes' school say it is compounded of the four elements, all actually present in it. Occult qualities that the soul of the world puts into things to make them fruitful are commonly called fifth essences. God took the quintessence out of all created things and fashioned man of it; man is the quintessence of the greater world, and so may not only imitate heaven but rule it. In medicine the quintessence is the soul dwelling in a remedy, its super-elemental mystery and the true inner balsam of our bodies, which fire parts from the body; the quintessence of gold is said to renew the whole body.

## fire

Fire tests and separates: as all things are proved by fire, so is the knowledge of physic. It parts the false from the true and the quintessence from the body, divides things into their three first things, and reveals their agreement, operation and difference and ever new remedies. Fire is also an element, whose fruits are the stars, the zodiac's signs and the seven rulers of the world. What Paracelsus calls the element of fire is an essential fire of life working within the body, perhaps the greatest secret against all diseases. Life may be prolonged by remedies as a fire by laying on wood. The root of a disease seems to be the fire that must be quenched, not its smoke. Temptation and tribulation are a fire man must pass through to blessedness, and the Holy Spirit is taken to renew by fire to make hidden things manifest.

## firmament

The firmament is the heaven with its fixed stars and planets, whose course is free and governed by none; it is the second natural book, and the school of physic is roofed not with tile but with the firmament. It is also the element of fire, the heavenly element; it is twofold, with an element dwelling in it that holds all astra and seeds, and it sends out the bodies of the stars, sun, moon and planets. Severinus counts fire or the firmament first of the elemented elements. Man has a firmament within him with its own stars and course, not governed by the outer one, and the sidereal man is one with the firmament of the astra. There seems also to be a lower firmament with a sun to which the element of fire is likened, and the word of God appears to be called the firmament of all physic.

## first matter

The first matter is what God first produced by the word fiat: threefold, holding the three principles, a shapeless, confused essence that philosophers call the chaos and hyle, mother of the world, and Paracelsus the limbus. Its earth was the word of the Lord, and it was the seed of all things to be created: God drew every creature out of it, and in respect of it all things were created perfect. The first matter was made a world, and the great world was made man.

## globes

Two globes, the upper and the lower, are explained by astronomy and philosophy; the properties of the bodies in the upper globe are seen in the astra and bodies of the lower. Severinus divides the places of the elements into these two: the upper holds fire or the firmament and air, the lower water and earth, like the yolk of an egg. Beneath the globe of the moon God seems to have created all metallic, mineral and vegetable forms, and there too the more incorruptible substance is found.

## gold

Gold is above all a metal of medicine: rightly refined it would show most divine actions, yet few have God's gift to make solid gold drinkable. It is set down for the heart and thickens the excessive sublimation of mercury, though a man may have much gold and know nothing of its healing virtue. The physical essence in physic is called gold. Gold is also money, which cheers the spirits when the purse is full, but Croll says he did not seek to make gold and silver.

## Hermes

Hermes is the ancient philosopher called most wise and most pious, of reverend antiquity, the foremost of natural philosophers and the first prophet; his pillars stood in Egypt. One adept is said to have laboured happily like Hermes for more than twenty years. Hermes' school seems to be the line of earlier philosophers trained in the secret art, who kept its secret by silence.

## imagination

The imagination is a name of man's sidereal body: it encloses and is all the astra, holds the same course, nature and power as heaven, and is one with the astra of the firmament; being all the astra, it works many operations. It is a working power: it marks the unborn child without touch, draws other men's fancy like a lodestone, infects the stars, breeds the plague, and with true “Gabalia” can produce growing things and metals. It is natural faith, which can both cause and cure diseases. The firmament also has an imagination, but without reason, as man has it with reason, and the whole heaven is called nothing but imagination. Man's reason, used as God wills, is a chain binding the five spirits of the elements, whose imagination is also in him, so that they rest in his imagination. Imagination is also counted among the invisible diseases of the mind.

## light of grace

The light of grace is one of the two lights, within which are all things and without which is nothing; it is named with theology and set beside the light of nature. It begets a true theologian, though not without philosophy, and physic takes its rise from it and ends in the light of nature. By its heavenly help we may, with Christ and all the elect, possess eternal life, and by it the intimate vision and intuitive knowledge of God come to the separated soul even in this world. By it and the light of nature certain treasures are to be made manifest to all in due time.

## light of nature

The light of nature is a divine analogy of the visible world with the body of man. It is the firmament, the great world, which supplies man naturally with all things. Within man it is an inward teacher, named with the inward invisible man and the inner angel, that teaches like a sound doctor; a physician should be born of it and of the light of grace. It is also each man's freedom and power to discern and judge, which no one may take from another and which was never confined to Galen alone. Paired with the light of grace as one of the two lights, it is God's treasury confirmed in scripture and makes a true philosopher, as grace makes a theologian; by it things are shown and men become skilled in chemistry and truly philosophize.

## limus terrae

Limus terrae is the slime, clay or dust of the earth from which man was taken. It is explained as the visible frame of the whole world, heaven and its spheres with the earth: the great world with all its creatures, a hidden secret thing, twofold, visible and invisible. Man himself is called the limus terrae, as made up of all bodies and created things. It also means the earthly mind, after which the friends of God do not live, for they live after the image and inspiration of the Most High.
