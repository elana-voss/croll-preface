## lodestone

The lodestone is the drawing stone, used in drawing plasters and taken as a likeness for anything that attracts or works unseen. In man, the sidereal spirit, born of the astra together with him and still united to them, is the lodestone and has a magnetic nature; the drawing body and spirit of man are the lodestone of the microcosm, pulling the force of the astra to him. As the earthly lodestone is a spirit in its body and draws to itself, so the imagination of man attracts the fancy of other men. The antipathic lodestone has wonderful virtues and works as if bewitching, spiritually and invisibly.

## magic

True and lawful magic, set wholly apart from witchcraft, is the perfection of all philosophy, the chief wisdom of God's works and full knowledge of hidden nature. It does things beyond nature's usual order by fitly applying agents and patients, observing the consent and dissent of things; far better treasures lie in it, with the true Cabala. Natural magic has kinds: one does invisibly and spiritually, by art, what nature does visibly and bodily; another, called cabalistic, works through the imagination, so that the true magician can draw the working of the stars, stones and metals into it. Magic, like faith, transplants minds and has power over all spirits. Mechanical magic can make earth swim on water, which common people hardly believe. Severinus speaks of an incomprehensible magic, seemingly a hidden working, by which what God planted in the elements is ever supplied out of the treasures of divine wisdom.

## magician

The true magician is the wise man. He alone can discern physic, which is a spirit and not a body. He can draw the working of the stars, stones and metals into his imagination, and if he is a true magician he is not below the angels in any magical operation. The name stands for wisdom joined to hidden power over things.

## magistery

Magistery means mastership, and it seems chiefly to name the philosophers' great work: Lully searched into it, it is tied to the matter of the elixir, and the philosophical magistery has an origin of its own. In Lully's words, God may open to the artist what is hidden in the magistery of this art. Nature too is said to make itself merry with its own magistery, rejoicing in its master, its art and its virtues. In the plural, magisteries seem to be a kind of high prepared medicine, counted among the arcana beside fifth essences and named together with elixirs.

## matrix

A matrix is a womb: the place where a seed or astrum dies and rots and then brings forth its fruit, the astrum going forward into a new body. The four astra of the elements are the seeds in four matrices, and the astrum of each element bears its fruits in a strange region or matrix, as the fish lives in the water. In a larger sense the matrix is the mother of a whole: water is the matrix of the world, on which the Spirit of God moved, and the great world was the matrix of Adam, the mother of all things brought forth. Severinus says that three first things are found in every matrix, which seems to mean every place of birth.

## matter

Matter is spoken of in two states. In respect of the first matter all things were created perfect; the last matter, things as they now stand, is what the chemist perfects by fire, drawing the secret things or astra out of it back into the first. It seems that in digestion, likewise, the archeus raises the first being of the body's nourishment out of the last matter of food. The one nature is the essence and matter of all things. Gross bodily matter drowns and hinders the power of a medicine, for secrets work without matter and body, and separation yields a pure, crystalline matter fit for the body. The chief medicine is made of the most simple and incorruptible matter under heaven, generated by God out of the spirit of this world, that is, the soul or spirit of the world. Lesser uses: the philosophers say the matter of their mystery may be had everywhere, being in every elementated thing; Morienes seems to tell King Calid, who asked after the matter of the elixir, that it is of the king himself; Lully seems to say he got his matter out of a mean and worthless thing; the matter of the stone is said to be in everything by the vegetable spirit; and Paracelsus calls the matter of diseases tartar.

## medicine

Medicine is a remedy given for disease, rightly prepared by one's own labour and used in the fear of God; sometimes the best medicine is to use none at all. Above every other stands the one chief medicine of the most ancient philosophers, universal and famous, which the wise had from the beginning of the world. God created it for the preservation of life. It is made of the most incorruptible and effective matter under heaven, the soul or spirit of the world, and is in itself the matter of our creation.

## mercury

Mercury is one of the three principles of every compound body, with sulphur and salt, and from these three all physic proceeds. It is the liquor or moisture: like the elixir it gives the virtues, and by a steady supply of vital and vegetative moisture it cherishes the other two, which by constant action grow dry and old. It is also the moisture of what nourishes, and all ulcers are healed by incarnating mercury. In plain use it is quicksilver, to which the watery, fleeing moisture of green wood is likened. Mercury may be exalted above its natural degree in three ways and so seems to cause diseases; distilled by the heat of digestion, it causes apoplexies of every sort.

## metals

Metals are fruit of the elements, like living things and plants, created not for man's food but for his physic. Some, out of simplicity, hold that metals are of no use in physic, and that view is answered. Above all in the perfect metals God has written on nature with his own sacred finger; being free of corruption themselves, their spirits free man's body from corruption as plants, which are corruptible, cannot. Precious stones take their colour, form and tincture from metals by the forming of the stars, for they are metals transplanted. Metals as riches seem to be left to those with an impious hunger to be rich, and the philosophers trained in the school of Hermes name the metamorphosis of metals beside the curing of diseases.

## mind

The mind is the part of man that comes from God and unites him with God: the spirit God breathed into man, God himself dwelling in us, and the steersman of the soul. When the soul turns back within itself into the mind, it draws near to God, sees all things and needs no outward teaching; the man who lives in nothing but the mind is as an angel. Embracing the mind, the image of divinity united to God by Christ, brings actual blessedness. At the last all minds, in unspeakable friendship, shall be one thing in one mind above every mind; this may be God himself, called there the intimate vision of God, or simply the oneness of all minds. Against it stands the earthly mind, after which the friends of God do not live.

## mummy

Mummy is the preserving balsam of life. The true medicine, as natural mummy and kernel of nature, is held in the vital sulphur. Man has an innate mummy, his inward nature or balsam, the vital body and liquor of life, seated in the midst of the heart; by it nature heals its own infirmities and keeps the body from corruption, and its destruction is ultimate death. The plague infects the mummy and sulphur of man. The Word of God is a supercelestial mummy, comforting mortals more than their own. Mummy seems also to stop bleeding in man because of like anatomy, and salt and mummy are named together as something that can be spent, after which, it seems, things breed nothing but worms.

## mystery

A mystery is a hidden thing of God or of nature: true philosophers come to understand and even imitate the greatest mysteries of nature, while impostors are unworthy of them, and divine mysteries are greater than words can set forth. In chemistry a mystery is the healing part of a thing, which the art separates into its own receptacle; the super-elemental mystery is the quintessence. The word seems also to cover the soul's turning back within itself into the mind, by which it draws near to God, a mystery the academic spirit cannot understand and which is not to be spoken of openly. The philosophers say the matter of their mystery, seemingly their work, may be had everywhere, being in every elementated thing.

## natural death

Natural death, spoken of philosophically, is only the wasting of the natural moisture and heat, as in a burning lamp: after old age the vital spirit is fully extinguished, consumed and scattered. Diseases can cause it. It differs from the fatal or ultimate death, the utmost term God has fixed for each, which belongs to theology. Yet natural death is also equated with the term of life God appointed to man for disobedience, and up to that term the medicine keeps a man sound.

## natural heat

Natural heat is the heat in man that digests nourishment. It is the heat of the sun and moon in us, the microcosmic heat by which everything is digested for sustenance and multiplication. The celestial spirit, which is one with our natural spirit, is our natural heat whenever its breathing in the body is not lessened or hindered by outward things. When this heat or fire grows weak, it can be strengthened, inflamed and increased.

## nature

Nature is the medium that joins the lowest things to the highest by harmonious consent, called animal, vegetable or mineral after its subject. It is the order and series of God's works, obeying his word and borrowing all its virtue from him; the one nature is the essence and matter of all things, the specific of every creature, and the invisible fire by which all things multiply. Within the body nature is the genuine physician of all diseases, the inward mummy or balsam, a virtue and not a quality; the physician is her servant and minister, not her master. As she now is, she gives nothing pure but mixes everything with impurities, to move men to learn chemistry. She is a book and a sanctuary whose light teaches like a sound doctor, and art and nature consent like mother and daughter. Heraclitus called nature the daughter of war. Every nature best preserves itself.

## nothing

Man's own nothing is what he falls into when he turns from God to himself or to creatures, claiming them and himself as his own; it is joined with his own evil, to his ruin. Whatever is not God is nothing and ought to be esteemed so. The most beautiful nothing is the shadow and region of darkness and death, which must be cast behind on the return to the Being of beings. Yet the friends of God, from divine love, cast themselves headlong into the fountain of the abyss and the sea of nothingness; this seems to be a nothingness sought rather than fled.

## philosophy

True philosophy is the mind's right knowledge of itself. It must be grounded on Scripture and on Christ the cornerstone and so return into God; at its end it knows God, the maker of all things, and passes into him with a full image of his likeness. Without it no one can be wholly godly, and it is one with theology. In a narrower sense philosophy teaches the virtues of earth and water, as astronomy does of fire and air, and the two together make a perfect philosopher. It is also a practical physic that keeps remedies ready for the physician, the mother of physicians. The philosophy of the heathen is polluted and corrupt, the spring of all error, and must not rule Christians.

## physic

Physic is the art and profession of healing, and above that the healing power God set in things: nothing else than the created and incarnate mercy of the heavenly Father, a favour given by God, placed in nature beside his justice, which is the disease and venom. This healing power is wrapped in rinds, barks, husks and coverings and must be separated from the impure elements by the chemists' art; it is a spirit and not a body, which only the wise man can discern. The work of curing is the refreshing of strength and balsam and the removing of the impurities that cause diseases. True physic is the gift of the most high God alone, not to be learned from the heathen; covered in bark and rind, it must be got out and rightly prepared by fire, and it is what was sought with exceeding love. True physic seems also to be a branch of knowledge, named beside astronomy and chemistry, without which the truly philosophical preparations of the medicines of Theophrastus cannot be understood.

## poison

Poison is what harms and kills, as in the draught by which the malicious adversaries of Paracelsus brought him to a violent death. Nothing in this lower world is so noble that it has no poison in it, set close beside its healing essence or physic; this poison, a tartarous, mucilaginous impurity, must be separated from healing medicines and balsams. Roger Bacon says that where God in mercy placed plenty of physic, there by his justice he set poison to guard it. Poison can overcome poison: nature destroys one by another, as opium, with a greater degree of poison, overcomes a pestilence, and every poisonous creature holds an antidote to its own. Paracelsus gives the name of poison and tartar to the excrements of meat and drink that breed most diseases.

## regeneration

Regeneration is new birth, in matter and in man. In the chemist's work the first life of herbs and medicines must die so that a second life is reached through putrefaction and regeneration; without it no hidden secret of physic is attained. The chief medicine seems to be a whole new regenerate world, which by its regeneration can renew man, the little old corruptible world. In man, regeneration is the means by which Christians have their seed from God, which the heathen lack: it overcomes a bad birth, is a celestial transplantation, and gives true philosophy and all sciences. Hermes and others of clean hearts knew it before the Word was incarnate, though they concealed it. At the end of this world a regeneration of soul, spirit and body seems to sparkle out through the body, and its time draws near.

## resolution

Resolution is the dissolving of a body. An artificial or spagyric resolution brings to light the properties that lay hidden in its composition, and by a natural and due resolution the power of gold would be brought into act so that it could exercise its virtues. In the body, resolution is one of the four ways in which salt destroys health; the salt of the urine is forced into resolution by the violence of separation; and what strengthens natural heat is turned into a simple spirit by resolution and joining with the nourishment. Paracelsus says that death from the resolution of the “Iliad” may be hindered by the industry of the physician, while death from the being itself cannot.

## salt

Salt is one of the three principles of every natural body, beside mercury and sulphur. By coagulation it gives solidity, colour and taste to all bodies; when green wood burns it remains in the ashes, unctuous, slight and lasting. Separating the substances of opium shows a bitter, hot salt in it that penetrates and causes sweat, and the body's surplus of salt is separated by the urine. In a wider use of the word, salt is whatever will melt, can be resolved into a watery moisture and is then dried and hardened by fire; all earthiness is the salt of Paracelsus. Salt is also named with mummy as a thing that can be spent.

## secret

A secret is a hidden thing that lies in God and in nature, of which God is the first inventor; the man united to God has the most absolute knowledge of all secrets and is their keeper. The ancient philosophers strictly forbade such secrets to be profaned, and they have always been hidden from the common sort of those who profess philosophy, though they are now being brought to light and made common. A secret seems also to be a hidden medicine or its working power: secrets stand beside remedies, work without matter and body because diseases are not bodies, and only their knowledge and use is wanting. Highest of all is the one universal physic, the most secret of secrets, by which all things may be done: the greatest earthly treasure, which evil men would abuse to the ruin of the world.

## secrets of nature

The secrets of nature are the hidden things of nature, which true philosophers and skilled physicians search out, come to understand and at last really imitate. To find them out seems to be the chief thing in temporal matters, and the end of physic is not to scrape up money but to discover them. Cheats and impostors are called ignorant and unworthy of them, as of the mysteries of God.

## seed

A seed is the beginning and end of a thing: as a tree grows from its seed, so the seed of grain is its astrum, an invisible body that, cast into the earth, brings forth a visible one, and each fruit is again astrum and seed. The earth, though it seems dead, holds the seeds of all things. The first, pure seeds of things carry the roots of sicknesses. The first matter was the seed of all created things, the great world the seed of man, and the word Fiat the seed of the whole world; man is the last and perfect seed, though not a seed like others. Severinus holds that seeds lay hid in the elements from creation. The interpreters of secret nature say the seeds of all things lie hid in man. Truth seems to be a seed planted from God by regeneration.

## separation

Separation is the parting of purity from impurity, or truth from falsehood; it is what a spagyric resolution means. Severinus speaks of separating the strong from the weak and the pure from the impure in certain bodies. In creation, the spiritual elements seem to have set the visible bodies apart from themselves by separation, as the soul parts its body from itself and still dwells in it. Each bodily member also seems to have its own separation, beside its own digestion, emunctory and excrement.

## sidereal body

The sidereal body is man's second, invisible body. Adam had two bodies, a visible elemental one from the earth and nourished by it, and an invisible, insensible sidereal one from the astra of the firmament, which lives by the firmament. The sidereal body is the house of the spirit's life, and it is this body that is capable of attaining philosophy. Not man alone has one: everything, sensible or insensible, has a sidereal spirit or sidereal body in it.

## sidereal spirit

The sidereal spirit is the invisible man: the life that dwells and works in the body day and night and is properly the man, not flesh and blood. It comes from the firmament and is fed from air and fire; it is mortal, distinct from the immortal soul breathed in by God, and as it is the life of the body, so the Spirit of the Lord is the life of the soul. It draws all arts and human wisdom from the rays of the stars, carries the body wherever it imagines and dreams, and stirs the dexterity by which each man forges his fortune. In a star it is the invisible astrum, which is the star properly, and every thing has one. In proud men it seems to rule the old man. All natural things may be learned from it, perhaps from man's own or from the firmament's.

## spagyric

A spagyric is one who can separate the pure from the impure and discern true from false, a son of art exercised in chemical work; a physician must be a good spagyric, restoring his patient to health by a wise alchemical preparation. The word seems also to describe that art of preparing medicine and those who practise it: the spagyric profession, spagyric physicians, and a spagyric state whose sincere citizens may join together in union. Spagyric physic, physic duly prepared, seems to be preferred above the mixed compounds of the shops, and spagyric is glossed as divinely distinguishing.

## specific

A specific is a thing's own inward power, set against its outward qualities, by which it cures. Medicines work not by external, superficial qualities but by an internal, proper, specific and harmonious virtue, which comforts the heart and serves to cure; opium overcomes poison by its specific venomous virtue, having a greater degree of poison. As a noun, the one nature is the specific of every creature, which seems to mean that which gives each creature its own kind.
