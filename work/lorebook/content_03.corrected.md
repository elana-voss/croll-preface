## spirit of life

The spirit of life is the life-spirit of the body: a single spirit united throughout the whole body of man, yet varied according to the part in which it dwells. The strengthening power of human and animal bodies proceeds from it; it moves and acts the native or radical humour, and it is nourished in turn by the humours of life. It is also called the spiracle, the breathing hole. The fifth essence is said to be truly the spirit of life, piercing through all other spirits and altogether one and the same with the spirit of our body.

## spirit of the world

The spirit of the world is the invisible and insensible body of the astra, also called Nature. The invisible man and the natural spirit of our body are one and the same with it, as whiteness is one with snow: the heavenly spirit and the spirit of the body are a single spirit. It is also named the soul of the world, the universal spirit that lives in and quickens the whole mass, works all in all and fills the whole world, a united life that gathers and knits all things together, whose magnetic spark lives and works effectually. Avicenna, building on Plato, the Arabians and the Chaldeans, calls it the soul of the world poured into all things. This same spirit is the matter of the chief medicine, the most incorruptible and effective matter there can be under heaven, holding the force of all heavenly and earthly things. By it imperfect and impure metals are cleansed of their infirmities as the bodies of men are, and of it the great God generated the most simple matter for restoring human nature.

## spirit

Spirit is the sidereal spirit in all things, called heaven or the astrum, an invisible, insensible living body, without reason in things that grow and with reason in man. Man has two spirits: a sidereal one from the firmament, and the intellectual soul inspired from God by the breath of life. A universal spirit lives in, quickens and fills the whole world. Spirit is also the life and balsam of natural things, a supernal fire got by art from things in which the virtue of sun and moon is most potent. Some Theophrastians add spirit to salt, sulphur and mercury as a fourth principle answering to air, got from minerals and vegetables but not from animals. Severinus speaks of the Spirit of God moving upon the waters at the creation. Four elementary spirits seem to belong to the elements; with a fifth kind they seem to make five, whose imagination is in man.

## sulphur

Sulphur, or oil, is one of the three principles of every natural body, beside mercury and salt. By a pleasant mixture it tempers the coagulation of the salt and gives the body substance and transmutation. Opium holds a sweet narcotic sulphur, and the body's surplus of sulphur is purged through the entrails. A vital sulphur seems to be meant as a life-bearing sulphur in bodies: the true medicine, as natural mummy and kernel of nature, is contained in it as in the treasure of nature, and the plague infects the mummy and sulphur of man, the microcosm.

## sun

The sun is the heavenly Father, who richly bestows his gifts on us all, shining on all alike. It is also the sun of the sky, whose beams give the heat that brings forth all kinds of flowers and herbs, and which melts snow though it cannot be touched. Properly, the sun is not its visible body but the spirit in that body. The lower world seems to have its own sun as well: the inferior astronomy has its own heaven, sun and moon, and the earthly firmament has its sun, a thing below answering to the sun above. Theophrastus seems to be the rising sun whom the ambitious physicians should have endured without gazing on his clouds.

## taciturnity

Taciturnity is constant silence. By it the philosophers kept the secret of the art closed, remaining altogether dumb about it, and the gifts of the art are to be used in the same constant silence, without pride, since pride provokes the envy of all men. Silence about the secret is thus both the way the art has been guarded and the manner of living with its gifts.

## tartar

Tartar is the excrement of food and drink, coagulated in man by the spirit of salt. Whatever we eat and drink holds a mucilaginous, clayish and sandy tartar harmful to health; the archeus of the stomach must destroy this tartar of the elements, or it turns into the tartar of man. As the natural surplus, the mucilage of salt, tartar is the mother of almost all diseases of all coagulate bodies. Paracelsus divides it into a foreign kind, coming from food and drink, and an innate kind, coming from the hereditary blood.

## three principles

The three principles are mercury, sulphur and salt, also called the three first. They are found in every compound body, and no natural body can be parted by any dissolving skill into more or fewer than these three: liquor, oil and salt. The physical body is said to be nothing but sulphur, salt and mercury. When the first life of herbs and medicines dies and the chemist's art brings them to a second, the three seem to show themselves with their hidden virtues, stripped naked and visible in a clear mercurial, an oily sulphurous and a bright saline form. Severinus seems to hold that all compound bodies come from them and are resolved into them again, and that they are found in every matrix. Natural actions proceed from spirits in which the mechanic sciences of the three have their vigour. Health is their agreement and union in a body, and they are the cause of all diseases; diseases spring from their invisible mechanic spirits and happen by their exaltation, and the best physicians judge their broken natural proportion to be the true origin of bodily disease. Lesser uses: the three first of vinegar, which Galen could not separate, glossed as qualities; and whey, butter and cheese as the three first of milk, all earthiness being salt.

## tincture

Tincture seems to be named with spirit as the source of action: natural actions, and the actions of cures, proceed from spirits and their vital tinctures, not from bodies or their dead qualities. Precious stones take colour, form and tincture from metals by the forming of the stars, as rubies and garnets have the tincture of gold. Tincture is called the fifth essence of the microcosm. By God's curse after the fall new tinctures came in, which are infinite evils. Severinus seems to speak of bodies whose tinctures hold agreeing qualities though no actions proceed from them, and of a radical tincture that with the balsam preserves the elements. Alphidius seems to set the true tincture against the mere colour that giddy seekers find instead, and Paracelsus is taken to say that the tincture so cleanses the balsam that its effect is seen to the tenth generation.

## transmutation

Transmutation is the change of a body or substance into another state. A vehement imagination can transmute not only one's own body but sometimes another's. The salt in man may be changed into all sorts of salts, and as the transmutation is, so is the disease. Things of natural composition are subject to corruption and transmutation. Sulphur, by its pleasant mixture with salt, gives a body substance and transmutation, which seems to mean its capacity to change.

## universal medicine

The universal medicine heals all diseases that proceed from the corruption of the humours, however great and desperate their cure, and powerfully expels the acute diseases that proceed from the impressions of the stars. Many holy men used it before the flood. It has no effect when a person has reached the utmost term appointed by the supreme Being, or when a disease is inflicted by God beyond nature.

## virtue

Virtue is the power in a thing to heal or harm, set apart from its quality, such as hot or cold. Nature itself is a virtue, not a quality, and without virtue a quality is dead and wholly accidental. Physic works on man's body and cures disease by virtue: things hold hidden and restrained virtues, some serve by a specific and harmonious virtue to cure and comfort the heart, opium acts by its specific venomous virtue, and the innate virtue of things that grow from the earth is caused by heavenly influence. Virtue is also moral goodness, which is never found without the marks and wounds of envy.

## Vulcan

Vulcan seems to stand for fire as the means of trial: things are searched and examined by the help of Vulcan before they are commended, for the proper trial of fire discerns and separates the false from the true. The poets praise Vulcan as the truest inventor and teacher of the mysteries of the arts. His name is set on an alchemy that serves the commonwealth and on the spagyric and Hermetic school, and seems there to mean the art of fire.

## the Word

The Word is first God's creating word, Fiat, “Let there be”, by which God produced the first matter, created heaven and earth, and brought all things out of darkness into light, the Word speaking and the Spirit cherishing. The Word of God is also the heavenly medicine that alone heals all things. In it lies hidden all force beyond natural actions, and by it all medicines become powerful; herbs are not the medicines but only a sign of the Word signified. The Word is the uncreated mercy of God; incarnate, it may be found in the creatures, yet is not known to every one; physicians have wrought great cures by the created Word, the incarnate mercy, and the true Cabalist performs his work in the Word by mind, faith and prayer. Words spoken, written, carved or hung about the neck also work cures: medicines are visible bodies, words invisible ones.

## adept

An adept is one who has attained it: a complete philosopher and keeper of God's secrets, whom it is useless to tempt with promises of reward. Only adept physicians know the mental diseases, which are healed without the help of natural physic. Paracelsus explains adept philosophy as long life, free from all infirmity until natural death.

## anatomy

Anatomy seems to mean the inward make of the great world and of man, which agree: the outward world is a speculative anatomy in which, as in a glass, the lesser world man may be seen, and whoever can anatomize it gains true knowledge of man, its child. A sound physician's knowledge proceeds from the anatomy of both worlds; this essentiated and elemented anatomy, which reveals disease and cure, is set above the local anatomy of dead bodies. Things of the same anatomy seem to answer each other, so that malady and medicine share one anatomy and like cures like, as mummy stops bleeding in man. Anatomy also seems to name the chemists' art of taking bodies apart, separating the pure medicine from the impure body. The anatomy of death is said to dwell in the whole state of our life.

## artist

The artist is the skilled worker in chemical employments, a master of his craft whom the preparations require, not a perverse or foolish one; yet no skill of artist or nature can make the wedlock of life and death perpetual. Lullius holds that the artist must agree with God, the first cause, without contrariety, and that God should move the artist's soul to true understanding. God himself is the perfect artist of all things, the most high Creator. Elias, who is to come and restore all things, is called the artist, which seems to mark him as the restorer.
