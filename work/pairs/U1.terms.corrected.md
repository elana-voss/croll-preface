# The True Physic in Plain Terms

This page sets out what these passages of the Admonitory Preface say about the True Physick, the subject of the author's first heads (OPEN.24, OPEN.25, OPEN.28) and of the chapters CH1 to CH5. One wish in CH6 is read beside it, and the Description of the little World Man (MICRO) is added to its first head. **Stated.** means the text says it outright. **Interpreted.** marks my inference, with the passages it rests on. **Several readings.** gives each defensible reading with its basis. **Plain.** means the words mean what they plainly say.

## Process or not

The division made from the readings of the whole Preface finds that the book marks a process here. Its basis is the head of a whole chapter, “How the True Physick, which is covered bark and rind, is to be got out, and rightly prepared by Fire” [CH3.0], announced as “That it is to be fetcht out, and prepared truly, by fire.” [OPEN.26]. It names the operation “this Separatory art which teacheth rightly to discerne and seperate the Good from the Bad, the Profitable from the Unprofitable” [CH3.14], and three operations in a row as a way: “The Chymicall way of Subtiliating Extracting, and Separating” [CH3.40]. Their end is “that pure and Christiline matter may be administred to our bodies” [CH3.7]. The finding is the division's, not a claim the book makes about itself.

## In brief

1. **Stated.** The author gives the True Physick heads of its own: what it is, where it lies hid, how it is got out and prepared by Fire, how it works, and what Physitian it requires [OPEN.24; OPEN.25; OPEN.28; CH1.0; CH2.0; CH3.0; CH4.0].
2. **Stated.** Every natural body resolves into three and only three, Mercury, Sulphur and Salt, and this is seen in a Spagiricall resolution [CH1.19].
3. **Stated.** The True Physick is contained in the three Families of Nature. It is not a matter of qualities but a specifix vertue hidden in the Seeds and planted by the Creator [CH2.2].
4. **Stated.** The True Physick is covered with bark and rind and is got out and prepared by Fire. Without Chymicall Phylosophy all Physick is lifeless [CH3.0; CH3.8].
5. **Stated.** Nature is a vertue, not a quality, and is helped by vertues rather than qualities. Nature is the healer, and the Physitian is her servant. The body of the simple is cast away and only its vertue or Astrum is taken [CH4.6].
6. **Stated.** The most hurtful diseases spring from the Seed or Astra's and the invisible Spirits of the Three principles [CH4.6]. As there are Three from which all Physick proceeds, there are three sorts of diseases; Ulcers, mattery sores and Inflammations are cured by Mercury, Salt and Sulphur [CH4.10].
7. **Stated.** The Physitian must be a good SPAGIRUS, one who can separate the pure from the impure [CH5.12].
8. **Interpreted.** The Description of man is joined to the Physick because the Physick works on man as on a little world made of all things [OPEN.24; CH4.6; MICRO.12].
9. **Stated.** The author says he has not handled all things, and that by the Philosophical law some things are left obscure [CH6.76].

## What the author sets before the reader

**Stated.** The author offers the reader “two of the most excellent Spagyrik Mysteries” [OPEN.3]. He says of them “whose preparations, after the mistakes of expence, time and labour, have all of them been first proved by my own handy experience” [OPEN.3].

**Stated.** He refuses “much lesse to fill up a huge Volume with the copies of large Receipts” [OPEN.3], and the margin says “The greatest Receipts the greatest Deceits for the most part.” [N005]. The many descriptions of the men he sought out were “all reduced into one onely, and that same first proved by my own Chymicall triall” [OPEN.3].

**Several readings.** He examined what he gathered “by the help of Vulcan” [OPEN.3]. (a) Vulcan stands for the work of fire, as in “the Alchymy of Vulcan” [CH3.8], and the “School of Vulcan” stands in the same paragraph as medicines “which must be prepar'd and try'd by Fire” [CH6.76]. (b) A margin note says “Astrum, Vulcanus and Archaeus are the same thing” [N123] (note-only).

**Interpreted.** Two things here point ahead to the True Physick: preparations proved by his own trial, and many descriptions reduced to one tested one. A margin note in CH3 makes a like contrast when it sets “the Spagyrick Physick duely prepared” against the mixtures in the shops [N172] (note-only). That OPEN.3 already speaks of the True Physick is my inference.

**Stated.** The author expects that the Mysticall Hermetick Philosophers “will accuse me for breaking open the seale of Chymistry” [OPEN.10].

**Stated.** His answer is that the heirs of true wisdom will get far better Treasures “by asking, seeking, and knocking with unwearied paines and watchfullnesse” [OPEN.10], while the sluggish despisers of these secrets will never finger them “by reason of their indisposition and unfitnesse to manuall operation” [OPEN.10].

**Stated.** He also says that the mysteries are “mingled together in a fit place” [OPEN.10]. See the open questions.

## What that Physick is

**Stated.** The first head reads “What that Phisick is which cureth mens diseases, and few Phisitians know it.” [OPEN.24]. The chapter that answers it is headed “CHAP. I. Of the True Physick.” [CH1.0]. The book spells the word both Phisick and Physick.

**Stated.** CH1 speaks of the Three Principles: “No body compos'd by Nature can by any dissolving skill be parted into more or lesse then Three, viz. Into Mercury or liquor, Sulphur or Oyle, and Salt” [CH1.19]. The author grounds this in creation: “For the Holy Triunity when it spake that Triune word FIAT created all things Triune, as in a Spagiricall resolution is plainly to be seen.” [CH1.19].

**Stated.** The margin note glosses the Spagiricall resolution: “i. e. A seperation of purity from impurity, or truth from falshood.” [N052] (note-only). Like words return in the duty of the Physitian, who must “seperate the pure from the impure” [CH5.12].

**Stated.** The author says that “The Spagyricks can make this plaine by visible experience and uncontroulable certainty.” [CH1.19]. His example is green wood, which has three moistures. Of these, “these two are consumed by the fire; The third, viz. the Salt is unctuous, very little, thin and lasting, and remains in the ashes” [CH1.19].

**Stated.** Each of the Three has its office: “The Salt by coagulation gives Solidity, Colour and Tast to all bodies” [CH1.19]. Sulphur “gives the Body Substance and Transmutation” [CH1.19]. Mercury is the one “which like the Elixir giveth the vertues” [CH1.19].

**Stated.** The same Three make man's body: “Thus also the Earth as it is indued with that threefold substance of Salt, Mercury and Sulphur, is the cause of the materiall body of man” [CH1.19].

**Several readings.** The margin says “When the Salt or Mummy is spent things” breed nothing but worms; a gap stands in the next word [N053] (note-only). (a) Mummy is a second name for Salt, the lasting part; the note joins the two names with “or” [N053]. (b) Mummy is the preserving power in a body, as in CH4, where Nature heals itself “by the innate Mummy” [CH4.6] and is called “the Internall Mummy or inward Balsom” [CH4.6].

**Interpreted.** The names Mercury, Sulphur and Salt do not mean only the common substances. The text explains them as liquor, oil and the lasting part that remains in the ashes, and finds them in green wood. Sulphur in wood is only “like brimstone” [CH1.19]. So the names stand for three parts that every body has.

## The little World Man, added to the first head

**Stated.** The author joins this Description to the first head: “Whereunto is added a perfect Philosophicall Description of the little World Man, almost forgotten and unknown.” [OPEN.24].

**Stated.** Man is made of all things: “Man is compacted of all bodies and created things” [MICRO.12], and “So that all the properties of Animalls, Vegetables and Mineralls entred into him” [MICRO.12]. In the same way, “all Externall things are nothing else but the Body of Man” [MICRO.24].

**Stated.** Man has two bodies: “Thus Adam had two bodies, that is, a visible Elementall, and an invisible Sydereall body.” [MICRO.31].

**Stated.** The Astra's are not “the seaven visible coales of Heaven which are but the bodyes of the Astra's” [MICRO.32]. In growing things “the seeds of any graine or Wheat is the Astrum, viz. the invisible body” [MICRO.32], and “Bodyes are nothing else but the Excrement of the Astra's” [MICRO.32].

**Stated.** A margin note gives the Astra's a part in disease: “The Astra's send the plague into us through our skin as the Sun doth his beams thhrough the glasse.” [N124] (note-only).

**Interpreted.** The Description bears on the Physick. The Physick is “a certaine specifix vertue concealed in the very Seeds” [CH2.2]. The seed is the Astrum, the invisible body [MICRO.32]. In the cure “the vertue Heaven or Astrum of it only to be taken” [CH4.6]. CH4 gives the reason for the name: “Man is therefore called a Microcosm or little world, because the whole world preserveth, nourisheth and cureth him.” [CH4.6]. That this is why the Description is added is my inference [OPEN.24].

## Where that True Physick lies hid, and may be found

**Stated.** The head reads “Where that True Phisick lyeth hid, and may be found.” [OPEN.25]. The chapter that answers it is “CHAP. II. Where that True Physick is to be Found.” [CH2.0].

**Stated.** It is found in all three kinds of natural things: “in those three Families of Nature, the Vegetable, Animall, and Minerall” [CH2.2], and the author adds that from these “are commonly had medicines enough to preserve the health and cure all bodily diseases” [CH2.2]. In them “there is contained that True and specifix Physick of materiall distempers” [CH2.2].

**Stated.** It is not a quality. It “doth not consist in the four outward, naked, superficiary and Relollaceous qualities” [CH2.2], and the author notes that Theophrastus “learnedly discourseth” of this [CH2.2]. It is something else: “it is a certaine specifix vertue concealed in the very Seeds, naturally proceeding without art” [CH2.2]. The Creator planted it in every growing thing by his Word, “whereby all things were brought out of darknesse into Light” [CH2.2].

**Interpreted.** The vertue proceeds “without art” [CH2.2], yet the next head says it must be got out by Fire [CH3.0]. On my reading, nature makes the vertue and the art only frees it from its cover.

**Stated.** The CH6 wish asks that each worker be set “to find out one out of those Three Families of Nature” [CH6.30].

**Interpreted.** Man, made of the properties of “Animalls, Vegetables and Mineralls” [MICRO.12], is on my reading cured from the same three Families [CH2.2; CH6.30].

## How it is got out, and rightly prepared by Fire

**Stated.** The head reads “How the True Physick, which is covered bark and rind, is to be got out, and rightly prepared by Fire” [CH3.0].

**Interpreted.** Bark and rind are figures for the outward body of a thing. The text says that in Galen's days “they knew not how to separate the bark and husk from the kernell” [CH3.40]. In CH4 it says “the body or Earth of Simples is to be cast away” [CH4.6]. The narrowest reading is that the bark and rind are the gross body, and the kernel is the vertue.

**Stated.** Chymicall skill is needed for any Physick: “for without this Chymicall Phylosophy all Physick is but livelesse” [CH3.8]. The author blames physicians who “use to leave the preparation of their medicines most commonly to some carelesse and covetous Apothecary to the great dammage of their Patients” [CH3.8].

**Stated.** The operation and what it yields: “By this artificall resolution of bodyes the propertyes which before layd in the compositions of them are now brought to light.” [CH3.8]. Hence “Therefore a Physitian should be exercised much in this true Analysis and vitall Anatomy of bodies” [CH3.8], because “there is no constant quality of any body which is not to be found either in the Salt, or Mercury, or Sulphur of the same body” [CH3.8].

**Stated.** A margin note gives “Reasons why the Spagyrick Physick duely prepared is to be preferd before other Hetroclite mixtures in the shops.” [N172] (note-only). The first is that many sick people “will take it more easily and willingly” [CH3.17].

**Stated.** The text names the way: “The Chymicall way of Subtiliating Extracting, and Separating being imperfect, was not much used in Galens dayes” [CH3.40]. Galen, as the author reports his words, tried to find a way “to separate the contrary parts of vineger as well as of milk” [CH3.40], and “might have obtained his desire if he had been well skild in the Distillatory Art” [CH3.40].

**Stated.** The margin gives the three in milk: “Whey, Butter and Cheese are the three first things of milk, for all Terresterity is Salt.” [N181] (note-only). The text says Galen would gladly have served Theophrastus (Paracelsus) “to separate the three first (qualities) in vineger” [CH3.40], “but especially to learn the preparations of those high Magisteryes and Elixirs” [CH3.40].

**Several readings.** The vinegar passage calls the three first “(qualities)” [CH3.40] (wording-dependent). (a) The word in brackets is an added gloss, and the three first are the Three of CH1.19, as the milk note's “all Terresterity is Salt” suggests [N181]. (b) Qualities is meant as the word says, though CH2.2 denies that the True Physick lies in qualities [CH2.2].

**Stated.** Men neglect it for gold: “almost all men have a burning desire after making Gold” [CH3.43] “but regard not the due preparation of their Medicines” [CH3.43]. The margin names “four Pillars of Physick, Phylosophy, Alchymistry, Astronomy, and the Physicall vertue or medicine.” [N185] (note-only).

**Stated.** The author calls the Art “this Divine Spagyrie, the most wonderfull and approved Art” [CH3.45], which “hath been very much disparaged by the ruder sort” [CH3.45]. Yet abuse does not condemn it: “the bettter the thing is, the more destructive is the abuse of it” [CH3.45].

## How, and with what Vertue, Physick worketh upon Mans body

**Stated.** The head reads “How, and with what Vertue, Physick worketh upon Mans body and cureth his Diseases” [CH4.0].

**Stated.** Like, not contrary: “medicines should not be contrary to the grieved part, but very agreeable to it” [CH4.6]. The medicine has the same outward Nature as the part affected has inward, “that the Internall Nature which stands in need of it may both be strengthned and succoured by the superabundance of that Externall Nature” [CH4.6]. “When the fruits of the Earth, Aire, Water and Fire of the Microcosm are sick, they must be restored by fruits like themselves of the Macrocosm.” [CH4.6].

**Stated.** Nature heals, and the Physitian serves her. Nature “it selfe healeth its own infirmities by the innate Mummy” [CH4.6], and “when her own inward strength fayleth shee is to be supplyed with outward helpe by her servant the Physitian” [CH4.6]. Sometimes it is best “to leave the ooperation to the sole Archaeus or Art of Nature” [CH4.6]. The margin adds “The Inward Physitian doth the work, when the Naturall Physitian faileth.” [N188] (note-only, wording-dependent).

**Stated.** Vertue, not quality: “Nature is not a quality but a vertue” [CH4.6], “for without vertue the quallity is dead and wholly accidentall” [CH4.6]. Opium helps in the plague “not by the coldnesse of Opium, but but by the Specifick venemous vertue of the Opium” [CH4.6]. “Thus Nature destroyeth one poyson by another, it subdueth a weak evill by a stronger” [CH4.6].

**Interpreted.** The author also allows contraries: “But Contraryes cure because they piece up the defects and wants” [CH4.6]. On my reading this does not clash with like curing like. What must not be contrary is the medicine's Nature to the part's Nature. What may be contrary is a vertue set against the vertue of the disease, as with opium. The text itself draws the line between vertue and quality [CH4.6].

**Stated.** Where diseases come from: “Roots of diseases in the body of Man are neither hot nor cold” [CH4.6]. The most pernicious of them “do not spring out of the matter of the body, or out of the four Humours, but from the Nature of the Seed or Astra's and Invisible mechanick Spirits of the Three principles” [CH4.6]. “These Forgers and Invisible Astra's of diseases were not knowne to the Ancients.” [CH4.6].

**Stated.** So the medicine must be a spirit as well: “Physick is a Spirit (not a body) which the Magician or wise man only can discerne” [CH4.6]. “the body or Earth of Simples is to be cast away” [CH4.6], because “it is necessary that the life should worke upon the life, and the Spirit upon the Spirit by sepration of the impure body” [CH4.6]. “This is the true and lively Anatomy.” [CH4.6].

**Interpreted.** Here CH4 ties back to CH3. Casting away the body of the simple and keeping its vertue or Astrum is what the bark-and-rind head of CH3 promises. The “true and lively Anatomy” [CH4.6] matches “this true Analysis and vitall Anatomy of bodies” [CH3.8].

**Stated.** The cure goes to the root: “when the Centre, Root, and Seed of diseases are pluckt up and removed the worke is done” [CH4.6]. “That Physitian which cureth by complexion is like him that would extinguish the hurtless flame and let alone the fire in the coals” [CH4.6].

**Several readings.** Whose words these root sayings are. Margin notes beside them name Paracelsus and his writings: “Paracelsus in the Tincture of medicines.” [N189], “In his Book of ancient Physick.” [N191]. (a) The words are the author's own, and the notes point to where Paracelsus says the same. (b) They are Paracelsus's words, as the notes beside them suggest [N189; N191].

**Stated.** The Three of CH1 return as a rule of cure: “As there are Three from which all Physick proceedeth, Mercury, Sulphur, Salt; so likewise there are three sorts of diseases, Mercuriall, Sulphurean, Saline” [CH4.10]. “All Ulcers are cured by incarnating Mercury; All mattery gluish sores by Salt; All Inflamations by Sulphur.” [CH4.10]. He adds “This needs no Reasons to confirme it.” [CH4.10].

## What manner of Physitian that Physick doth require

**Stated.** The head reads “What manner of Phisitian that Phisick doth require for its Minister.” [OPEN.28].

**Stated.** “Secondly, a Physitian must be a good SPAGIRUS” [CH5.12], “one that can seperate the pure from the impure, and restore his Patient to health by a wise Alchymicall preparation” [CH5.12]. The margin says “A Philosopher and Physitian spring one out of another” [N202] (note-only).

**Stated.** The Art is older than Paracelsus, “though he were not the Auther of that Art” [CH5.14]. He sought to restore “the True and Philosophicall Physick which none in his time assayed to restore” [CH5.14], which had been “lost and obliterated for many years” [CH5.14].

**Stated.** Others followed him, and the author hopes that their writings will help their readers so that they “might by a daily practice of Piety fetch out that huge Talent and Treasury which is hid and shut up within themselvs” [CH5.20].

**Several readings.** How CH5.20 relates to the True Physick. (a) The inward Treasury is a further good that the author sets beside the Physick. These men's study is the Physick, and their writings lead to the inner life. (b) The inward fetching out is the same course as the outward one, getting out the hidden, read in man. The words “fetch out” [CH5.20] and “hid” [CH5.20] echo the heads “fetcht out” [OPEN.26] and “lyeth hid” [OPEN.25].

**Stated.** Who may give Physick. Many physicians “cannot indure that Physick with its preparations should premiscuously be imparted to all sorts of men” [CH5.62]. The author calls the belief behind their fear “a foolish conceit of the frantick vulgar sort, who think that a Secret looseth its vertue when tis made known” [CH5.62]. Yet “neither God nor Nature hath made every man a Physitian” [CH5.62]. The margin adds “Paracelsus would not have Physick be concealed.” [N227] (note-only).

**Stated.** The ground beneath it is faith: “The best and chiefest Foundation of Physick is faith in God, and love to our neighbour, if this be wanting all Art is nothing worth.” [N227] (note-only).

## The work wished for, and the author's closing words

**Stated.** The author wishes the Great Ones would bestow some part of their wealth “upon men piously and consciensciously learned and skilfull in Chymistry” [CH6.30], setting each “to find out one out of those Three Families of Nature” [CH6.30], so that “they might fetch out the most choyse Physicall Mysteries” [CH6.30], “separating them by Fire into their Three First” [CH6.30].

**Interpreted.** This wish repeats the whole course of the True Physick in its own words: the Three Families of CH2, the fetching out and the Fire of CH3, and the Three of CH1 and CH4. It stands in CH6 and speaks of Mysteries in the plural, not of the one chief Medicine that CH6 is headed for.

**Stated.** The cost of the work: the Prince supplied the author with necessary expences, “who was altogether unable to bear and undergoe so great a burden as all these Medicins come to, which must be prepar'd and try'd by Fire” [CH6.76].

**Stated.** Physick “is not yet come to the Limit of perfection, but many things remain to be discovered to future ages” [CH6.76]. Of the secrets by which life was lengthened, he says: “Wherefore we want nothing but the knowledge of Secrets, and their use.” [CH6.71].

**Stated.** What he holds back: he writes “so far as was lawfull, and so much as was allowable by God, have I imparted candidly” [CH6.76]. He also writes “I have not handled all things here in this place, to avoyd prolixity” [CH6.76], and “It is provided for by the Philosophical law that some tedious things should be left obscure to young Schollers and for intelligent & wise men to find out” [CH6.76].

**Several readings.** The closing hope is that “that Ancient, True, & Philosophical Medicin, which by reason of the long continuance and injury of time” [CH6.76] is not yet fully known may be restored. (a) This is the True Physick under another name: CH5.14 calls what Paracelsus restored “the True and Philosophicall Physick” [CH5.14], also lost through time. (b) It is the chief Medicine, which the author gives a head of its own in CH6.

### Open questions

- What the author means by mysteries “mingled together in a fit place”, and where they are mingled [OPEN.10].
- Whether Vulcan in “by the help of Vulcan” means the work of fire or the inner spirit that a note equates with Astrum and Archaeus [OPEN.3; N123; CH3.8; CH6.76].
- Whether Mummy is another name for Salt or the healing power of Nature [N053; CH4.6].
- Whether the three first (qualities) of vinegar are the Three Principles or qualities [CH3.40; N181; CH2.2].
- Whose words the root sayings of CH4.6 are, the author's or Paracelsus's [CH4.6; N189; N190; N191].
- How the inward Treasury of CH5.20 relates to the outward getting out of the Physick [CH5.20; CH3.0].
- Whether “that Ancient, True, & Philosophical Medicin” is the True Physick or the chief Medicine [CH6.76; CH5.14].
- What the author holds back, having imparted only “so far as was lawfull” [CH6.76] and having “not handled all things” [CH6.76].
