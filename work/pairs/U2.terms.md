# That One Only Chief Medicine of the Most Ancient Philosophers in Plain Terms

This page sets out what the given lines of the author's Admonitory Preface say about one unit: the Medicine that the sixth chapter's head names “Of that one onely chiefe Medicine of the most Ancient Phylosophers” [CH6.0]. The author's list of heads gives it the sixth place [OPEN.29]; he first sets it apart in his opening [OPEN.6]; the fifth chapter names its limit [CH5.68]; and the sixth chapter treats it from its first paragraph [CH6.1] to the close of the Preface [CH6.76]. Each point carries one of four marks: **Stated** where the text says it outright, **Interpreted** where the point is this page's inference, worded as such, **Several readings** where the lines allow more than one, and **Plain** where the words mean what they plainly say. Points that rest on a margin note alone are tagged note-only; points that rest on a single English word of the translation are tagged wording-dependent.

## Process or not

The division made from the readings of the whole Preface found that the book does mark a process, and that this Medicine is the second of its two units, of the kind the division names Medicine, wrought by Art. Its basis, as the division gives it: the head of the third chapter, “How the True Physick, which is covered bark and rind, is to be got out, and rightly prepared by Fire” [CH3.0]; the author's list of heads, “That it is to be fetcht out, and prepared truly, by fire.” [OPEN.26]; “this Separatory art which teacheth rightly to discerne and seperate the Good from the Bad, the Profitable from the Unprofitable” [CH3.14]; “The Chymicall way of Subtiliating Extracting, and Separating” [CH3.40]; and the end those operations lead to, “that pure and Christiline matter may be administred to our bodies” [CH3.7]. For this unit in particular, the division rests on the sixth chapter: the Medicine is “the greatest miracle of Nature wrought by Art” [CH6.38]; a way is named, “the Moyst way of the Ancients not as yet intensly exalted to the eye of the Basilisk” [CH6.38]; the True Phylosophers hold that none attains the end “before the fat or blood of the Sun and the Dew of the Lunary, be joyned into one body by the circular wheel of the Elements with the help of Art and Nature in the form of an Hexagon” [CH6.21]; a margin note names “Gold animated by the Salt of Niture is made the chief subject of the Phylosophers metallick Physicks” [N263]; and the work is “in this Rode or Kings Highway and Phylosophical Reason is to be accomplished” [CH6.48]. The division found this a separate unit from the True Physick because its result differs: one Medicine that heals all diseases of the humours [CH6.42]. The division also records that the steps of this course are covered, and not given plainly in these lines [CH6.21; CH6.38].

## In brief

1. **Stated.** The author sets this one Medicine apart from all he offers: nothing in Nature is more choice than his own medicines, except this one, which the most ancient Philosophers had from the beginning of the world [OPEN.6; OPEN.29].
2. **Stated.** It cannot keep a man alive past the term of life; against that it is of no effect [CH5.68; CH6.38; CH6.42].
3. **Stated.** The author calls it the greatest gift of wisdom given to man, by which all things may be done, on the Plannets of inferior Astronomy, on the health of the body, and in a Magicall use he passes over [CH6.1].
4. **Stated.** Few or none find it; God gives it to whom he will [CH6.18; CH6.21; CH6.44].
5. **Stated.** Its knowers swore never to write it plainly, and gave it only to those who understood allegorically [CH6.21; N281].
6. **Stated.** The True Phylosophers hold that it is reached only when the fat or blood of the Sun and the Dew of the Lunary are joined into one body, with the help of Art and Nature, if God please [CH6.21].
7. **Stated.** One drop of it, as a Latex or liquor, raised a man at the point of death to full health in one night [CH6.38].
8. **Stated.** It heals all diseases that come from the corruption of the humours, except at the appointed term or where God sends the disease as punishment [CH6.42].
9. **Stated.** It is made by gathering all the virtues of the Creation into one mass by Art [CH6.48–49; CH6.38].
10. **Stated.** The Phylosophers of Hermes school call it the Life and Light that quickens our Balsom, and a Fift Essence; the author says God created this Fift Essence as the root of life [CH6.50–51].
11. **Stated.** God created it to preserve life up to the appointed term; Adam and holy men before the flood had and used it [CH6.71].
12. **Stated.** At the close the author says the Ancient, True and Philosophical Medicine is not yet fully known, and may be restored [CH6.76].

## Set apart from what the author offers

**Stated.** In his opening the author says that among the medicines he offers nothing in Nature is more choice. He says “there doth not lye hid any more choyce and efficacious, then these chief secrets of the more hidden Medicines here offered” [OPEN.6], with one exception: “except only that universall and most famous Medicine which the most ancient wise Philosophers had from the beginning of the world, and have extoll'd it as a miracle to their just and lawfull Heirs” [OPEN.6]. The unit is thus marked first by being set apart from what the book offers. [OPEN.6]

**Interpreted.** This page reads the exception as saying that the author's own medicines are not this Medicine: he compares them to it and puts it above them. The narrowest reading the line supports is a ranking; it does not say whether he himself has it. [OPEN.6]

**Stated.** The author's list of heads gives it its own place: “Of the universall and chiefe Medicine or Phisick of the most Ancient Philosophers, commended of many, possessed, yea seen, or believed of very few.” [OPEN.29] It is praised by many and held by few. [OPEN.29]

## Its limit: the term of life

**Stated.** In the fifth chapter, among the reasons men die, the author says “No Medicine can preserve the corruptible body from death, the wages and punishment of sin; but there is one thing that hindereth corruption, reneweth youth, and prolongeth the short life, as in the Patriarchs” [CH5.68]. Yet even so “we must of necessity die at last in respect of the punishment of sin” [CH5.68]. [CH5.68]

**Stated.** He then names this Medicine and its limit: “And in this case the universall and chiefest medicine is of no effect, because no industry or skill of Artist or Nature can perpetuate the wedlock of life and death” [CH5.68]; that wedlock “by an immutable necessity is destin'd to a divorce” [CH5.68]. [CH5.68]

**Several readings.** The “one thing” that renews youth and prolongs life “as in the Patriarchs” [CH5.68] may be this Medicine: the sixth chapter says “Many holy men used this universall Medicine before the flood, which Adam also had in his Family” [CH6.71], and links it to long life. Or it may be something the lines leave unnamed: the same paragraph names “the universall and chiefest medicine” [CH5.68] separately and by its own name, so the “one thing” need not be it. [CH5.68; CH6.71]

**Stated.** The fifth chapter also describes what strengthens a weak Naturall Heat: “the macrocosmicall Heat of Sun and Moon, if it could be got, even the most simple matter which the most Great God generated of the spirit of the world” [CH5.44], “for the restauration and conservation of Humane Nature” [CH5.44]; such things are “deduced by art into act, viz. turned into such a simple spirit as is the spirit of our life” [CH5.44]. [CH5.44]

**Interpreted.** The sixth chapter says of this Medicine almost the same words: “it is the most simple Matter which the Best and Greatest Lord generated out of the spirit of this world for the restauration and preservation of Humane Nature” [CH6.51]. On that likeness this page reads the fifth chapter's “most simple matter” [CH5.44] as this Medicine, there named by what it does to the Naturall Heat, with the words “if it could be got” [CH5.44] showing it is hard to come by. The narrowest reading is that both passages describe one matter. [CH5.44; CH6.51]

## The greatest gift of wisdom

**Stated.** The sixth chapter opens: “I shall here supply what yet pertaineth to that chief and universal Phylosophical Phisick” [CH6.1], “than which a greater gift of wisdom we never read that divine bounty bestowed on man” [CH6.1]. Not even the Reasonable Soul can find anything more noble “than this most secret Secret of secret Secrets, by which even wonders, yea all things may be done” [CH6.1]. A margin note calls it “This occult Minerva of Phylosophy & this only most precious Jewell is inestimable.” [N253] (note-only). [CH6.1; N253]

**Stated.** The author names two fields of its work. First, on “the Plannets of inferior Astronomy, whose imperfection and drosse it causeth to vanish by piercing them with its most powerfull impressions” [CH6.1], “for it segregateth all extraneous Sulphureity and impure Terrestreity from metallick and Humane bodies” [CH6.1]. Second, on health: “the recovering covering and preserving of the languishing and lost health of the Human body, by its fiery vigour” [CH6.1]. [CH6.1]

**Interpreted.** The bracket that explains the first field speaks of “metallick and Humane bodies” [CH6.1], and the second field is the human body; on this pairing this page reads “the Plannets of inferior Astronomy” [CH6.1] as a covered name for metallic bodies. The reading is supported elsewhere in the chapter: its work is “in the curing of diseases and the metamorphosis of Mettalls” [CH6.50], and a margin note says that it “suddenly and unawares burneth up any imperfect mettall whatsoever” [N274]. The book itself does not say outright that the Plannets are metals. [CH6.1; CH6.50; N274]

**Stated.** A third use he passes over: “to say nothing now of the Magicall and super Celestial use” [CH6.1], which comes “when the Gonetick influence of the beames of the Sun and Moon and the fourth revolution is finisht upon its native Earth” [CH6.1]; then “it is endowed absolutly with all created Influentiall power as well in the Elementall world, as in the Celestiall or super celestiall” [CH6.1]. [CH6.1]

**Interpreted.** The words “the fourth revolution is finisht upon its native Earth” [CH6.1] mark a stage in its making, after which it has its full power. What the revolutions are, the given lines do not show; the narrowest reading is that the making goes through more than one turn and that four are needed for this use. [CH6.1]

**Stated.** The author gives his witnesses: the consent of learned men in every age, “the ocular inspectation and certification of many of our time” [CH6.1], and their writings, “which are woven of so many Hieroglyphicall, Magicall, and Mathematicall Coverings” [CH6.1]. He says the knowers wrote of it in covered form. [CH6.1]

## Who finds it

**Stated.** The author says no one should wonder that “among so many thousands that seek, there is not one to whom the doors of Nature are unlockt” [CH6.18], since God “giveth his gifts to whom he will, and withdraweth them from whom be pleaseth” [CH6.18], and “knoweth that it is not expedient for all to their salvation, that they should have Honors & riches with their health” [CH6.18]. A margin note says “The one only and true way to all Secrets is to have recourse to God the author of all good” [N261]. [CH6.18; N261]

**Several readings.** In the same paragraph men “seek the Kingdome of God, and study to finde out the Celestiall Stone in the last place” [CH6.18]. (a) The Celestiall Stone is this Medicine, and men should seek it last, after the Kingdom of God; a margin note in the chapter says “in this Stone the universal Nature of things shineth forth” [N283]. (b) The words say that men seek God's Kingdom and the Stone only last, that is too late, and that is why none finds the doors unlocked; the note beside it puts recourse to God first [N261]. A margin note later uses the same name for something reached by the mind turned to God: “By the access of this beam, ray, or celestiall Stone all drosse is purified, and the darknesse of ignorance is driven away.” [N298] (note-only). The given lines do not show that the Celestiall Stone and the Medicine are one thing. [CH6.18; N261; N283; N298]

## The sworn secret

**Stated.** The author says “This is that Secret laid up and buryed in the most secret Treasury of the Mind and Memory” [CH6.21], of which the more hidden Phylosophers “have in a wise and ingenious craft covenanted and sworn they would never write it plainly in any Book” [CH6.21]. “Nor hath any of them who kept this knowledge in the secret of their heart, made it known to any but such who had an understanding allegorically.” [CH6.21] A margin note gives the reason: “God would not manifest this knowledg to all, to avoyd offence.” [N262] (note-only). [CH6.21; N262]

**Stated.** The author says the Phylosophers are free to rename what they speak of: because they are Lords of Things, “they should also have power to dispence or give Names at their pleasure” [CH6.21], “and cloath their children as they listed” [CH6.21]. Yet “in their precepts, as in a glasse, it hath been sufficiently made known to men of a wise understanding, who are chosen of God to such mysteries” [CH6.21]. [CH6.21]

**Interpreted.** This page reads the freedom to “give Names at their pleasure” [CH6.21] as the author's own warning that the names the Phylosophers give this Medicine and its matter are covers, not plain names. The narrowest reading is that the reader cannot take such names at their face. [CH6.21]

## What must be joined

**Stated.** The True Phylosophers, the author reports, all set themselves upon “one and the same thing” [CH6.21] and refer it to God, “Neverthelesse religously affirming, that none can attaine the desired end” [CH6.21] (these are their words as he reports them: QUOTED, True Phylosophers) “before the fat or blood of the Sun and the Dew of the Lunary, be joyned into one body by the circular wheel of the Elements with the help of Art and Nature in the form of an Hexagon” [CH6.21], “which can never be done except the most High God please” [CH6.21]. They add that this holds “though they sleight all particulars, which naturally doe altogether want the virtue of tincturing, unlesse they proceed from the first fountaine” [CH6.21]. [CH6.21]

**Interpreted.** The narrowest reading these words support: the making of the Medicine needs two things, named here “the fat or blood of the Sun” and “the Dew of the Lunary” [CH6.21], joined into one body; the joining goes round by “the circular wheel of the Elements” [CH6.21]; it needs both Art and Nature; and the lesser medicines, the “particulars” [CH6.21], cannot tincture unless they come from the first fountain. What the two things are, and what the wheel and the Hexagon mean, the lines do not say plainly; the same paragraph says the Phylosophers swore never to write it plainly [CH6.21]. [CH6.21]

**Stated.** The author ends the paragraph in his own voice: “let that man use what Arts soever he can, yet shall he never get any thing against the Will of God; for the Spirit proceedeth from Grace, who inspireth whom he will” [CH6.21]. [CH6.21]

**Stated.** A margin note beside the paragraph says of the work: “This is not believed, but being proved with the gain of labor, toyl and hazard, it is made plain toward the end which proveth the work.” [N263] It goes on: “On this the hinge of the whole busines turneth” [N263], “as Gold animated by the Salt of Niture is made the chief subject of the Phylosophers metallick Physicks” [N263] (note-only). [N263]

**Several readings.** (a) The note names the chief subject of this Medicine: gold, given life by “the Salt of Niture” [N263]; it stands in this Medicine's chapter, beside the sworn secret [CH6.21]. (b) The note speaks of “metallick Physicks” [N263] in the plural and does not itself name the one chief Medicine; it may speak of a class of works of which this is one. Whether “Gold” [N263] here is plain or is itself one of the names given “at their pleasure” [CH6.21], the given lines do not show (note-only, wording-dependent). [N263; CH6.21]

## The wonder seen: one drop

**Stated.** The paragraph speaks of a time “being a long time astonished and amazed at the greatest miracle of Nature wrought by Art” [CH6.38], among the changes made in the cold, “the various and manifold metamorphosis of the Inferior Astronomy made in the cold” [CH6.38], during “the Moyst way of the Ancients not as yet intensly exalted to the eye of the Basilisk” [CH6.38]. One wonder passed all: “by giving one onely drop of that Latex or liquor” [CH6.38], “a man desperately sick and at the point of death was recovered” [CH6.38], “restoring him to firme and perfect health in one night” [CH6.38]. [CH6.38]

**Stated.** The Latex is described as that “in which as in a storehouse the dispersed vertues of all Celestiall and Terrestriall bodies were by a wonderfull artifice invisibly gathered together in an heap, nay, in which the whole world was centred” [CH6.38]. It worked by “its Fiery, Astrall, and Celestiall Invisible Nature conveying to the Heart a beam of the Naturall Life” [CH6.38]. [CH6.38]

**Interpreted.** The paragraph says the wonder “seemed strange and most worthy to be seen” [CH6.38] and that someone was “a long time astonished and amazed” [CH6.38]; the given lines do not name who. This page reads it as told as something witnessed, the narrowest reading of those words. [CH6.38]

**Interpreted.** The words “the Moyst way of the Ancients” [CH6.38] name a way of working, and “not as yet intensly exalted to the eye of the Basilisk” [CH6.38] names a stage short of a higher one. The changes are “made in the cold” [CH6.38]. This page reads these as marks of the course of making: a moist way, worked in the cold, with a further exalting still to come. A margin note calls something “The Physophycall Basilisk” that “like Lightning suddenly and unawares burneth up any imperfect mettall whatsoever, and on a sudden Produceth another new forme” [N274]; the Hermes school likewise compares the Medicine's working to “Lightning and the eye of a Basilisk” [CH6.50]. So the Basilisk's eye is a name for the Medicine at its height of power; what the stages are in plain words, the lines do not say. [CH6.38; N274; CH6.50]

**Stated.** The author then says what the Medicine does: “For this Kingly medicine, and the Empresse of all other, causeth Humane Bodies immediately and as it were miraculously to rise againe from what desperate diseases soever” [CH6.38], with the limit “if God hath not otherwise decreed, for many diseases are a Divine innate punishment, for which there is no cure in Nature” [CH6.38]. A margin note: “There is no Physitian can cure death, but Christ.” [N275] [CH6.38; N275]

**Stated.** He calls it a world: “this whole new regenerate world is able by vertue of its Regeneration to renew the little old corruptible world Man” [CH6.38], to “consume what is superfluous, to mend what is defective” [CH6.38], “and preserve it therein till the appointed Terme of death imposed on mortalls for their sin” [CH6.38]. [CH6.38]

**Interpreted.** The book sets the Medicine, a “new regenerate world” [CH6.38], over against man, “the little old corruptible world Man” [CH6.38]: the great world made new renews the little world. The narrowest reading is that the Medicine holds the whole world in small, as the Latex was that “in which the whole world was centred” [CH6.38]. [CH6.38]

## All diseases of the humours

**Stated.** “All diseases proceeding from the corruption of humors, how great and desperate soever their cures be, are healed by this universall medicine” [CH6.42], “as a Carpenter that squareth all kind of timber” [CH6.42], “except the party be at the utmost Terme appointed him by the supream Being; or the disease be inflicted by God, besides Nature, as a punishment and speciall affliction” [CH6.42]. [CH6.42]

**Plain.** The two exceptions here are the same two the earlier paragraphs give: the appointed term [CH5.68; CH6.38] and disease sent by God as punishment [CH6.38]. [CH6.42]

## The Artist and God

**Stated.** The author approves what Lullius “rightly concluded” [CH6.44] (QUOTED: Lullius): “between the Artist and God the first cause, there ought to be an agreement without contrariety” [CH6.44], so that God moves the Artist's soul “that he may open to him what is hid in the magistery of this Art” [CH6.44]. [CH6.44]

**Stated.** The author warns of men who abused it: “two publick Phylosophers of divers Nations in our time abusing the Gifts of God” [CH6.44] “came to dolefull and lamentable end” [CH6.44], “answerable to the unworthy publishing of this most True Phylosophicall Art” [CH6.44]. [CH6.44]

**Several readings.** A margin note beside the end of the paragraph reads “The originall of the Philosophicall Magistery.” [N278] and goes on: “Most doe otherwise now, who make thir boast among others that they have the clear knowledge of it, when it is only but imaginary” [N278]. (a) The originall is the first way of holding it, the silence the paragraph ends on, which most now break by boasting. (b) The originall is its source, God moving the Artist, as Lullius has it in the same paragraph [CH6.44]. The note alone does not settle which (note-only). [N278; CH6.44]

## The road and the one mass

**Stated.** Many hunt after “this happinesse” [CH6.48], “yet have been perswaded that they should never attaine the same, either by other means or any Arts” [CH6.48], “but by a wonderfull and most abstruse comprehending of all the vertues of the whole Creation flowing and running together in one certaine masse” [CH6.48], which “in this Rode or Kings Highway and Phylosophical Reason is to be accomplished” [CH6.48]. Whose persuasion this is, the lines do not show: the voice is UNCLEAR. [CH6.48]

**Stated.** The next paragraph, in the author's voice: “All these spirituall virtues and active qualities being by great help of ingenuity and Art like the lesser world heaped up together and concentred into one masse” [CH6.49], they bring “a kind of sweetest and admirable illustration of the Mind” [CH6.49], “the knowledge of all Naturall things and Heavenly seerets, and a perfect operation” [CH6.49], and the use of “this choycest and admirable Magisterie, together with a flowing plenty and Abundance of all Things” [CH6.49]. A margin note: “The defect of Nature must be supplyed by the industry of Art” [N280]. [CH6.49; N280]

**Interpreted.** Read together, these lines give the one plain account of the making in the given lines: the virtues of the whole Creation are gathered “by great help of ingenuity and Art” [CH6.49] into “one masse” [CH6.49], as in the Latex the virtues of all bodies were “by a wonderfull artifice invisibly gathered together in an heap” [CH6.38]. The how of the gathering is not given. The narrowest reading: the work is a gathering into one, done by Art where Nature falls short [N280]. [CH6.48–49; CH6.38; N280]

## The Reason given by Hermes school

**Stated.** The author reports that the Phylosophers “train'd up in Hermes school” [CH6.50], “though they have been altogether dumb in keeping close the secret of the Art by a constant Taciturnity” [CH6.50], “yet usually give this Reason” [CH6.50]. A margin note says “They durst not under paine of Anathema to speak of it, but in a picture of Aenigmaticall words” [N281]. [CH6.50; N281]

**Stated.** Their Reason (QUOTED: the Phylosophers of Hermes school): “because the greatest Medicine, being artificially prepared with the help of wise Nature the Mistresse of Science, should be (or is) the Life and inlightning Light” [CH6.50], and “that which quickneth or maketh alive our Balsom, that is, the spirit or celestiall” [CH6.50] vapour of life; “it may be the Essence of our Life: the Fift Essence compounded of the four Elements, in which are all the Elements actually” [CH6.50], “bound together with a golden chaine without any contrariety” [CH6.50]. Its matter is so subtle that it works “in the curing of diseases and the metamorphosis of Mettalls, like as in Lightning and the eye of a Basilisk, as is manifest by Experience” [CH6.50]. [CH6.50]

**Stated.** Their Reason closes with a comparison: “This is so in respect of the four qualities of the body as the Heaven is incorruptible in respect of the four Elements.” [CH6.50] A margin note adds: “It exciteth motion in our bodies and reviveth the Elements” [N281]. [CH6.50; N281]

**Interpreted.** In their words the Medicine stands to the body's four qualities as the Heaven stands to the four Elements: something above them that keeps them [CH6.50]. Their own wording hedges: “should be (or is)” [CH6.50] and “it may be” [CH6.50]. This page reads the hedge as theirs, and not as the author's doubt. [CH6.50]

## The Fifth Essence and its names

**Stated.** The author, in his own voice, takes up their word: “The most High created this Fift Essence as the root of life in Nature for the preservation of the four qualities of the Humane body” [CH6.51], “as he did the Heaven for the preservation of the universe” [CH6.51]. In it “the Influences of all celestiall bodies which are communicated to the sublunary to every one in particular, these are concentred in this one” [CH6.51]. [CH6.51]

**Stated.** He then gives it a run of names: “The Theater of the secrets of all Natur's Light” [CH6.51]; “the Glasse of God's Mysteries, and the Miracle of universall Nature; the Fift Essence of the whole frame of the world, and the whole world Regenerated, wherein the Treasure of all Nature lyeth” [CH6.51]; “the Son of the Sun and Moon, who by his ascending into Heaven and descending into the Earth hath obtained all power of superior and inferior things” [CH6.51]; “the Habitation of all metallick, minerall, and vegetable Forms, which God created under the Globe of the Moon” [CH6.51]. [CH6.51]

**Interpreted.** The name “the Son of the Sun and Moon” [CH6.51] answers the True Phylosophers' joining of “the fat or blood of the Sun and the Dew of the Lunary” [CH6.21] into one body: what is born of the two is called their Son. The narrowest reading: the Medicine is named as the offspring of the two things that must be joined. What Sun and Moon name here in plain words, the lines do not show. [CH6.51; CH6.21]

**Stated.** He says it is “truly the spirit of Life which pierceth through all other spirits” [CH6.51], which “is altogether one and the same with the spirit of our body, the bond between the Body and Soul” [CH6.51]; to keep peace between soul and body “there is need of the Balsom of life as a means to be sought from Externalls, by which the internall is restored” [CH6.51]. And: “it is the most simple Matter which the Best and Greatest Lord generated out of the spirit of this world for the restauration and preservation of Humane Nature, which hath been altogether unknown to the Physitians of our time” [CH6.51]. [CH6.51]

**Stated.** Margin notes beside this paragraph add names. “By this Art the knowledge of almost all things, and in this Stone the universal Nature of things shineth forth.” [N283] “The Tincture is the Fife Essence of the Microcosm” [N283]. Paracelsus (QUOTED: Paracelsus) calls it “the Defensive of old age, the Universall medicament, which like the invisible Fire consumeth all diseases” [N283]. “Those Ancients who had most perfect knowledge of Things have called this Fist Essence the middle nature of Soules.” [N284] (note-only). [N283; N284]

**Interpreted.** The notes use “this Stone” and “The Tincture” [N283] for what the paragraph calls the Fift Essence [CH6.51]. This page reads Stone, Tincture, Fift Essence, Balsom, Magisterie and universall Medicine as names the book gives to one thing, each from a different side; the narrowest reading is that the notes name the paragraph's subject (note-only). [N283; CH6.51; CH6.49; CH6.42]

**Stated.** The author says why physicians do not know it: it “never came into their Schools who goe not into the Temple of Apollo through the right door, but break through the Roofe” [CH6.51]. [CH6.51]

## Long life, up to the term

**Stated.** The author distinguishes two deaths. “I speak here Phylosophically of the Naturall death, which is onely the wasting of the Naturall Moysture and Heat, as may be seen in a burning lamp” [CH6.70], “not Theologically of the Fatall death and utmost Terme which God hath prefixt to every one” [CH6.70]. Of the first, “it is in our own hand and power to put an end to our life if we will, and to prolong it without offence to GOD” [CH6.70], “so may he prolong his life by removing these impediments, so that at last he may attaine to the appointed Terme of the Nature of life” [CH6.70]. [CH6.70]

**Stated.** “God hath created the aforesaid Medicine for the preservation of life, which may preserve our body as well from the corruption of our Parents, as from the defect of our own government, cure its infirmities, and repaire what is wasted” [CH6.71]; it can “chase away from us all diseases which cause the naturall death, untill ultimate death the most Terrible of all Terrible things” [CH6.71]. Paracelsus (QUOTED: Paracelsus) says “the death which is from the resolution of the Iliad may be hindred by the industry of the Physitian, but that which is from the Ens or Being cannot” [CH6.71]. ## Not yet fully known

**Stated.** At the close the author says he has imparted “so far as was lawfull, and so much as was allowable by God” [CH6.76], and “I have not handled all things here in this place, to avoyd prolixity” [CH6.76]. He hopes that “that Ancient, True, & Philosophical Medicin” [CH6.76], which through time and “the unworthines of our age” [CH6.76] “is not yet fully known, may in a shorttime by the heavenly assistance, be restored to its lost lustre & ancient splendor” [CH6.76]. [CH6.76]

**Interpreted.** The unit's entry links these closing words to this Medicine: “Ancient, True, & Philosophical” [CH6.76] answers the head's “most Ancient Phylosophers” [CH6.0] and the first paragraph's “Phylosophical Phisick” [CH6.1]. The narrowest reading is that the author, at his close, counts this Medicine as lost to his age and still to be restored. [CH6.76; CH6.0; CH6.1]
